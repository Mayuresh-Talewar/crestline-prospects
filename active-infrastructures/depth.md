# Active Infrastructures Limited: story angle (step 1)

Every figure here is in data.md with its page and basis. Savings are not computed at this step.

## Respect first

- A Nagpur builder, incorporated 26 September 2007, that has done what most contractors of its size never do: it listed on NSE Emerge on 28 March 2025 after an IPO that raised Rs 77.83 Cr at Rs 181 a share (FY25 AR).
- It builds water supply, sewerage, roads and public buildings across Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and Tripura. In FY26 97.0% of group revenue was works contracts (Rs 93.71 Cr of Rs 96.57 Cr, FY26 AR Note 26).
- FY26 was a year of growth: consolidated revenue from operations Rs 96.57 Cr, up 7.6% on FY25; standalone profit after tax Rs 10.10 Cr against Rs 5.69 Cr; consolidated PAT Rs 10.28 Cr. Unexecuted order book Rs 125.33 Cr at 31 March 2026 (4 projects, 95.78% water), up from Rs 108.26 Cr a year earlier, plus a roads subsidiary with Rs 92.43 Cr.
- The accounts are clean and open: unmodified audit opinion, no overdue receivable beyond two years, 98.6% of receivables under six months old, a ratio table with reasons for every variance. A CFO who publishes this is not hiding anything.

## Then the question: where do the bills wait?

Not "why are you slow". The filing shows the money is real and earned. What it cannot show is where each rupee is sitting between the work and the cash.

1. Receivables of Rs 49.94 Cr against revenue of Rs 96.57 Cr: 188.7 days of billing (Computed, closing balance over the year's revenue). Management's own turnover ratio of 1.89 gives about 193 days. Which bill, which client desk, which certificate, since when: the balance sheet cannot say.
2. Cost of services incurred and not yet billed: Rs 19.92 Cr at 31 March 2026 against Rs 0.45 Cr a year earlier (Note 7). That money has left the company and is waiting for a measurement, a certification or an invoice. Whose desk holds each piece is not in the accounts.
3. Debtor days moved from 80.3 (FY24) to 211.9 (FY25) to 188.7 (FY26) on one consistent basis (Computed). For the parent alone, management explains the fall in receivables turnover (3.21 against 5.52) as a "substantial increase in average accounts receivable". It does not say which projects or clients.
4. Borrowings rose to Rs 80.23 Cr from Rs 55.83 Cr, in the group, while the parent itself holds none (consolidated 8,023.21 against 5,582.79 lakh). The overdrafts disclose 9.20% and 11.25% variable rates. Each day a bill waits is financed at one of those rates, but the accounts do not tie a rate to a bill.
5. The MD&A names the risk itself: "Delayed payments in public sector contracts", mitigated by "factoring facilities".

What the accounts cannot tell them, and the booklet should say plainly: the site, the running bill, the desk it sits on, and since when. An ERP records what happened, not how long each step took or whose desk it sat on.

## Guardrails for steps 2 and 3

- Use consolidated FY26 as the one basis. Do not set a standalone figure beside a consolidated one.
- Debtor days and WIP days are on revenue; creditor days are on cost. Never net them into a cycle.
- Inventory (Rs 60.94 Cr) is mostly "Inventory of Goods (Building)" (Rs 41.02 Cr, Note 7), not contract work. Only the services part (Rs 19.92 Cr) is contract work in progress.
- Interest only at the disclosed 9.20% or 11.25% overdraft rates, labelled as the subsidiaries' rates.
- No ERP is named. A process map is illustrative only.
- Quote the CFO only with a source in words. No management quote was captured in this step (no results call or transcript was read).
