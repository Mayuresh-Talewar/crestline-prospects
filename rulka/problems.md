# Rulka Electricals: three problems (RE-3)

Every figure: Rulka Electricals Limited, standalone, Annual Report 2025-26 (AR26), filed 07 Sep 2026. FY26 is set against the FY25 column printed in AR26, never against an older report. Days are on closing balances over 365 days (re-calc-output.txt sections 4 and 8). Page numbers are PDF pages.

## First, what went right
FY26 was a strong year. Revenue rose 38.23% to Rs 110.20 Cr (AR26 p90). Operating cash flow turned from an outflow of Rs 15.04 Cr to an inflow of Rs 6.12 Cr (AR26 p91). Borrowings fell 45.21%, from Rs 8.76 Cr to Rs 4.80 Cr (notes 5 and 8, p97 and p99). Stock days came down 27.30, from 106.12 to 78.82. Nitin Aher, Whole Time Director, on the FY26 call (transcript filed 09 Jun 2026, p3): "For the projects based EPC business sustainable cash generation is a important as revenue growth". The three problems below are about keeping that cash, and they start from the company's own accounts.

## Problem 1. Receivable days did not move while the business grew
* Figures: closing receivable days 119.74 in FY25 and 118.69 in FY26, 1.05 days shorter (the Annual Report ratio note uses average balances and gives other figures; this chart uses closing balances only). Receivables rose from Rs 26.15 Cr to Rs 35.84 Cr.
* Source: AR26 p89 (balance sheet) and p90 (revenue), filed 07 Sep 2026; days computed in re-calc-output.txt section 4.
* Label to print with the chart: "Receivable days, closing balances, standalone. Source: Annual Report 2025-26, p89 and p90, filed 07 Sep 2026."
* Why it matters: one receivable day is worth Rs 0.3019 Cr (110.20 / 365). The book holds 118.69 of them.
* What the accounts cannot tell: which bills and which clients hold those days, and how each day splits between Rulka's own acts (measure, prepare, review, submit, invoice) and the client's (acknowledge, certify, pay). The balance sheet shows one number per year.

## Problem 2. The overdue part of the book grew tenfold
* Figures: receivables more than 6 months past their due date were Rs 0.94 Cr (3.61% of the book) at 31 Mar 2025 and Rs 9.59 Cr (26.77%) at 31 Mar 2026, 10.17 times. Of the FY26 book, Rs 328.83 lakh is 1 year or more past its due date.
* Source: AR26 note 14, p101, filed 07 Sep 2026; shares and growth in re-calc-output.txt section 3.
* Label to print with the chart: "Trade receivables more than 6 months past due date, standalone. Source: Annual Report 2025-26, note 14, p101, filed 07 Sep 2026."
* What the accounts cannot tell: which bills make up the Rs 9.59 Cr, on which site, for which client, since when, and whether each one is certified and unpaid or still waiting at the client's desk. Note 14 gives buckets only. The company's own June 2026 presentation (p17, filed 08 Jun 2026) names "Delays in government project payments" as a threat; the accounts cannot show which of these bills that is.

## Problem 3. The cycle got 66.80 days shorter, and the receivable leg gave 1.05 of them
* Figures, FY25 to FY26: cash cycle 153.85 to 87.05 days, 66.80 shorter. By leg: stock days down 27.30 (106.12 to 78.82); payable days up 38.45 (72.01 to 110.46), which shortens the cycle; receivable days down 1.05. Trade payables went from Rs 12.32 Cr to Rs 25.56 Cr.
* How to state it: the cycle moved on the stock side and on the supplier side. The payable rise is where the cycle moved, not an improvement, and it is drawn in a neutral colour. The next days are in the receivable book.
* Source: AR26 p89 and p90, note 9 p99 and notes 21 and 22 p102, filed 07 Sep 2026; legs in re-calc-output.txt section 8.
* Label to print with the chart: "Cash cycle by leg, closing balances, standalone, FY25 and FY26 as printed in the Annual Report 2025-26, filed 07 Sep 2026."
* What the accounts cannot tell: when each supplier falls due against its own terms, so whether the longer payable days are agreed credit or late payment; and which receivable bills could carry the next days instead.
