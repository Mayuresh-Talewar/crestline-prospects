# Problems: C S Construction India (deepened 2026-10-10)

Respect first. A Nagpur family firm started in 1983, with 90+ projects completed in four decades (company profile p3, read 10 Oct 2026). Operating income ₹103.10 Cr in FY22, ₹186.23 Cr in FY23 and about ₹319 Cr in FY24; reported profit after tax ₹4.03 Cr then ₹10.17 Cr (CRISIL rationale, 8 Aug 2024). Order book ₹1,808.9 Cr at 31 Mar 2025 with 38 projects under execution (company profile p6). Never paired with the CRISIL figures.

Ownership rule: acknowledged, certified, collected and released are the client's acts; measuring, preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing are the company's own. Nothing below states a cause that no source states; where a cause is not published, the line says what Crestline would measure.

## 1. Cash: capital held with clients in three forms (stage: deposits and retention; owner: the client releases)
- Verified: CRISIL, 8 Aug 2024: working capital requirements "remain high due to requirement for maintaining various deposits with customers for bidding, security and retention", and "with increase in revenues, the working capital requirements shall increase".
- Amounts: NOT FOUND for any of the three. No retention percentage, no deposit balance.
- Mechanism: each form is released on a different trigger (tender closing, contract completion, the end of the retention period). Which trigger each rupee is waiting for is not published and is not guessed here.
- Crestline would measure: for each deposit and each retention balance, the client, the project, the clause or date that releases it, and the days since it fell due, from the ERP's own records and deposit register.
- Framing: three kinds of money the client holds, named by CRISIL itself.

## 2. Collection: who the clients are, and that each runs its own clock (stage: certification and collection; owner: the client)
- Verified: CRISIL, "derives its entire revenue from tender-based orders". Profile p13 (page image, 10 Oct 2026): seven Indian Railways zones, IRCON, DFCCIL, RITES, MPPTCL, Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh public works departments, MRIDC and the Government of Karnataka.
- Verified, site /ongoing-projects/, 10 Oct 2026: of 27 listed ongoing projects, 10 are for a state PWD, 10 for Railways, 5 for MRIDCL, 2 for other bodies (Crestline's arithmetic on the site list; the profile states 38 under execution, so the two are never paired).
- Not published: payment days, certification days, credit period, ageing, any named delay.
- Mechanism: not stated by any source. Several different public bodies means several approval chains. Whether they differ in days is a measurement, not a claim.
- Crestline would measure: certification and collection days per client body and per project, from the dates the ERP records, and which step holds the days.
- Framing: a client list any contractor would be proud of; the question is only how long each body's clock runs.

## 3. Billing and certification: what the company can prepare faster (stage: billing and certification; owner: the company prepares, the client certifies)
- Verified: Finance and Accounts team of 8 and a CFO since Jan 2024 (profile p4 page image; site /management/). 38 projects under execution (profile p6).
- Not published: bill preparation days, submission days, share of bills certified short or returned.
- Mechanism: not stated. The only fact is the count of finance staff and of live projects; no ratio between them is drawn.
- Crestline would measure: measurement to bill prepared, review, submission, and acknowledgement, split by whose move ends each wait.
- Framing: the company's own steps are the part it can move without asking anyone.

## 4. Vendor: procurement is project by project (stage: vendor and stock; owner: the company)
- Verified: Procurement team of 7 (profile p4 page image). "Procurement of requisite plant and machinery is done on project-to-project basis" (profile p5; site /overview/, 10 Oct 2026). The company owns piling rigs, cranes, batching plants and more than 1,000 MT of shuttering (profile p5).
- Not published: trade payables, creditor days, MSME dues, vendor advances, vendor terms, supplier concentration, subcontract share.
- Mechanism: not stated. Project-by-project buying means terms are set per project; whether they differ is not published.
- Crestline would measure: vendor terms agreed against days actually paid, per vendor and per site, and advances still unadjusted. Paying later is never a saving.
- Framing: seven people buying for many sites; the ERP's dates show where a purchase waits.

## 5. Growth raises the need (stage: cash; owner: both)
- Verified: operating income ₹103.10 Cr (FY22) to ₹186.23 Cr (FY23) and about ₹319 Cr (FY24) (CRISIL). Order book to be executed over 12 to 24 months at Aug 2024 (CRISIL, not paired with the 2025 figure). One day of FY23 operating income is ₹0.5102 Cr (Crestline's arithmetic on the published accounts), so a day out of a wait is worth that once.
- Mechanism (stated by CRISIL): working capital requirements rise with revenue.
- Crestline would measure: the same deposits, bills and payables as above, ranked by rupees and days, so growth does not hide the oldest rupee.

What the accounts cannot tell them: site, bill, desk, since when. Not printed: rating grade, limits, lender, utilisation, cash accrual, unencumbered cash, coverage, concentration, the sister company, the unsecured loan (README CS-2, sensitive-garvis.md).
