## 1. Management on working capital, collections and the clients (exact words)
1. **WC will normalise, Power T&D is the stretch.** Paresh Mehta, CFO, Q4 FY26 call p6: "we believe that we should go back to the old norms of 110 to 120 days, this is more of a transitory as in the last call also, we had said there was some buildup of receivables in our Power division, which probably by end of June and September quarter, most of things would get cleared. So we believe that we should go back to normalcy by post September." (Analyst Vaibhav Shah's question: "Last year, we were around 110 days odd. This year, it has almost doubled." The metric is not defined; do not equate with a computed row.) Strength: Moderate (one call, repeated in spirit on 12 Aug 2026, see 4).
2. **How long each stream takes to pay.** Paresh Mehta, Q4 FY26 call p9: "General experience on the power sector is that the billing cycle is almost 5 to 7 months by the time which we get all money, almost part money of 30% to 40% received in advance, but balance is received after 5 to 7 months. This is the power, where we have an order book of around INR4,600 crores. On the road sector, largely depend on milestone based on completion of various stages, but there is monthly billing at state level. So the cycle is around 3 to 3.5 months." Earlier, Paresh Mehta, Q2 FY26 call p10: "revenue recognized get converted into cash from clients in a span of, let's say, 45 to 90 days' time. In the power, it is sometime 90 days. In roads, it is 45 days." The two statements differ for Power (90 days against 5 to 7 months) and the booklet should not print both as one fact. Strength: Weak-Moderate (two statements, they conflict).
3. **After the March-June build-up.** Paresh Mehta, Q1 FY27 call p15: "So execution is not hurt by collection, but collection was getting increased in the Power T&D sector, which will now get realized in the next couple of quarters. So we expect that that should rationalize by this year-end 4Q. And most of the payment should be in, which is today locked up in working capital and debtor that is unbilled revenue and debtors. The roads and other they are almost in sync with what is planned and as per milestone." And p16: "approximately INR250-odd crores has been infused in March for the new projects which have taken off where billings yet to start... advances paid to contracts for new contracts. So that way it has increased, but it is all as planned." Strength: Moderate.
4. **Debt and interest.** Paresh Mehta, Q1 FY27 call p11: interest cost falls through "realization of our WIP working capital receivables and WIP in our power sector, which is due to be collected in the next 2 quarters as well as monetization of assets". p10: after the sale of the six HAM projects third-party debt "approximately in the range of ... around INR500 crores to INR600 crores". Q4 FY26 call p9: "as of March '26, our working capital debt is INR1,126 crores, less cash of around INR580 crores, so approximately INR600 crores... debt level will be in the range of INR500 crores to INR600 crores by March '27". Strength: Moderate.
5. **Why finance cost rose** (company, annual report, not a call): "Finance cost has increased mainly due to increased working capital cycle on account of nature of projects where initially working capital requirement is more. The cost also increased due to increase in interest bearing mobilisation advances taken from client for execution of projects." AR FY26 MD&A, PDF p44 (printed 30), image checked; MD&A signed by Chairman Ashok Katariya, 11 Aug 2026. Same sentence pattern in FY25 and FY24 MD&A. Strength: Strong (3 consecutive annual reports + auditor-checked numbers).
6. **Revenue drivers (company's reasons):** "extended monsoons, delay in contract documentation and land approvals, slower pace of bids floated by the Authority" (AR FY26 PDF p43). The Q4 FY26 call (Satish Parakh p2) adds "rising prices of key materials such as cement, bitumen, steel, fuel, along with labor shortages". The company never gives slow collection as a reason for the revenue dip.
7. **NHAI payments:** no statement by management on NHAI payment timeliness was found in six transcripts and the FY26 AR (NOT FOUND). What exists: Acuite - "high dependence on state and central government authorities for receipt of payments" (24 Feb 2026 p3); NHAI's show-cause notice and settlement (section 5).
8. **Asset monetisation.** Paresh Mehta, Q1 FY27 call p8: "4 assets, we expect to clear by Q2, revised from Q1, largely because certain compliances to be done for handing over the project to the investors... by September end or first part of October definitely we should be able to get the money in... the other 2 assets need some time... by December and may be Q4". p9-10: total price "approximately INR1,100 crores"; 4 assets "approximately INR700 crores"; later "it's INR1,150 crores, just a rounding off" (p11).
9. **Guidance FY27.** Satish Parakh, Q4 FY26 call p6: "we are targeting 20% of revenue growth". Paresh Mehta p6: "Our estimation for FY '27 ... in the range of 9.5% to 10.5%". Then Q1 FY27 call p7 Satish Parakh: "guidance, we are lowering down from 20% to between 10% to 15% because this quarter has been flat due to various reasons and various uncertainties, particularly supply chain." Order inflow "INR6,000 to INR8,000 crores" (p7), earlier "INR8,000 crores to INR10,000 crores" (Q4 FY26 p6). Analyst Vaibhav Shah (p9): "EBITDA margins would be 9% to 9.5% for the entire year?" Paresh Mehta: "Yes." and, to "So we are lowering the guidance?": "Yes, by 0.5% because we lost something in this quarter".
10. **Receivable provisioning (CFO, Q2 FY26 call p9):** "Debtors which are slightly dated like 6 months and above, there is some provisioning done. And whenever the money is received, because these are money received from either SPVs of ABL or from Government. The moment we receive that, these ECL provisions get reversed." ECL charge in the cash flow, standalone: FY22 reversal of 9.6, FY23 reversal of 4.4, then 27.9 (FY24), 69.3 (FY25), 110.5 (FY26) (C; AR cash-flow pages). Q4 FY26 ECL charge Rs 28 Cr (CFO, Q4 FY26 call p6).
11. **Interest cost guide (CFO, Q1 FY27 call p19):** standalone FY26 Rs 313 Cr; FY27 "Rs 225-240 Cr"; FY28 "approximately INR200 crores, INR210 crores, assuming there is a increase in turnover".
