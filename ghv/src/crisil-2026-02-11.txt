Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities of GHV (India) Pvt Ltd (GHV-India, part of GHV group) to ‘Negative’ from ‘Stable’ while reaffirming the rating at ‘Crisil A’.  The short-term rating has been reaffirmed at ‘Crisil A1’. 
The revision in outlook reflects reduction in scale of operations as the GHV group is expected to generate operating income of around Rs 3,000 crore for fiscal 2026, which remains lower than earlier expectations. While this would translate into growth of nearly 23.5% against operating income of Rs 2,428 crore for fiscal 2025, it will still be less than Rs 3,360 crore achieved in fiscal 2024. While the operating income is impacted, the operating margin is expected to improve to 11-12% for fiscal 2026, from a low of 9.7% in the previous fiscal. Operating performance was impacted due to slow execution of two large projects, Coastal Road and CC roads; which together amount to Rs 4,230 crore and formed 33% of the order book as on September 30, 2025. Both the projects, located in Maharashtra, faced delay in receipt of approvals due to state elections in the previous fiscal, and execution during this fiscal was impacted by the prolonged monsoon. With these issues getting now resolved, execution is expected to pick up in the last quarter of fiscal 2026. The group expects to generate operating income of around Rs 1,500 crore in the last quarter of fiscal 2026, against around Rs 1,600 crore generated in the first nine months. 
Over the medium term, the scale of operations will be supported by a strong combined order book of Rs 12,717 crore as on September 30, 2025, translating into order book to revenue ratio of 5.2 times (on revenue for fiscal 2025), resulting in strong revenue visibility for the medium term. The order book remains healthy at Rs 8,420 crore even after adjusting for orders wherein letter of award or appointed date is yet to be received. While the order book is well diversified across segments - roads (43%), urban infrastructure (37%), solar and transmission (8%), railways (5%) and others (6%), it remains concentrated in terms of geography, with Maharashtra and Gujarat accounting for 45% and 15%, respectively, of the entire orderbook. Nevertheless, the management is also focused on geographical diversification and has added a cross-border project to build a ‘smart manufacturing hub’ in UAE, which forms 21% of the current order book. Pick up in pace of construction of delayed projects, timely receipt of appointed dates and expected pace of execution of new projects will remain a key monitorable.
In addition to weaker operating performance, the financial risk profile of the GHV group was also impacted on account of a stretch in working capital cycle as reflected in gross current assets (GCA) rose to 287 days as on March 31, 2025, from 116 days as on March 31, 2024, due to stretch in receivables and increase in unbilled revenue, given the delay in payment from the government of Maharashtra, due to state elections. Consequently, gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOL/TNW) ratios weakened to 0.99 times and 2.20 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025, with total external debt rising to Rs 843 crore in fiscal 2025 against Rs 530 crore in fiscal 2024. Pressure on the working capital cycle has started to ease with receivables and unbilled revenue reducing to Rs 660 crore as on September 30, 2025, from Rs 1,190 crore as on March 31, 2025. Consequently, debt has reduced to Rs 618 crore (excluding optionally convertible debentures of Rs 140 crore) as on September 30, 2025. Working capital cycle is expected to improve further and debt is expected to reduce further to around Rs 400 crore by March 31, 2026, and this is a key rating sensitivity factor. 
Sustained improvement in operating performance and working capital cycle is expected to support liquidity. Fund-based bank limit utilisation was moderate, averaging 87% for the past 12 months through January 2026, with peak utilisation reaching 99% in the second quarter of fiscal 2026. Part of the stretch was on account of reduction in sanctioned limits, as requested by the group, from Rs 173 crore in October 2024 to Rs 166 crore in September 2025 and further to Rs 97 crore in January 2026. Average utilisation has gradually reduced to 90% in the third quarter of the fiscal and sustenance at these levels will be key monitorable. Non-fund-based limits have been utilised at an average of 85% over the same period. The company also had unencumbered cash and equivalents of Rs 83 crore as on September 30, 2025, and is expected to maintain liquidity of Rs 80-100 crore.
Current liquidity and expected improvement in net cash accrual should sufficiently cover the balance equity commitment of around Rs 77.4 crore towards the under-construction hybrid annuity model (HAM) projects, along with the limited incremental working capital requirement and planned capital expenditure (capex) of around Rs 50 crore over the medium term. The GHV group currently holds five HAM projects, of which three are operational with a track record of receiving 1-3 annuities till date in a timely manner, without material deductions. The remaining two projects have achieved physical progress of 40% and 35%, respectively, and are expected to be completed by June 2026 and December 2026, respectively. 
Crisil Ratings has also revised its analytical approach to fully combine the business and financial risk profiles of GHV-India with GHV Infra Projects Ltd (GHV-Infra). This is because both the companies have entered into a business support agreement (BSA) for sharing all common resources including manpower, machinery, credit lines, etc. for execution of projects sub-contracted to GHV-Infra by GHV-India to enhance its technical and financial qualifications with GHV-India gradually transitioning the entire business to GHV-Infra. GHV-India is also expected to provide financial support to GHV-Infra, in case of any requirement and has also received an approval from shareholders of GHV-Infra for entering into related party transactions amounting to up to Rs 15,000 crore with GHV-Infra. GHV-Infra is a listed company (erstwhile named Sindhu Valley Technologies Ltd) and was acquired by the promoters of GHV-India in March 2025.
The ratings continue to reflect the extensive experience of the promoters in the construction segment and ramp up in orderbook providing revenue visibility, and the adequate financial risk profile of the company. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to risks posed by intense competition, geographic concentration risk and cyclicality inherent in the construction industry.

Analytical Approach
Crisil Ratings has fully consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of GHV-India and GHV-Infra, together referred to as the GHV group. This is because the companies have entered into a BSA for sharing of all common resources including manpower, machinery, credit lines, etc., for execution of projects sub-contracted to GHV-Infra by GHV-India to enhance GHV-Infra’s technical and financial qualifications. GHV-India is also expected to provide financial support to GHV-Infra, in case of any requirement and has also received an approval from shareholders of GHV-Infra for entering into related party transactions amounting to up to Rs 15,000 crore with GHV-Infra. The business of GHV-India is expected to be gradually moved to GHV-Infra over the medium-to-long term.  
Crisil Ratings has also combined the business and financial risk profiles of GHV group and its subsidiaries, associates and joint venture (JV) entities, which have been set up in the normal course of business to bid for or undertake engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) projects. Crisil Ratings has also moderately consolidated special purpose-vehicles (SPVs) where the GHV group has provided a corporate guarantee or shortfall undertaking, to the extent of support required over the medium term. The SPVs wherein CGs have been provided have not been fully consolidated as the CGs are expected to fall off once the projects are completed/start receiving annuities. 
Loans and advances from related parties and other unsecured loans of Rs 35 crore and Rs 492 crore respectively, as on March 31, 2025 (Rs 0.13 crore and Rs 345 as on March 31, 2024, respectively) are mainly mobilisation advances received from HAM SPVs and are expected to be repaid in line with project progress and receipt of milestone payments. These advances are also non-interest bearing and have been treated as advances. The group does not have any interest-bearing mobilisation advances. 
Crisil Ratings has treated optionally convertible debentures of Rs 140 crore from Authum Investments and Infrastructure Ltd (Authum), as quasi-equity. This is because these OCDs are expected to be converted to equity in the first half of fiscal 2027 (the agreement stipulates that the same can be converted in 15 months from date of allotment). Further, while these instruments bear a coupon of 18% per annum, the same is expected to be accrued.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Extensive experience of the promoters 
The promoters have been in the infrastructure industry for around five decades and have forged strong relationships with customers, suppliers and contractors. Access to in-house manpower and fleet of machinery leads to superior execution with no delays and limited reliance on third parties. The promoters have also acquired a strategic stake in Sindhu Valley Technologies Ltd, a listed entity for transitioning the business to GHV-Infra. 
GHV-India has executed a BSA for sharing all common resources, including manpower, machinery, credit lines, etc., with GHV-Infra, and shall extend necessary support to help the latter execute the sub-contracted projects, so as to enhance its technical and financial qualifications. GHV-India is also expected to provide financial support to GHV-Infra, in case of any requirement and has also received an approval from shareholders of GHV-Infra to enter into related party transactions amounting to up to Rs 15,000 crore with GHV-Infra.
The promoters planned to ramp up the orderbook, having incurred capex of Rs 66 crore in fiscals 2023 and 2024, to enhance capacities for executing a larger orderbook. The management now plans to incur limited capex of Rs 50 crore in fiscal 2027. Furthermore, it is focused on executing EPC projects wherein no upfront equity investments shall be required. This would help limit capital blocked in projects and sustain moderate leverage in the medium term. The operating margin is supported by no subcontracting expenses, owned fleet of machinery and equipment, and early completion bonuses. Additionally, GHV bids for projects backed by strong counterparties, such as Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai, Government of Gujarat and Karnataka Neeravari Nigam Ltd, to name a few. Apart from roads and bridges, GHV is also focusing on alternative projects in segments such as industrial infrastructure development, urban development, railways, solar, flue gas desulphurisation and pipelines. The management has also diversified operations internationally with a project in Ras-al-Khaimah (UAE). 
Ramp up in orderbook providing revenue visibility
Orders worth Rs 12,717 crore as on September 30, 2025, translate to orderbook to revenue ratio of 5.2 times (on revenue for fiscal 2025), resulting in strong revenue visibility in the medium term. The management plans to ramp up the orderbook to Rs 20,000 crore in the near term. GHV-India has successfully added orders worth Rs 8,300 crore over the 12 months ending September 30, 2025. However, certain projects awarded in fiscal 2025 are still awaiting appointed date. The order book remains healthy at Rs 8,420 crore with orderbook to revenue ratio of 3.4 times even after adjusting for orders wherein letter of award or appointed date is yet to be received. 
Furthermore, orders are diversified across segments, such as roads (43%), urban infrastructure development projects (37%), solar and transmission (8%), railways (5%) and others (6%). This reduces susceptibility to variation in revenue from focus on one segment. However, geographic concentration remains high, with Maharashtra and Gujarat accounting for 45% and 15%, respectively, of the orderbook. The management has undertaken a cross-border project to develop a ‘smart manufacturing hub’ on the border of UAE, in Ras-al-Khaimah, which accounts for 21% of the orderbook for diversification.
Revenue has recorded a compound annual growth rate of 23% over the past five fiscals, even though the scale of operations has weakened from earlier expectations. The group expects to generate operating income of Rs 3,000 crore in fiscal 2026, reflecting 23.5% growth year-on-year (Rs 2,248 crore reported for fiscal 2025, as against Rs 3,360 crore in fiscal 2024). Operating performance was impacted due to slow execution of two large projects, Coastal Road and CC roads; which together amount to Rs 4,230 crore and formed 33% of the order book as on September 30, 2025. Both the projects, located in Maharashtra, faced delay in receipt of approvals due to state elections in the previous fiscal, and execution during this fiscal was impacted by the prolonged monsoon. With these issues now resolved, execution should pick up in the last quarter of fiscal 2026. The group expects to generate operating income of around Rs 1,500 crore in the last quarter of fiscal 2026, against around Rs 1,600 crore generated in the first nine months. While operating income has been impacted, operating margin is expected to improve to 11-12% for the current fiscal, from a low of 9.7% in the previous fiscal. Ability of GHV to turnaround the operating performance and sustain its margin by capitalising on the orderbook remains monitorable.
Adequate financial risk profile
The financial risk profile is likely to improve in fiscal 2026, after having moderated in fiscal 2025, due to pressure on working capital management. GCAs rose to 287 days as on March 31, 2025, from 116 days as on March 31, 2024, due to stretch in receivables and increase in unbilled revenue, given the delay in payment from the government of Maharashtra, due to state elections. Consequently, gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOL/TNW) ratios weakened to 0.99 time and 2.20 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025, with total external debt rising to Rs 843 crore in fiscal 2025 (Rs 530 crore in fiscal 2024). Pressure on the working capital cycle has started to ease with receivables and unbilled revenue reducing to Rs 660 crore as on September 30, 2025, from Rs 1,190 crore as on March 31, 2025. Consequently, debt has reduced to Rs 618 crore (excluding optionally convertible debentures of Rs 140 crore) as on September 30, 2025. Working capital cycle is expected to improve further and debt may be brought down further to around Rs 400 crore by March 31, 2026, and this is a key rating sensitivity factor. 
Sustained improvement in operating performance and working capital cycle should support liquidity. Fund-based bank-limit utilisation was moderate, averaging 87% for the 12 months through January 2026, with peak utilisation reaching 99% in the second quarter of fiscal 2026. Part of the stretch was on account of reduction in sanctioned limits, as requested by the group, from Rs 173 crore in October 2024 to Rs 166 crore in September 2025 and further to Rs 97 crore in January 2026. Average utilisation has gradually reduced to 90% in the third quarter of the fiscal and sustenance at these levels will be key monitorable. Non-fund-based limits have been utilised at an average of 85% over the same period. The company also had unencumbered cash and equivalents of Rs 83 crore as on September 30, 2025, and is expected to maintain liquidity of Rs 80-100 crore.
The financial risk profile is also supported by investment of Rs 140 crore by Authum in GHV-Infra; the management’s stance to incur only limited capex of Rs 50 crore in fiscal 2027, and undertake only EPC projects with no incremental equity investments in the medium term. Accordingly, internal accrual generated by GHV shall suffice to fund the balance equity commitment in under-construction HAM projects and partially cover the incremental working capital expenses. Gearing is expected to remain below one time and TOL/TNW may come down to 1-1.3 times in the medium term. Interest cover is likely to improve to 5-8 times in medium term from 3.3 times in fiscal 2025.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses
Limited geographical and revenue diversification
While GHV has executed projects across 10 states in India, Maharashtra and Gujarat form 45% and 15% of the current orderbook, respectively. These two states are key focus regions, backed by strong supplier relationships, established market presence of the group and execution synergies. GHV’s ability to diversify to other regions, while maintaining profitability, remains to be seen. Further, operations continue to be focused on road projects, along with rising category of urban and industrial development projects, which contribute a major portion of the current orderbook. 
Operating performance remains susceptible to concentration risk, arising from focus on road projects. Though GHV is focusing on project alternatives in other infrastructure segments as well, its ability to scale up these segments profitably remains to be seen. Further, the top four orders form 70% of the executable orderbook exposing the company to execution risk, amidst delay in appointed dates or other regulatory approvals. The company also faces technical risk arising from industrial and urban development projects in India and abroad, given the need to liaison with authorities. Timely implementation of orders within the budgeted cost will remain a key rating sensitivity factor.
Orders worth Rs 12,717 crore, as on September 30, 2025, provide adequate revenue visibility. However, certain projects awarded in fiscal 2025, are still awaiting appointed dates. Excluding such others, the orderbook stands at Rs 8,420 crore, translating to orderbook to revenue ratio of 3.4 times. Diversification of orders across segments reduces susceptibility to variation in revenue from focus on one segment. 
Exposure to intense competition and cyclicality inherent in the construction industry
While increased focus of the Government of India (GoI) on the infrastructure sector, especially roads and highways, should benefit players in the medium term, most of these projects are tender-based. The competitive scenario necessitates that players bid aggressively to bag the contracts, which restricts the operating margin to a moderate level. Also, given the cyclicality inherent in the construction industry, the company’s ability to maintain profitability margin through operating efficiency is critical.
Revenue is also susceptible to economic cycles that impact the construction industry. Furthermore, GHV mainly caters to state government agencies, whose expenditure is directly linked to the economy. Nevertheless, GHV is more focused on undertaking EPC orders from state counterparties with fund balance, to execute projects involving less competition and fetching a decent margin.  
GCAs rose to 287 days as on March 31, 2025, from 116 days as on March 31, 2024, due to stretch in receivables and increase in unbilled revenue, given the delay in payment from the government of Maharashtra, due to state elections. Consequently, gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOL/TNW) ratios weakened to 0.99 time and 2.20 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025, with total external debt rising to Rs 843 crore in fiscal 2025 (Rs 530 crore in fiscal 2024). Pressure on the working capital cycle has started to ease with receivables and unbilled revenue reducing to Rs 660 crore as on September 30, 2025, from Rs 1,190 crore as on March 31, 2025. Consequently, debt has reduced to Rs 618 crore (excluding optionally convertible debentures of Rs 140 crore) as on September 30, 2025. Working capital cycle is expected to improve further and debt may be brought down further to around Rs 400 crore by March 31, 2026, and this is a key rating sensitivity factor.

Liquidity Adequate
Liquidity is supported by expected net cash accrual of Rs 180-320 crore per annum, should comfortably cover the debt obligation of up to Rs 110-130 crore over the medium term. The management plans to incur limited capex of Rs 50 crore in fiscal 2027. Equity commitment of around Rs 77.4 crore, towards the ongoing HAM projects, and incremental working capital expenses will be funded through internal accrual. Fund-based bank limit utilisation averaged 87% for the 12 months through January 2026 with utilisation peaking at 99% in the second quarter of fiscal 2026. Part of the stretch was on account of reduction in sanctioned limits, as requested by the group, from Rs 173 crore in October 2024 to Rs 166 crore in September 2025 and further to Rs 97 crore in January 2026. Bank limit utilisation gradually reduced to 90% in the third quarter of fiscal 2026. Furthermore, the company had unencumbered cash and cash equivalents of Rs 83 crore as on September 30, 2025, and is expected to always maintain liquidity of Rs 80-100 crore.

About the Company
The Mumbai-based company, GHV-India was incorporated in 2009, by Mr Jahid Vijapura and his three brothers, Mr Mustaq Vijapura, Mr Farooq Vijapura and Mr Shafi Vijapura, by reconstituting a partnership firm, GH Vijapura & Co. The firm was set up in 1965, by the late Mr Hussein M Vijapura, and was engaged in infrastructure development.
GHV-India is an EPC contractor, which undertakes construction of roads, bridges, dams, irrigation projects and airport runways. The company executed its first large irrigation project in 1977, in partnership with the Government of Gujarat. Post that, the group has successfully completed numerous irrigation projects with other state governments. 
GHV-India has expertise in construction of runways/resurfacing, parking bays and taxiways. The company has worked with airports in Mumbai, Pune, Vadodara, Udaipur, Patna, Guwahati, Belgaum, Diu and Kochi. Currently, the orderbook comprises mostly of national and state highway projects.
The promoters acquired a strategic stake in GHV-Infra (erstwhile Sindhu Valley Technologies Ltd), a listed company, in March 2025. The business of GHV-India is expected to be gradually transferred to GHV-Infra over the medium to long term.
