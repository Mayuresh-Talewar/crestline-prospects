# GHV (India) Pvt Ltd: working-capital problems (GH-4, 7 Oct 2026)

Source: CRISIL Ratings documents on GHV (India) Pvt Ltd only (src/crisil-*.txt). CRISIL-adjusted and rounded. Rs crore.
Entity labels (README rulings): FY24 and earlier = **GHV (India) standalone**. FY25 onwards = **combined (GHV India + GHV Infra, CRISIL)**. GHV Infra Projects Ltd's own orders and figures are never used. Computed figures come from `calc/ghv-savings.output.txt`.
Framing: CRISIL itself says the pressure is easing (receivables + unbilled 1,190 to 660 in six months). These are problems of visibility and timing, written with respect for a company that has grown for five decades.

**Correction to data.md / ticket:** the ">85% of orders from NHAI" line is in CRISIL **15 Sep 2023** (§ Strengths), not 18 Mar 2024. The 18-Mar-24 rationale names NHAI, AAI and MES as counterparties with no share. Cited below as 15-Sep-23.

- Four years of steady improvement (222 to 116), then one year that undid all of it. CRISIL (11-Feb-26): "gross current assets (GCA) rose to 287 days as on March 31, 2025, from 116 days as on March 31, 2024".
- Computed: 287 days at Rs 6.66 cr a day (FY25 combined income / 365) is about Rs 1,911.5 cr of gross current assets at 31-Mar-25.
- Caveat to print: FY25 is a combined figure and FY24 is standalone; CRISIL sets the two side by side in one sentence, and so may we, labelled.

## P2. Receivables and unbilled work: Rs 1,190 cr, about 179 days of income

- Receivables + unbilled revenue Rs 1,190 cr at 31-Mar-25, Rs 660 cr at 30-Sep-25 (combined, CRISIL 11-Feb-26).
- Computed: 1,190 is 178.7 days of FY25 combined income, and 62% of the implied gross current assets.
- Earlier the receivable cycle was short: "healthy receivables cycle of 40-50 days compared with ~100 days earlier" (GHV India, CRISIL 18-Mar-24). Retention counted in receivables: Rs 23 cr Mar-23, Rs 45 cr Mar-24 (CRISIL 7-Jan-25).
- Rs 530 cr came out between March and September 2025: the company has shown it can move this balance. The question is how to see the next stretch before it reaches the year-end figure.

## P3. Delayed Maharashtra government payments (CRISIL's stated cause)

- CRISIL 11-Feb-26: the stretch was "due to stretch in receivables and increase in unbilled revenue, given the delay in payment from the government of Maharashtra, due to state elections."
- Maharashtra is 45% of the combined order book at 30-Sep-25 (CRISIL 11-Feb-26). The counterparties CRISIL names in Feb-26 are the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai, Government of Gujarat and Karnataka Neeravari Nigam Ltd.
- Two different waits sit inside "receivables + unbilled": work done and not yet certified or billed, and bills raised and not yet paid. The first is partly the company's own desk; the second is the client's. CRISIL publishes them as one number.

## P4. From one strong payer to state and municipal payers

- 15-Sep-23 (GHV India): "over 85% of the orders are coming from a strong counterparty, that is NHAI."
- 18-Mar-24 (GHV India): counterparties named as NHAI, AAI and MES.
- 11-Feb-26 (combined): counterparties named as MCGM, Government of Gujarat and Karnataka Neeravari Nigam; Maharashtra 45% and Gujarat 15% of the order book; top four orders 70% of the executable book.
- The payer mix moved from a central authority to state and municipal bodies, and the stretch followed. Each payer pays on its own clock, which a company-level GCA figure averages away.

- Gearing 0.99x and TOL/TNW 2.20x at 31-Mar-25 (combined, CRISIL 11-Feb-26). Outlook revised to Negative on 11-Feb-26, tied to "susceptibility to variation in working capital cycle leading to higher-than-expected reliance on debt".
- Fund-based limit utilisation (12-month averages): 66% to Jun-23 (15-Sep-23), 65% to Jan-24 (18-Mar-24), 78% from Oct-23 (7-Jan-25), all GHV India; 87% to Jan-26 with a 99% peak in Q2 FY26 and 90% in Q3 FY26 (combined, 11-Feb-26). CRISIL notes part of the rise came from limits cut at the group's request: Rs 173 cr (Oct-24) to 166 (Sep-25) to 97 (Jan-26).
- Computed: the gap from Rs 618 cr to the ~Rs 400 cr CRISIL expects is Rs 218 cr, which is 32.7 days of FY25 combined income.
- No interest rate on the working-capital lines is published. The only rate CRISIL states is the 18% coupon on the Rs 140 cr OCD from Authum, treated as quasi-equity and accrued. So no interest saving is printed anywhere.

## What their books cannot tell them

A rating rationale and a trial balance both report the year-end balance. Neither answers:
- **Which project and which bill.** Rs 1,190 cr is one number; nobody can see from it which RA bill, on which site, for which client, makes up the 179 days.
- **Unbilled against unpaid.** Work executed and not yet certified (the company's measurement and submission, the client's certification) is a different queue from an invoice the client has not paid. CRISIL publishes them together.
- **Whose desk, since when.** How long each bill has sat at measurement, submission, certification and payment, and whose act ends each wait.
- **Against which terms.** Each contract's own certification window and payment term, so a 90-day payer is not flagged for behaving normally and a 30-day payer running at 120 is.
- **Before year-end.** The stretch became visible in the March 2025 balance; the 99% peak arrived in Q2 FY26. A weekly view by payer would have shown Maharashtra's delays as they built.
