## 1. The rationales' own words on working capital and liquidity

Oldest first.

1. SMERA, 30 Jan 2016 (R1 p1, text layer, prose): "The ratings are constrained by the working capital intensive operations inherent with the construction industry and customer and geographical concentration risk."
2. CRISIL, 10 Nov 2016 (R2 p1, text layer, prose): the ratings "continue to reflect its working-capital-intensive operations, and customer and geographic concentration in its revenue profile."
3. India Ratings, 11 Sep 2017 (R3, web text, no page image exists): "The company's debtors account about 30% of its total revenue. JMMIPL's utilisation of fund-based working capital limits was 94%-95% during the 12 months ended May 2017." And: "The decline was on account of an increase in debtor collection period to 172 days in FY17 (FY16: 131 days)."
4. Brickwork, 07 May 2021 (R6 p2, text layer, prose): "Liquidity is characterised by expected cash & cash equivalents of Rs. 15.08 crs in FY2021, cash credit utilization level is at 87% and current ratio of 2.03x as on 30.9.2020."
5. CARE, 12 Oct 2022 (R7 p3, image-checked): "The monthly collection efficiency on an average is above 55%, thus aiding the liquidity. There are no significant long pending debtors in the books of the company." And: "The average maximum utilisation of fund-based limits and non-fund-based limits stood at 93.60% and 74.74% for last twelve months ended August 31st, 2022."
6. CRISIL, 27 Jan 2023 (R8 p5 to 6, image-checked): "Gross current assets were at 168-181 days over the three fiscals ended March 31, 2022. This is due moderate debtor of 80 days, due to delay in realization from the government authorities and higher revenue booked in March every year. Inventory was 73 days as on March 31, 2022 which is due to high work in progress gets built up due to milestone based billing." And: "Bank limit utilization is high at around 88 percent for the past twelve months ended October 2022."
7. CARE, 21 Nov 2023 (R9 p2, image-checked; repeated word for word in R11, 05 Jul 2024): "With the elongated working capital cycle at 87 days in FY23 (FY22: 74 days) owing to high inventory and receivable days, the maximum utilisation of fund-based limit stood at 82% for last twelve months ended September 30, 2023. The gross current asset days continues to remain above 200 days as on March 31, 2023." The paragraph opens on promoter support [S5].
8. Infomerics, 28 Mar 2024 (R10 p4, image-checked): "Construction business, by its nature, is working capital intensive as a large amount of working capital remains blocked in earnest money deposits and retention money. High work in progress gets built up due to milestone-based billing. This apart, its clients are government departments/entities having various procedural requirements where payments are relatively slow. The operating cycle stood at 91 days in FY23 driven by an inventory cycle of 81 days and an average collection period of 71 days. Further, the company's creditor's period stood at 61 days in FY23." And: "Gross current assets were 275 days as on March 31, 2023, as against 266 days as on March 31, 2022."
9. Infomerics, 28 Mar 2024 (R10 p5, text layer, prose): "The average fund based working capital utilization for the 12 months ended December 2023 stood moderate at around 66% which provides limited liquidity buffer."
10. Brickwork, 28 Jul 2025 (R14 p4 to 5, image-checked p5): "as the majority of orders are from government agencies, this results in high dependence on timely clearances for tenders and payments." And (R14 p6, image-checked): "Additionally, average fund-based and non-fund-based working capital utilisation in the past 12 months were ~70% and ~85%, providing a liquidity buffer."

What the trend says, in plain words: the debtor and inventory days the agencies printed sit at 70 to 80 each from FY22 to FY23, but the gross current asset days ran 168 to 181 (FY20 to FY22, CRISIL) and 266 to 275 (FY22 to FY23, Infomerics). The gap between those two is the money that never shows up as a debtor or as inventory: deposits, retention and unbilled work. Two agencies differ on how to count it (CRISIL's 168 to 181 against Infomerics' 266 for the same FY22), so the level is uncertain and the direction is not: it grew. Fund-based utilisation eased from the mid 90s (2017, 2022) to about 70% (2025) while non-fund-based utilisation stayed at 80% to 85%.
