# J. M. Mhatre Infra: problems (JM-2+3, LIGHT)

Frame: a Maharashtra road and bridge builder with a large unexecuted book, Rs 2,035.11 Cr at 31 May 2025 (R14 p3, published 28 Jul 2025), about 65% of it roads and bridges (R14 p4), every project in Maharashtra (R14 p2). That book will be turned into cash through RA bills: the company measures the work, prepares, reviews and submits each bill and invoices it; CIDCO and state government bodies acknowledge, certify and pay it. The three problems below are about how long that conversion holds the company's money, told from the company's own last published cycle.

Rules held here: every figure is standalone and carries its document, page and date (source codes as in data.md). One vintage per chart: all three charts use FY23 audited figures from one document, R10 (public rating rationale, published 28 Mar 2024). No other agency's day count for FY23 is shown beside it. No day count exists after FY23, so nothing here says what the cycle is today.

## Problem 1. Collection: 71 days of revenue waiting on government payers

- **Figure.** Average collection period 71 days in FY23; inventory cycle 81 days; creditors period 61 days; operating cycle 91 days (81 + 71 less 61, reproduced by jm-calc s4).
- **Source.** R10 p4, published 28 Mar 2024, FY23 audited, standalone. Image-checked.
- **Their words.** "its clients are government departments/entities having various procedural requirements where payments are relatively slow." (R10 p4)
- **Why it matters forward.** Every rupee of the Rs 2,035.11 Cr book passes through this wait. At the FY25 scale a day of income is Rs 2.19 Cr (FY25 total operating income Rs 798.93 Cr, provisional, published 28 Jul 2025, divided by 365; calc). FY23 days (the latest published) valued at FY25 provisional income per day; on FY23 income the figures would be larger.
- **Chart (FY23, R10 only).** Four horizontal bars on one day scale: inventory 81, collection 71, creditors 61 (drawn as a span that shortens the cycle; the label reads 61 with no sign), operating cycle 91.
- **What the rationale cannot tell.** Which site the 71 days sit on; which client body (CIDCO, or which state body); which RA bills; whether the bill is waiting to be certified or is certified and waiting to be paid; whose desk it sits on (ours to measure, prepare, review, submit or invoice; the client body's to acknowledge, certify or pay); since when.

## Problem 2. Work done and not yet billable: 81 days of inventory

- **Figure.** Inventory cycle 81 days in FY23 (the largest of the three legs).
- **Source.** R10 p4, published 28 Mar 2024, FY23 audited, standalone. Image-checked.
- **Their words.** "High work in progress gets built up due to milestone-based billing." (R10 p4)
- **Reading.** On a milestone contract, executed work cannot be billed until the company has measured the milestone and the client body has accepted it. Much of the 81 days is therefore built work waiting for a bill, not material in a yard. The rationale does not split the two, so this file does not either.
- **Chart (FY23, R10 only).** The same cycle bars as Problem 1 with the inventory bar emphasised: inventory 81, collection 71, creditors 61, operating cycle 91.
- **What the rationale cannot tell.** Which project and which milestone; how much is work in progress and how much is stock; whether the wait is ours (measuring the work, preparing, reviewing and submitting the bill) or the client body's (acknowledging and certifying it); how long each milestone has been open.

## Problem 3. Money outside the debtor and stock lines: gross current asset days 275

- **Figure.** Gross current assets 275 days at 31 Mar 2023, against 266 days at 31 Mar 2022, on the same document; the operating cycle for FY23 is 91 days.
- **Source.** R10 p4, published 28 Mar 2024, FY23 audited, standalone. Image-checked.
- **Their words.** "a large amount of working capital remains blocked in earnest money deposits and retention money." (R10 p4)
- **Reading.** The current assets run far beyond what the debtor and inventory days explain. The rationale names deposits and retention as part of the difference; it does not size either.
- **Chart (R10 only).** Two bars, gross current asset days 266 (31 Mar 2022) and 275 (31 Mar 2023), with a reference rule at the FY23 operating cycle of 91.
- **What the rationale cannot tell.** How much is retention, how much is deposits and how much is anything else; which project and which client body holds each; which retention has met its release milestone; since when.
