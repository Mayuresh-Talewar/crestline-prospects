# Om Power Transmission: depth.md (story angle; figures from data.md only)

## Respect first
A 15-year-old Ahmedabad EPC company that grew revenue from Rs 182.76 Cr (FY24) to Rs 449.16 Cr (FY26), lifted PAT to Rs 40.02 Cr, listed on NSE and BSE in April 2026 and was upgraded by CRISIL to BBB+/Stable. AA-class GETCO registration, 400 kV execution, 765 kV eligibility, order book Rs 621 Cr, 82% from public utilities. Audited FY26 shows profit growing 81%.

## The question (where bills wait)
Revenue grew 61% in FY26 and trade receivables grew 146% (Rs 90.11 Cr to 221.42 Cr). Debtor days (Crestline's arithmetic on the audited FY26 accounts) moved from 117.7 to 179.9; unbilled work (contract asset) from Rs 6.68 Cr to 24.05 Cr (8.7 to 19.5 days). The cash flow statement states the result: receivables absorbed Rs 134.71 Cr, vendors funded Rs 78.02 Cr, and operating cash flow was Rs 25.63 Cr negative against Rs 40.02 Cr of profit. Management says its working-capital cycle is "around 75 days"; the same arithmetic gives 74.5 days, with the cycle held down by 129.8 days of payables, Rs 44.34 Cr of it owed to micro and small suppliers.
What the accounts cannot show: which bill, on which project, at which desk (measurement, certification by the utility, invoice, payment), since when, and how much of the Rs 221.42 Cr is retention (retention is inside receivables and not separately disclosed). Ageing shows Rs 176.11 Cr not yet due and Rs 50.58 Cr past due (Rs 30.27 Cr under 6 months). 64.0% of revenue comes from one customer, and 93% from ten.
Crestline angle: a bill-by-bill clock above the ERP, so the question "what is stuck, whose desk, for how long, how many rupees" has an answer before the quarter ends. CRISIL already lists "better collections from its major customer" as monitorable and a stretch beyond 250 GCA days as a downgrade trigger.

## Safe lines
- Own words: "disciplined working capital management" (MD, p.15); cycle "around 75 days" (p.27); fresh-issue money is "directed largely towards long-term working capital" (p.26).
- Never say the company mismanages cash. Say what the money did: receivables rose faster than sales.
- Paying vendors later is never a saving. DPO is shown as a fact, not a target.
