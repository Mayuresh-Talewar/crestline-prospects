# SJ Contracts: problems (SJ-2, 2026-10-09)

Respect first. All figures: CRISIL Ratings, standalone, INR crore, fiscal years to 31 March. Documents: rationale of 15 Apr 2025 (A15) and rationale of 1 Feb 2024 (F24).

SJ Contracts Private Limited grew operating income from Rs 610.18 Cr (FY23) to Rs 963.40 Cr (FY24) and reported profit after tax from Rs 17.86 Cr to Rs 41.16 Cr (A15). CRISIL reaffirmed Crisil A-/Stable and A2+ on 15 Apr 2025 (A15). That is a company trusted with a great deal of work. The problems below are questions the published accounts cannot answer, not faults.

## Problem 1. The book of work is larger than the year behind it
- Order book Rs 1,770 Cr as on 30 Nov 2023 (F24) and Rs 2,484 Cr as on 31 Mar 2025, "to be executed within the next 12-36 months" (A15). About 2.6 times FY24 operating income (2,484 / 963.40; the dates differ, so it is printed only with that wording).
- As that book executes, every month adds running-account bills that each wait for several desks. Nothing published says how long.
- What the accounts cannot tell: which site, which bill, which desk, since when.

## Problem 2. Income rose 57.9% in one year, and the accounts show the year, not the bill
- Rs 610.18 Cr to Rs 963.40 Cr, FY23 to FY24 (A15); 963.40 / 610.18 less 1 = 57.9% (computed).
- A year of growth is a total. Where each rupee of it sat between work done and money received is recorded only in the bill-level dates of the company's own ERP.

## Problem 3. A day has a price, and no published figure says how many days
- Debtor, creditor and working-capital days are not published in either rationale (F24, A15). So the booklet does not state SJ's wait; it prices one day.
- One day at FY24 operating income is Rs 963.40 / 365 = Rs 2.6395 Cr. Ten days = Rs 26.39 Cr; thirty days = Rs 79.18 Cr. Scenarios, not forecasts. No interest (no rate published). Rows overlap and are never added.

Kept out (Garvis only): sensitive-garvis.md items, FY25 net worth estimate, margin range, interest coverage, founding year, titles, CIN, concentration and unbilled-WIP claims.
