### Letter, Edition 1 (Chairman and Managing Director)

Dear Mr. Debnath,

The Annual Report puts OVAL's order book at ₹796 Cr, about 5.2 times FY26 revenue. It also shows profit of ₹14.81 Cr and operations that used ₹29.96 Cr of cash in the same year. Part of that is what growing fast on government work costs, and the report says so itself.

This booklet asks one narrow thing: how quickly executed work becomes a bill, and a bill becomes cash. Unbilled work was ₹16.86 Cr of the ₹35.07 Cr receivable at 31 March 2026. Billing ten days sooner releases about ₹4.20 Cr once, ₹3.26 Cr on works revenue alone. These are modest sums, and I have not inflated them.

Billing is the one step OVAL carries out on its own; certification and payment sit with the client. I would like to show you how this reads on two or three government EPC projects, read-only, before the order book multiplies the work.

(letter body: 150 words)

### p14 Chapter 12: read-only
Headline: It reads the books. It never writes to them.
Fixed: read-only access; nothing is posted back to any system; every figure the source cannot supply reads NA with a reason; Crestline is not an ERP, not accounting software, not project management; it covers EPC works contracts, and trading and other services are out of scope; it does not keep a bank-guarantee register.
Before any reading: an adapter for OVAL's accounting system is built first. Until then Crestline does not connect to it.
