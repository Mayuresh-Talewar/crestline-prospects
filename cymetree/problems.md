# Cymetree Projects LLP: problems (deepened 2026-10-10)

Respect first. All figures INR crore, CRISIL Ratings rationale 31 Jul 2026 (standalone, years ended 31 March) unless marked. Company-site lines are self-description only (cymetreeprojects.com, accessed 10 Oct 2026). Ownership rule: measure, prepare, review, submit, invoice are Cymetree's; acknowledge, certify, pay are the client's. Nothing from KEPT OUT or sensitive-garvis.md. Where a source does not state a cause, the entry says what would be measured.

Client mix shares, creditor days, MSME dues, retention, contract assets, ageing, operating cash flow and named clients are NOT FOUND (see data.md "Depth"). Problems below are therefore stated at the depth the evidence supports and no deeper.

## 1. Growth outruns the bill route (stage: billing and certification; owner: ours first, then the client's)
- Figures: operating income 4.8 (FY23, CRISIL text) to 69.76 (FY24) to 154.50 (FY25, table); PAT 6.06 to 17.97; order book 846 (May 2026), 12 to 24 months. CRISIL's FY26 estimate 340 to 345 prints once, as an estimate.
- Mechanism: every new site adds running bills that must be measured, checked, prepared, reviewed and submitted (ours) before the client acknowledges and certifies (theirs). CRISIL does not say where bills wait; it says the working capital need "shall increase" with revenue.
- Crestline would measure: days in each billing stage per bill from the ERP dates, and which stage grew fastest as sites were added.
- Framing: a firm that tripled its book in a year has no single place where bills wait; the question is where each one does.

## 2. The long credit period (stage: collection; owner: the client's clock, ours to chase and document)
- Figures: debtor days "around 120 to 145" over the three fiscals ended 31 March 2026; CRISIL's stated reason: the firm "is required to extend long credit period in line with the industry standards". Gross current assets 130 to 150 days "majorly driven by the debtor days" (GCA range kept out of print).
- Mechanism (stated by the source): the credit period given is long by industry practice, so the wait after invoicing is the client's. The source gives no split between credit period and delay past it.
- Crestline would measure: per invoice, days to payment against the payment term in each contract, so that "within the term" and "past the term" are separated. Ranges cannot do this.
- Framing: the term is the client's to honour; Cymetree's part is invoice quality and follow-up date.

## 3. Credit period against a thin bank cushion (stage: cash; owner: shared)
- Figures: bank limit utilisation "moderate at around 54%" over the twelve months ended May 2026 (CRISIL). CRISIL also notes the promoters "may extend support in the form of unsecured loans to meet its working capital requirements". Cash balance, limits, lender and ratio levels are KEPT OUT of print.
- Mechanism: the receivable is funded from bank lines and partner support while the bill waits. CRISIL names a stretch in the working capital cycle as a downward factor, so the wait is also a credit matter.
- Crestline would measure: for each bill, the days it is funded, ranked by rupees, so the biggest waits are released first.
- Framing: print only the utilisation and the word CRISIL uses ("moderate").
