# Jyoti Structures: two working-capital questions on the running business (JS-3, fixed in JS-4)

Jyoti Structures Limited, **standalone**. Every figure below is from the company's Annual Report 2025-26 (AR FY26, submitted to the exchanges 22 Jul 2026) unless another document is named, and is graded in data.md. All ageing figures use the AR FY26 report's own Mar 2025 and Mar 2026 columns (one vintage), and Mar 2025 days use the FY25 revenue printed as the comparative in the same report (Rs 498.04 Cr, AR FY26 p89). Days are Crestline arithmetic: balance divided by that year's revenue from operations, times 365.

**The story.** A fast rebuild. Revenue from operations was Rs 750.87 Cr in FY26, up 50.76% (AR FY26 p89). Q1 FY27 revenue was Rs 251.46 Cr, up 61.00% (Q1 FY27 results p2, 4 Aug 2026). The order book stood at Rs 2,274 Cr on 1 Apr 2026 (AR FY26 p15), 3.03 times FY26 revenue. The question for the next two years is not whether the work comes in. It is how fast each new bill turns into cash.

## P1. The running book: Rs 238.93 Cr, 116 days of revenue
**Document:** AR FY26, trade receivables ageing note, p124 (filed 22 Jul 2026); revenue p89. **Standalone.**

**The figure.** Receivables up to three years past due were Rs 238.93 Cr at 31 Mar 2026: 116 days of FY26 revenue.

**Trend, one vintage (AR FY26 p124):**
| As at | Running book (up to 3 years) | Days of that year's revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 31 Mar 2025 | Rs 299.54 Cr | 220 days |
| 31 Mar 2026 | Rs 238.93 Cr | 116 days |

**The reading.** The rebuild is working. In a year when revenue rose 50.76%, the running book came down from 220 days of revenue to 116, and from Rs 299.54 Cr to Rs 238.93 Cr. That is the team billing more work and turning it into cash faster at the same time. Anything Crestline adds comes on top of that progress. The task now is to hold the pace while the Rs 2,274 Cr order book is billed out. At FY26 revenue, each day taken off the running book releases about Rs 2.057 Cr (Crestline arithmetic).

**Context panel inside the P1 chart: the newest part of the same book.** Labelled "same cash as above, not added". Rs 214.18 Cr of the Rs 238.93 Cr was less than six months past its due date at 31 Mar 2026: 104 days of FY26 revenue, against Rs 176.04 Cr and 129 days at 31 Mar 2025 (AR FY26 p124). The bucket grew in rupees with the business and shrank in days: fresh bills are turning faster. The company's policy reads: "Payment is generally due upon receipt of the invoice, payable within 90 days or less" (AR FY26 p105).

**What the accounts cannot tell.** The note is one total per age band for the whole company, and it counts age from the **due date of payment**, not from the day the work was done or the bill was raised. The days spent measuring, preparing, reviewing and submitting the bill, the client acknowledging and certifying it, and the invoice being raised do not appear in it at all. It does not say which transmission line the money belongs to, which utility or developer owes it, which RA bill it is, whether part of it is retention waiting for a contract period to end, or whether the next move sits on the company's desk or the client's.

## P3. Less client money now funds the work
**Document:** AR FY26, note 22, p100, and accounting policy p105 (filed 22 Jul 2026). **Standalone.**

**The figure.** Advances from customers were Rs 92.92 Cr at 31 Mar 2026, 45 days of FY26 revenue.

**Trend, one vintage (AR FY26 p100):**
| As at | Advances from customers | Days of that year's revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 31 Mar 2025 | Rs 134.21 Cr | 98 days |
| 31 Mar 2026 | Rs 92.92 Cr | 45 days |

**The reading.** This is how advances are meant to work. The company describes them as money "used to meet working capital requirements at the time of project mobilization stage" (AR FY26 p105), and as jobs move past mobilisation the advance is recovered through the bills. With less client money funding the work, how fast each bill turns into cash (P1) matters more every quarter.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** One line for the whole company. It does not say which project's advance is still open, how much each running bill recovers, or when each job's advance is fully recovered.

## Why two problems, not three (internal note, not printed)
Garvis ruling JS-4 (a): the under-6-months bucket (formerly P2) is the newest part of P1 and is now a context panel inside P1's chart. A third problem was looked for and none is provable on the running business within the rules: inventory was 69 days of FY25 revenue and 71 days of FY26 revenue (flat), most of it is factory stock that the product does not model, and the calc has no row for it. Payables are never a saving, and the remaining candidates are kept out by ruling JS-3. Two problems, none invented. The labels P1 and P3 are kept so they match the rulings and the calc map.
