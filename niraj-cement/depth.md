# Niraj Cement Structurals: story angle (NC-1)

All figures standalone, FY = year to 31 March, from the FY2025-26 annual report unless stated (see data.md for pages).

## Respect first
- A Mumbai contractor with roots back to 1972, listed on BSE and NSE, run by a board of six directors (four independent) and a promoter family now in its third generation.
- Revenue from operations has grown three years running: Rs 470.22 Cr (FY24), Rs 506.72 Cr (FY25), Rs 540.44 Cr (FY26). Profit after tax: Rs 10.28 Cr, Rs 15.22 Cr, Rs 21.60 Cr. First quarter of FY27 (consolidated, unaudited): revenue Rs 104.54 Cr, net profit Rs 3.57 Cr.
- Rs 385.37 Cr of FY26 revenue (71%) came through 13 joint-venture arrangements, a partner-led delivery model that stretches a modest balance sheet.
- Debtor days held near 47 on the company's own turnover ratio (7.76 times), against 45 a year earlier.

## Then the question: where do the bills wait?
1. The ageing is the headline. Of Rs 70.76 Cr of trade receivables at 31 March 2026, Rs 39.15 Cr (55%) is over six months old and Rs 32.91 Cr (47%) over a year. A year earlier the over-six-month share was 24% (Rs 16.48 Cr of Rs 68.45 Cr). The average held; the tail grew.
2. The company itself calls these balances slow moving and tied to arbitration and reconciliation of contractual obligations; Rs 21.17 Cr of claims for work done, retention and advances is under arbitration. The question Crestline asks: for each such bill, which step stopped it (measurement, certification, client acknowledgement, a disputed deduction), on which site, since which date, and who can release it?
3. Money also sits outside receivables. Short-term loans and advances are Rs 279.32 Cr against revenue of Rs 540.44 Cr, including advances to suppliers Rs 28.47 Cr. The balance sheet cannot say which project each rupee is waiting on. Frame as capital deployed into projects and partners; never as a finding (see sensitive file).
4. Funding moved. Short-term borrowing rose from Rs 0.58 Cr to Rs 68.04 Cr in a year, Rs 67.67 Cr of it from one non-bank lender, and finance cost went from Rs 0.08 Cr to Rs 3.01 Cr. No rate is disclosed, so no interest saving can be priced.
5. Money runs the other way on the supplier side: Rs 57.49 Cr of advances against subcontractors sits as a liability, and payables of Rs 23.16 Cr turn about every 21 days (company ratio 17.20, direct-cost basis). A purchase-to-pay versus bill-to-cash timing question, not a verdict on anyone.

## What the accounts cannot tell them (the Crestline gap)
Site, bill, desk and date. Note 8 gives five age buckets for the whole company. It does not show which JV, which authority, which running bill, or how long each stage took. An ERP records that a bill exists, not how long it waited at each desk.

## Not available, so not in the story
Order book, ERP, a disclosed interest rate, rated limits beyond a Rs 5 Cr proposal, any management quote on working capital (no earnings call or investor deck found).

## Notes for step 2 (single basis only)
Debtor days 47.0 (company ratio: revenue over average receivables). One day of revenue = Rs 540.44 Cr / 365 = Rs 1.48 Cr. Over-six-month tail Rs 39.15 Cr. Never pair the 21-day payable figure (direct-cost basis) with the 47-day figure (revenue basis) as a cycle.
