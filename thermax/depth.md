## 3. Disconfirm: evidence that working capital is NOT a problem here
This section is as important as the problem list. Each item is from a primary document.
1. Operating NWC is negative: STANDALONE Rs -85.99 cr (-4.8 days), CONSOLIDATED Rs -214.81 cr (-7.3 days), FY26 (Computed from AR26). Customer advances (Rs 1,899.53 cr consolidated) more than cover receivables plus contract assets less payables.
2. Industrial Infra segment net assets are only Rs 160.12 cr, 13.4 days of segment revenue (AR26 p311, Computed). In FY24 they were Rs 387.27 cr (31.7 days, AR24 vintage). The project segment is largely customer-funded. (Internal only, never carried into print: this FY24 figure is not the calc's scenario (c), and the two are easy to confuse.)
3. CRISIL, 19-Jan-2026: "Prudent working capital management. The net working capital cycle has been around 40 days due to prudent collection policy and inventory management. Despite having exposure to large turnkey projects, receivables have remained at less than 120 days ... This has also led to low dependence on working capital debt." Liquidity "Superior"; "net nil debt"; bank limit utilisation 48% average.
4. CARE, 14-Jan-2026: average utilisation of fund-based working-capital limits "remained low at 18%"; free cash and liquid investments over Rs 2,500 cr (30-Sep-2025).
5. Cash and liquid investments Rs 2,467 cr at 31-Mar-2026 (AR26 p21), above total borrowings Rs 2,288.01 cr. Debt growth is renewable-asset finance (CRISIL), not working-capital stress.
6. The MD refuses to book orders without an advance (Q1T p5).
7. Consolidated past-due share fell from 62.0% to 57.7% of gross receivables (AR26 p256, Computed), and the ECL charge fell from Rs 80.53 cr to Rs 42.82 cr (AR26 p227).
8. MSME payments beyond the appointed day fell from Rs 548.06 cr to Rs 294.31 cr, standalone (AR26 p396).

Net read after disconfirming: WC is not a balance-sheet problem. The real, provable issues are (a) a large stock of OLD overdue receivables (Rs 377.27 cr consolidated over a year past due, against a Rs 295.35 cr allowance), (b) cash conversion that swings year to year (OCF/PAT 38%, 172%, 75% over FY24 to FY26), (c) retention and unbilled building in TBWES, and (d) execution slippage on legacy PSU projects. Crestline's honest wedge is time and visibility in the project business, not a working-capital rescue. Verdict stays LIGHT.

## 5. What the accounts cannot tell them (for TH-3)
The filings give group-level ageing and segment-level net assets only. They do not show, by project or site: how long each milestone bill waits for certification, how much retention is held per project and when it falls due, which bills are overdue at which customer desk, or how long advances take to arrive after a handshake (the MD's "advance didn't come in"). Those are the questions Crestline answers; none of them can be numbered from public data.
