# Nikhil Infraspecialities: where the money waits (deepened 2026-10-10)

Sources: CRISIL Ratings rationale, 6 Jan 2025, standalone (FY24, year to 31 Mar 2024, about 18 months before 10 Oct 2026), and CARE Ratings press release, 14 Dec 2023 (inputs only). Calc: calc/ni-savings-output.txt, unchanged. A newer CRISIL action was searched for on 10 Oct 2026 and not found. Where a source does not state a cause, the entry says what Crestline would measure instead.

## Respect first
Operating income rose from Rs 46.12 Cr (FY23) to Rs 50.26 Cr (FY24) and profit after tax from Rs 3.10 Cr to Rs 3.39 Cr (CRISIL Ratings, 6 Jan 2025). Three plants in Maharashtra, promoters with more than two decades in building products, and a new line of private building contracts.

## Problem 1. Customer credit: the long credit period behind the 476 days
- Stage: collection (invoice raised to payment received).
- Owner: the customer's. A wait that ends in the customer's act (acknowledge, certify, pay) is theirs to release.
- Figures: GCA 476 days at 31 Mar 2024, "driven by large receivables and inventory". The company "needs to extend a long credit period to its customers" (CRISIL, Weaknesses). No days of credit, no ageing, no retention published.
- Mechanism: stated by CRISIL as the credit period given to customers. Why it is long (terms agreed, bills disputed, documents missing) is not stated.
- Crestline would measure: each open invoice with value, due date, days past it, the customer's last act and the next one.
- Framing: a company that sells material and builds with it gives credit to win work; the question is which invoices have outrun their own terms.

## Problem 2. Civil works bills: the one stream with a bill path
- Stage: billing and certification, from measurement to the customer's certificate.
- Owner: split. Measuring, preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing are the company's. Acknowledging, certifying and paying are the customer's.
- Figures: civil works are 15 to 20% of FY24 revenue, material sales 75 to 80% (CRISIL, "Extensive experience of the promoters"). The civil works are private building contracts. CRISIL says work-in-process is held "to ensure timely execution of its civil projects".
- Mechanism: not stated. Whether the wait sits before submission (ours) or after (the customer's) is exactly what the accounts cannot show.
- Crestline would measure: per contract, the days from work done to bill submitted and from submitted to certified, with the desk on each.
- Framing: the smaller stream is the one with the most steps, so it is where a wait is easiest to lose.

## Problem 3. Work in process and finished stock: ours, and not priced
- Stage: stock (inventory held for execution).
- Owner: the company's own desks.
- Figures: "large work-in-process inventory and finished stock" (CRISIL, Weaknesses). No stock days, no value. CARE (14 Dec 2023) names cement, sand and gravel as the inputs and says their price changes put the value of stock at risk.
- Mechanism: stated as holding stock to execute projects on time. How much is idle is not stated.
- Crestline would measure: nothing in the first look. It prices cash waiting in bills, not stock, so this stays named and unpriced.
- Framing: stock held to deliver on time is a service choice before it is a delay.

## Problem 4. Vendors: nothing published, so nothing claimed
- Stage: vendor (order to material received to payment).
- Owner: ours. Vendor terms are agreed by the company.
- Figures: none. No creditor days, payables, MSME dues or vendor terms in either document.
- Mechanism: none stated. Crestline never counts paying vendors later as a saving.
- Crestline would measure: each purchase order against the vendor's own due date, for early payment (cash given up) and late payment (a risk).
- Framing: the wait on the vendor side is the company's own to set.

## Problem 5. Cash: the bank line carries the wait
- Stage: cash.
- Owner: ours to manage; the pace is set by Problems 1 to 3.
- Figures: bank limits were used at 80% on average for the 12 months through November 2024; current ratio 1.27 times at 31 Mar 2024; cash credit limit Rs 30.00 Cr of Rs 40 Cr facilities; CRISIL expects cash accrual of Rs 7.5 to 11.0 Cr a year against yearly term debt obligations of Rs 2.0 to 3.5 Cr (all CRISIL, 6 Jan 2025, Liquidity and Annexure).
- Mechanism: stated only as the working-capital-intensive operation. How much of the used limit funds receivables, work in process or stock is not stated, and no borrowing rate is published, so no interest is priced.
- Crestline would measure: the same open items, so each rupee released has a named bill behind it.
- Framing: high use of a limit is what a growing business with long credit looks like.

## Scenario (unchanged)
One day = 50.26 / 365 = Rs 13.77 L. Ten days less on the 476 = about Rs 1.38 Cr; thirty days less = about Rs 4.13 Cr, released once. No interest. Rows overlap and are never added. A sizing, not a claim about any balance.

## Not found (stays "Not published")
Debtor, creditor and inventory days; ageing; retention; contract assets; certification delays; MSME; vendor terms; client mix split; order book; operating cash flow; titles of any finance head.
