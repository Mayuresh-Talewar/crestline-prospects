## 0. The "152 days" is NOT in any primary document (read this first)
[T] p.2, [IP] slide 5 and [AR26] PDF p.10/14 say only: "working capital days (including retention debtors) reduced by 43 days"; [T] p.7/9/16 add "excluding retention, down by more than 50 days" and a target of "net working capital of 56 to 60 days" over 8 quarters. The figures 152 and 195 appear in no primary document, in the whalesbook/multibagg coverage, or in the Yahoo/GuruFocus piece (all checked, none cite them). 195 minus 152 = 43 matches the stated delta, so the pair is probably a secondary reconstruction. Treat as UNVERIFIED until Rajesh Das confirms.
Our reconstruction (computed, not company-stated): NWC = (trade receivables + contract assets + inventory) - (trade payables + contract liabilities), over revenue x 365. FY25 = 195.4 days (ties to 195). FY26 = 186.3 days; only if the new non-current contract assets (Rs 245.15 Cr, nil in FY25) are excluded does it give 152.1. So about 34 of the 43 days may be a classification move (current to non-current), not collection. This is a hypothesis from arithmetic; the company does not say it. Ask it as a question, do not assert it.

## 6. What public data cannot tell (the Crestline pitch)
Verified from the documents above: receivables and contract assets are 62.7% of total assets, yet public disclosure stops at one ageing table from transaction date, with these gaps:
1. Which project, site, client desk or officer holds each of the Rs 683 Cr "not due" and Rs 374 Cr under-6-months rupees (ageing is not split by client, project, or public/private) [AR26 p.158].
2. Retention vs unbilled vs certified-awaiting-payment: merged into trade receivables by policy [AR26 p.148]. No days-in-stage for measurement, certification, bill submission, client approval.
3. Why Rs 245 Cr of contract assets moved to non-current in FY26 and which jobs/clients they are.
4. Contract-asset ageing, and what the Rs 54.93 Cr "other exposures" consist of, project by project.
5. The 43 days: split of collection vs reclassification vs retention; no per-site or per-client days, no ownership of the next 40.
6. Vendor side: payable ageing against each vendor's own due date, early vs late payment (DPO about 179 is an average; MSME 45-day exposure Rs 45.66 Cr outstanding, [R] p.11).
7. Escalation recoveries (Rs 10 Cr provision for escalation timing, [T] p.3) and advance recovery by project.
That is Crestline's question: where the money is trapped, for how long, why, and who can release it, which only the ERP event trail answers.
