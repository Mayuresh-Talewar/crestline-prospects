# A B Infrabuild: story angle (Step 1, AB-1)

Respect first. A Mumbai contractor that started as a proprietorship in 1999 and was listed in 2019, now builds railway bridges, foot-over-bridges and station works for Western Railway, MRVC, the Dedicated Freight Corridor, NHAI and MCGM. Revenue rose from Rs 183.8 Cr (FY24) to Rs 208.2 Cr to Rs 256.2 Cr (FY26, audited), profit to Rs 19.3 Cr, order book Rs 977.18 Cr (3.81 times revenue, Acuite, 31 Mar 2026), and Acuite raised the rating to BBB / A3+ on 3 Jul 2026. It did this with 34 employees (AR26), so the work is run lean through subcontractors (46% of FY26 cost of construction).

Then the question of where bills wait. The growth is real, and so is where the money went. All on the audited accounts, closing balances, one basis:
- Receivables: 41.6 days of revenue (FY24), 88.1 (FY25), 148.5 (FY26); Rs 20.96 Cr to Rs 50.23 Cr to Rs 104.21 Cr. Acuite names the cause itself: more railway work, and "the average receivable period from these government railway projects is around 180 days".
- Built but not yet certified or billed (the work-in-progress contract asset): 100.3, 142.2, 157.5 days; Rs 50.51 Cr to Rs 81.11 Cr to Rs 110.54 Cr.
- Retention held by clients: Rs 13.69 Cr, Rs 16.84 Cr, Rs 18.05 Cr.
- Vendor side: payables 44.9, 63.3, 69.8 days of cost; so suppliers and subcontractors now fund more of the cycle, yet the cycle (contract-asset days + DSO - DPO) still went 97 to 167 to 236 days.
- Cash: operating cash flow +Rs 5.4 Cr, then -Rs 25.5 Cr, then -Rs 51.2 Cr; funded by cash credit (Rs 85.2 Cr), a Rs 39.9 Cr rights issue and other debt. Acuite: ~92% average use of fund-based limits, "improvement in working capital management a key monitorable".

The question for the CFO desk, which the accounts cannot answer: for Rs 110 Cr of work built but not billed and Rs 104 Cr billed but not paid, which bill, on which site, with which railway or municipal desk, since how many days? An annual report shows the size of the queue; it does not show which bills are in it, whose desk each one sits on, or whether a bill waiting for certification is waiting on the company's own measurement book or on the client's engineer. That is what Crestline would show on top of the ERP.

Charts that carry it: the cash cycle (three legs, 236 days), the size-ranked panel of what is trapped, annual cash from operations, the three-year working-capital line, and the bill timeline with only Certified (157.5) and Payment received (148.5) filled. Do not use agency creditor days; do not draw DIO; no interest figure (no disclosed rate).
