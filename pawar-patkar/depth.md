# Depth: story angle (PP-1)

Respect first. A Nashik-region contractor that began as a partnership in 1991 and became a private limited company in 2006, a Class 1A licensed civil contractor (A25, C22). Revenue went from Rs 123.45 Cr (FY22, C22) to Rs 552.35 Cr (FY25 Prov., A25), PAT Rs 32.95 Cr, gearing 0.16x, interest cover 13.2x, and a first Acuité A-/Stable in Sep 2025 after CRISIL BBB in 2022. Order book ~Rs 1,433.96 Cr at 30 Jun 2025, 2.59x FY25 revenue, all in Maharashtra, plus Rs 268.65 Cr where it is lowest bidder. Work for MIDC, MHDCL and the State Police Housing & Welfare Corporation.

Then the question: where do bills wait? Only what the rating says, no invented causes.
- Gross current asset days went from 55 (FY24) to 106 (FY25 Prov.). Acuité puts it down to debtor and inventory days rising on high year-end billing and purchases (A25).
- Debtor days are stated only as 30-40. Suppliers are on back-to-back arrangements tied to billing milestones (A25).
- Fund-based limits ran 75.3% used and bank-guarantee limits 86.92% used (12 months to 30 Jun 2025); Rs 40 Cr more working-capital limit was added from June 2025. Rs 90 Cr of the Rs 200 Cr rated is bank guarantee (A25).
- Rs 28.65 Cr invested in subsidiaries/JVs plus Rs 16.78 Cr lent to a JV: about 27% of net worth outside the contract cash cycle (A25). Respectful framing only, and see Garvis rulings before using.

Question for the reader: at four times the FY22 revenue, when a bill is measured and certified at one of many Maharashtra authorities, which desk and which site is it waiting on? The accounts show the days, not the desk. Ask it as a question, never as a criticism.

Limits: no EBITDA, creditor or inventory days, or cycle length, so the only own-data day figures are GCA 106 and debtor 30-40. Savings can only be scenarios.
