# Claude Design master prompt: Swastik Constructions working-capital review (one booklet, one edition)

Paste everything below this line into Claude Design. Upload the Crestline logo file before you send it. Swastik Constructions has no logo file: its name appears as text only.


## 1. What you are building

A 16 page A4 portrait booklet titled "Working-capital review", printed and posted to The Partners, Swastik Constructions (a partnership firm). You build ONE design and produce ONE edition, for the reader in the reader table below.
It answers one question for the reader: what if Crestline were already running at Swastik Constructions, how much money and how much time would it save?
Every word and every figure is supplied in the Content section at the end of this prompt. This prompt carries content only; the design is yours.

## 2. Reader edition

Build one edition, for the single row of the reader table in the Content section.
- The edition carries that reader's name and title on the cover, the page 2 letter with its address block, and the page 2 salutation.
- Name the edition "Edition: The Partners, Swastik Constructions". That label is for the file only and is never printed.
- Body copy is written in a neutral voice. Quotes, where any appear, are credited by name and title, as the Content gives them.

## 3. Rules you must follow

1. Use ONLY the text and figures in the Content section. Do not write new copy, captions, taglines, teasers, pull quotes or headings. If a field has no text, leave it empty.
2. Copy every figure exactly as written: same digits, same decimals, same currency sign, same unit. Do not round, re-total, convert or recompute anything.
3. Headlines exactly as given, split into two lines as shown. Never reword them.
4. Footnotes exactly as given, on the page they belong to.
5. Lines that start with ">" are printed copy. At the start of a printed line, the labels KICKER, HEADLINE, HEADLINE line 1, HEADLINE line 2, Footnote, Footer, Hero, Side stat, Running head, Chart title, Closing line, Vertical tagline and Question are instructions: print only what follows the colon (Running head gives the left text, then the right text, split at the bar). Text in quotation marks in the reader table is printed. Chart content is given as "Data" and "With Crestline" lines and the list items under "Data" and "Rows": show the labels and values they give; the words "Data" and "Rows" are not printed, and "With Crestline" is printed as the scenario label. Everything else (counts, notes, "Legs shown", "Letter body") is an instruction and is never printed.
6. No internal references in printed text: no file names, ticket ids, checker notes, status markers or notes addressed to the team. A source is printed only in the words the Content gives for it.
7. No printed negatives about the booklet itself: never print what is missing, unknown, unchecked or left out ("not available", "we could not find", "to be confirmed"). Print "Not published" or "Not public" only where a printed line gives it as copy.
8. No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Use a comma, a full stop, "to" or a middle dot (·) as the Content does.
9. Ownership words stay as written: the client certifies and pays; Swastik Constructions measures, prepares, reviews, submits and invoices. Do not swap them.
10. Use the Crestline logo file as supplied, never redrawn or imitated. Swastik Constructions has no logo: show its name as text only.

### Booklet accuracy rules (apply to every page)
A1. Claims about Crestline must be true today. Make no claim about Crestline beyond the supplied text. No certification, no hosting region, no encryption claim, no client names, no "live in X days", and no present-tense claim that Crestline connects to the company's ERP. The pilot is one to three projects, read-only, 14 days, as the Content words it.
A2. Every quote names the right speaker, exactly as the Content credits it.
A3. Ours versus the client's follows the product rule. In cash-in steps, acknowledged, certified and collected are the client's acts; measuring, preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing are the company's own. Keep every owner tag as given.
A4. Use only the headlines given; never soften or invert one.
A5. Every chart shows only the values the Content gives for it, each with its value label. Nothing is shown from a figure the Content does not give.
A6. Interest only at a rate the company discloses. None is disclosed, so no interest figure appears anywhere.
A7. Savings are scenarios. Anything marked "With Crestline", "Scenario" or "days less" is shown as a scenario and labelled "Scenario, not a forecast." Scenario rows overlap and are never added together; print no total the Content does not give. Pages the Content marks as history only carry no scenario.
A8. Peers and comparatives appear only where the Content prints them.
A9. No other company's data. Do not bring in any company, name, figure or text from anywhere other than the Content, including from earlier booklets or your memory. No demo numbers beside the company's figures.
A10. Names and titles on the letter page and cover exactly as the reader table gives them.
A11. Never invent figures. Where the Content gives a stage, step or item without a value, show it without a number. Never create a split of a total.
A12. No signed numbers in print. Write inflow / outflow, increase / decrease. No "+" or "-" in front of a figure.
A13. Every problem chart shows the outcome with Crestline where the Content gives one, labelled with the days and cash it releases, plus "Scenario, not a forecast." If the Content gives no scenario value for a chart, show none.
B1. Never compute a figure that is not given in the Content.
B2. Process map stage values tagged "Estimate" are estimates, never measured days and never priced. Only the stage tagged "Published" is a published figure.
B3. Retention is a balance. Never turn it into days or a release date.
