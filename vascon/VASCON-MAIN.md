## 3. Management's own words (exact, speaker named)
- Somnath Biswas, CFO, Q1 FY27 call, 14 August 2026, p6 to 7: "over the last couple of years, 4, 5 years, we used to get a very healthy working capital cycle of 45 days ... we are seeing that the net working capital cycle has been increased from 45 days to almost 65 to 70 days." And it "will be aligned to the back". He ties it to projects "at the fag end of the completion" where "final bill, certification takes its own sweet time" (p8).
- Dr. Santosh Sundararajan, Q4 FY26 call, 13 May 2026, p10: "we want to end this year with about INR4,000 crores of order backlog". It is a backlog target, not revenue.
- Dr. Santosh Sundararajan, Q1 FY27 call, p3: new EPC orders of INR1,500 to 2,000 crores targeted in FY27; "temporary cash flow constraints in 2 major government projects" (Supaul and Sindhudurg named at p10).
- Vascon publishes no formula for the "net working capital cycle". Treat 65 to 70 as his description.

## 4. The five problems (full detail in problems.md)
1. Unbilled work: Rs 300.64 Cr to Rs 563.51 Cr, 143.7 to 216.8 days of revenue. Largest balance, partly Vascon's own desks.
2. Retention: Rs 45.26 Cr to Rs 107.18 Cr; up 73.8% since FY23 on revenue up 23.6%. The client's certificate releases it.
3. Billed debtors are inside terms: 50.4 days against a 60-day credit period. The days beyond terms are before the bill and in retention, so chasing collection alone would not move them.
4. Cash from operations turned negative and net debt rose (above).
5. Payables: 23.5% over one year old, Rs 104.33 Cr on supplier finance. Visibility only; no saving is claimed.

## 5. What Crestline does, and the money and time it would give back
Crestline reads the dates an ERP already records, rebuilds each bill, splits every wait into Vascon's desk (measure, prepare, submit, invoice) and the client's (certify, pay, release), prices it in rupee-days and names an owner and a deadline. Ours versus theirs follows the product rule: measuring, preparing, submitting and invoicing are Vascon's; certifying, paying and releasing retention are the client's.
Scenarios (copied from the savings output; per day Rs 2.60 Cr = 948.53 / 365; interest at Vascon's cash-credit range of 9.65% to 10.95%, saved only if the cash repays debt):

Overlap note: these rows overlap and are never added. Retention sits inside receivables; unbilled is net of advances; a 30-day step on unbilled can be the same 30 days as the overall cycle. Paying vendors later is never a saving; the vendor leg is held. Time back is days out of each wait; management hours are described, never numbered. Margin: carry cost only; interest sits below EBITDA, so EBITDA margin does not move.

## 8. What we can and cannot promise
Can say: the arithmetic on published figures; the pilot is read-only; one to three third-party EPC projects, in rupees; an adapter built for their SAP first, then 14 days of read-only analysis once it is connected; the auditor's SAP audit-trail note, factually, as a control point Crestline's own read-only record sits beside.
