Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has downgraded its ratings on the bank facilities of Vascon Engineers Limited (VEL) to ‘Crisil BBB+/Stable/Crisil A2’ from ‘Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+’.

 

The downgrade reflects the substantially weak operating performance in the real estate segment owing to slower-than-expected sales velocity, delay in receipt of approvals resulting in delay in launch of a key project, and execution delays in key EPC (engineering, procurement, and construction) projects. Debt level increased from Rs 205.00 crore as on March 31, 2025, to Rs 338.76 crore as on June 30, 2026, owing to larger working capital requirement and higher-than-expected debt in the real estate segment.

 

During fiscal 2026, revenue declined 12% on-year to Rs 949 crore. This continued in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, when revenue declined 31% on-year to Rs 151.96 crore. This was account of execution delay in two large government EPC projects and lower revenue booking in the real estate segment.

 

Consequently, operating margin also moderated to 5.7% in fiscal 2026 (fiscal 2025: 9.8%) and 3.25% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (first quarter of fiscal 2026: 5.89%).

 

Total collections in the real estate segment remained modest at Rs 119 crore in fiscal 2026 and Rs 20 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 due to lower-than-expected sales velocity and collections from the Orchids project in Santacruz (Mumbai) and delay in launch of a project in Powai (Mumbai). That said, with the company’s other Santacruz project, Prakash, expected to be launched in October 2026 and with festive demand, sales velocity is expected to pick up, and the company is likely to garner total collection of Rs 300-350 crore for the full fiscal 2027.

 

Furthermore, in the EPC segment, construction momentum is expected to pick up after the monsoon and with resumption of work in two key government projects. This should support double-digit revenue growth in fiscal 2027. With higher revenue booking, operating margin is expected to bounce back on-year to 8-9%, but remain below historical levels.

 

Due to increase in consolidated debt in fiscal 2026, interest coverage ratio moderated to 2.4 times from 2.9 times in fiscal 2025 and further to 1.6 times in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. That said, liquidity is comfortable, aided by adequate buffer of Rs 36 crore in fund-based facilities and unencumbered cash and equivalent of Rs 31.86 crore as on June 30, 2026. Overall, incremental collections, coupled with healthy liquidity buffer, should sufficiently cover debt obligation of Rs 72.41 crore in fiscal 2027 (Rs 25 crore already repaid year-to-date).

 

The ratings reflect VEL's established position in the EPC segment, healthy order book providing revenue visibility, extensive experience of the promoters, and longstanding execution track record across EPC and real estate development; the ratings also benefit from the company's diversified order book, with a significant proportion of government contracts and its demonstrated ability to raise capital, as evidenced by Rs 80 crore preferential warrant issue, of which Rs 20 crore has already been infused as of July 2026.

 

These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement, slow-moving inventory in some real estate projects, and increased dependence on debt funding for real estate expansion.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the consolidated financial and business risk profiles of VEL, along with its subsidiaries, given the common management and business synergies among the entities.

 

Subsidiaries include Marvel Housing Pvt Ltd and Vascon Value Homes Pvt Ltd. Associates and joint ventures include Phoenix Ventures, Vascon Saga Construction LLP, Vascon Qatar WLL, Ajanta Enterprises, Vascon Developers LLP, DCS Conventions & Hospitality Pvt Ltd, and Mumbai Estates Pvt Ltd.

 

Interest-bearing mobilisation advances have been treated as debt.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Experienced management and established execution track record

The company benefits from the extensive experience of its promoters and senior management in both the EPC and real estate segments. The recent strengthening of the real estate leadership team through the appointment of Mr Raveesh Rao as CEO is expected to support execution, monetisation, and growth of the real estate portfolio.

 

Established position in the EPC (buildings) segment, supported by healthy order book and counterparty profile

The company benefits from the management’s established track record in executing EPC construction contracts for the infrastructure, real estate and manufacturing segments. The company also has strong technical capabilities and domain expertise in the construction and execution of complex projects.

 

VEL had external orders worth Rs 2,531 crore as on June 30, 2026, and will continue to focus on government projects. Government entities accounting for nearly 77% of orders will lend stability to the operating performance and ensure uninterrupted cash flow over the medium term.

 

The company has entered into a memorandum of understanding with Adani Infra Ltd to collaborate as an execution partner under an early engagement model for the next five years. This should further boost the order book and revenue growth in the long term.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Large working capital requirement and slow-moving real estate inventory

Operations remain working capital intensive due to inventory held as collateral and ongoing project inventory in the real estate segment, as well as significant unbilled revenue and retention money in the EPC business. Real estate collections remained below expectations at Rs 119 crore in fiscal 2026 (Rs 58 crore in fiscal 2025) and Rs 20 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, compared with Rs 127 crore in fiscal 2024; due to slower inventory monetisation and delays in project launches and approvals, resulting in higher funding requirements and increased reliance on external borrowings.

 

Trade receivables stood at Rs 238.30 crore as on March 31, 2026, including retention money of Rs.107.17 crore (accrued but not due). Of the total receivables Rs 90.73 crore remained outstanding for more than two years. Out of these long-outstanding receivables approximately Rs 15 crore is under arbitration. Furthermore, the company has successfully recovered Rs 6 crore from a disputed claim, with the counterparty having agreed to repay the amount in monthly installments of Rs 0.5 crore until full recovery.

 

Exposure to risks related to saleability of real estate projects

Saleability and implementation risks will persist for the real estate segment, as reflected by sharp fluctuations in income, sales and collections over the past few fiscals. Certain ongoing and newly launched projects have witnessed limited progress on collection amid muted demand in fiscal 2026. Additionally, launches in fiscal 2026 were fewer than expected. The project, Prakash, was scheduled to be launched in fiscal 2026 and is now likely to be launched in October 2026.

 

Timely launch of real estate projects will be monitorable. Ability to liquidate and reduce real estate inventory, delay in completion or launch of projects, and additional debt raised to support any cash flow mismatch will remain monitorable.

Liquidity Adequate

With healthy operating margin and comfortable leverage, cash flow should suffice to cover the debt obligation and regular capital expenditure. Expected cash accrual of Rs 50-60 crore per annum, along with cash generated in the real estate business, will cover yearly debt obligation of Rs 70-80 crore over the medium term. Unencumbered cash and equivalent stood at Rs 31.86 crore as on June 30, 2026. Utilisation of the fund-based bank limit of Rs 119 crore averaged 59% during the 9 months through June 2026

Outlook Stable

The credit risk profile of VEL will continue to benefit from its healthy order book, improving operating performance, and healthy capital structure.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • Substantial scale-up in the EPC and real estate segments, along with a steady overall operating margin of 9.5-10%
  • Improvement in the working capital cycle and liquidity

 

Downward factors

  • Weaker operating performance, marked by lower-than-expected net collections in the real estate business (below Rs 58 crore)
  • Substantial delay in execution of orders or increase in real estate inventory, which may translate into increase in debt
  • Stretch in liquidity owing to lower-than-expected collections in the real estate segment and substantially low collections against longstanding receivables

About the Company

Incorporated in January 1986 in Pune (Maharashtra) and promoted by Vasudevan Ramamoorthy, Lalitha Vasudevan, Siddharth Vasudevan Moorthy, and Sowmya Aditya Iyer, VEL is engaged in the EPC and real estate development businesses. The company commenced operations with the construction of Cipla’s factory in Patalganga (Maharashtra) in November 1986. Until 1998, it undertook third-party contracts in the real estate segment.

 

VEL now constructs residential and office complexes, along with information technology parks, industrial units, shopping malls, multiplexes, educational institutions and hotels. Under the EPC segment, VEL has executed construction contracts for government departments and authorities.
