# B. G. Shirke: three working capital problems (SH-3)

Entity: B. G. Shirke Construction Technology Private Limited, standalone. Source for every figure: India Ratings (Ind-Ra) rating releases listed in data.md. Days are Computed as the agency's share of revenue x 365 and are labelled that way. Same vintage on every chart: R1, 20 Feb 2026, unless a line says otherwise.

## Respect first

The company is in a strong position and the copy says so before anything else. Ind-Ra upgraded it to **IND AA-/Stable** on 20 Feb 2026 (R1). It holds **Rs 1,548 Cr** of unencumbered cash including mutual funds at the end of 1HFY26 and has been net cash positive over FY24 to 1HFY26 (R1). It used on average only **27%** of its fund based limits in the 12 months to December 2025 (R1). EBITDA margin was **22.3%** in FY25 (R1). The order book is **Rs 15,700 Cr**, 2.65 times FY25 revenue (R1, Dec 2025).

So the pitch is days and cash, never interest and never distress. Money is not short. Time is what grew.

## Who owns which step

Measure, prepare, review, submit and invoice are the company's own acts. Acknowledge, certify and pay are the client body's acts. A wait that ends in a certificate or a receipt belongs to MHADA, CIDCO or the other body, never to the company's desks.

## Problem 1. The cycle grew from 113.2 to 160.6 days on flat revenue

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross WC, share of revenue (R1) | 31% | 36% | 44% |
| Gross WC days (Computed) | 113.2 | 131.4 | 160.6 |
| Revenue, Rs Cr | 5,790 (R1) | 5,890 (R1) | 5,916 (R1) |

- Source: Ind-Ra R1, 20 Feb 2026, financial summary and liquidity text; standalone. FY23 revenue is R1's own text (INR 57.9 bn); R3, 8 Nov 2023, prints the same.
- Basis: gross working capital as a share of revenue, as Ind-Ra defines it; days Computed (sh-calc rows `gross_wc_days_FY23` to `FY25`).
- Trend: one vintage (R1) for all three shares. Revenue grew 0.44% in FY25 (Computed); gross WC days grew from 113.2 to 160.6.
- R1's own reason: gross WC rose "primarily due to higher receivable days". Ind-Ra "expects the company's working capital requirements to remain broadly in line with FY25 levels over the medium term" (R1).
- What the report cannot tell: which project carries the extra days, which client body, which bill, and whose desk the bill sits on. It also cannot split the 160.6 days between work not yet billed, bills not yet certified and certified bills not yet paid.

## Problem 2. Receivables spilling over the year end, on a book of state agencies

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross WC, Rs Cr (Computed, share x revenue) | 1,795 | 2,120 | 2,603 |

Order book client mix, Dec 2025 (R1): MHADA 47%, CIDCO 15%, MMRDA 8%, Konkan Housing and Area Development Board 4%, PMRDA 3%, PCMC 2%; Maharashtra state agencies about 81% in all. Top 5 contracts 43%.

- Source: Ind-Ra R1, 20 Feb 2026. Rs Cr rows are sh-calc `gross_wc_RsCr_FY23` to `FY25`.
- Basis: standalone; gross WC in rupees is the R1 share multiplied by that year's revenue, Computed.
- Trend: one vintage (R1). Rs 1,795 Cr to Rs 2,603 Cr in two years.
- R1's own words: higher receivable days "largely related to the spilling over of receivables into the next financial year".
- Ownership: the certificate and the payment are the client body's acts. Submitting a complete bill early enough to be certified before the year end is the company's.
- What the report cannot tell: which client body's bills spilled over, on which project, which RA bill, how many days each sat before certification and after it, and whether the wait was at submission (ours) or at certification and payment (theirs).

## Problem 3. Net working capital at 17% of revenue, aided by advances and lengthened by early vendor payment

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net WC, share of revenue (R1) | 14% | 17% | 17% |
| Net WC days (Computed) | 51.1 | 62.1 | 62.1 |

- Source: Ind-Ra R1, 20 Feb 2026; standalone.
- Basis: net working capital as Ind-Ra defines it; days Computed (sh-calc `net_wc_days_FY23` to `FY25`, Feb 2026 vintage). The Nov 2023 release printed FY23 at 13%; it is not mixed in.
- Trend: one vintage (R1).
- R1's own words: net working capital as a percentage of revenue "remained stable at 17% in FY25 (FY24: 17%; FY23: 14%), aided by higher mobilisation advances", and "BGSCTPL's net working capital cycle has elongated from a low single-digit historically, as the company takes advantage of price discounts by timely/advance payments to vendors". Paying vendors early lengthens the net cycle; the advances help keep it level. Ind-Ra (R2, 6 Dec 2024) notes only MHADA and CIDCO advances carry no interest.
- Ownership: when to pay a vendor is the company's own decision. Paying early for a discount can be the right call; whether it is depends on the discount against the days of cash it uses.
- What the report cannot tell: which vendors are paid ahead of their due date, by how many days, what discount each payment bought, and which projects' mobilisation advances aid the net figure.
