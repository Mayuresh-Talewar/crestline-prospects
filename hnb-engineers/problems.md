# HNB Engineers: problems (respect first), deepened 2026-10-10

Respect: a Pune firm incorporated in 1973; over four decades in sewage and water treatment; 1,500 MLD commissioned; O&M on around 1,000 MLD; operating income Rs 221.53 Cr in FY25 against Rs 206.45 Cr in FY24; orders of around Rs 570 Cr (Jun 2023), Rs 1,147 Cr (30 Sep 2024) and Rs 1,279 Cr (1 Jun 2025) (CRISIL Ratings, 29 Aug 2023, 26 Nov 2024, 1 Oct 2025). Clients named on the company's own website are municipal corporations, water and sewerage boards and development authorities.

Basis for every GCA figure: CRISIL's gross current assets in days, one block, same wording in all three notes. No split, no interest, no saving beyond solutions.md.

## 1. Bills raised late in the year (stage: billing; owner: ours)
Figures: GCA 287 days (31 Mar 2023), 345 (31 Mar 2024), 412 (31 Mar 2025). CRISIL gives the same reason in all three: higher revenue booking in the fourth quarter leaves high debtors at year end. 29 Aug 2023: "a large portion of these receivables were realised in the subsequent quarter".
Mechanism (CRISIL's): a bill raised in March has barely started its clock at the 31 March count. Not stated: how much is billed in the fourth quarter, or why work reaches billing late in the year.
Crestline would measure: bills raised by month, days from measurement to submission, and the age of each bill at 31 March.
Framing: the year-end count is a timing of the count as much as of the work; the question is how much of 412 is simply young.

## 2. Credit given to government clients (stage: collection; owner: the client's)
Figures: 90 to 120 days of credit to government departments and urban local bodies (all three notes); "payments from counterparties can sometimes get stretched" (all three).
Mechanism: CRISIL ties the credit to dealing mostly with government bodies. Not stated: which clients stretch, by how much, or whether the delay is certification or release of funds.
Crestline would measure: days from submission to payment, client by client, against each contract's own term.
Framing: 90 to 120 days is a published norm for these clients; the open question is which bills run past it.

## 3. When customers pay late the bank line is used more (stage: cash; owner: the client's payment, HNB Engineers' funding)
Figures: fund-based limit used 83% on average, 12 months to Sep 2024 (CRISIL 26 Nov 2024), and CRISIL says use "increased due to delay in receipt of payment from customers"; 69% on average, 12 months to Aug 2025 (CRISIL 1 Oct 2025). FY24 revenue "lower than expected" because general elections delayed "funds from customers" (26 Nov 2024).
Mechanism (CRISIL's): late receipts push the cash need onto the bank line. 69% shows the line eased in the year the days rose again; the notes do not say why.
Crestline would measure: which receipts were late, by how many days, in which weeks, against the weeks the line was drawn.
Framing: the cash side is the client's late payment first; never presented as a funding fault.

## 4. The need is carried by suppliers and customer advances (stage: vendor; owner: ours for vendor terms, the client's for advances)
Figures: none in rupees or days. CRISIL (all three notes): the need is supported by extended credit from suppliers and creditors, mobilisation advances from customers and short-term bank funding.
Mechanism: supplier credit and advances fund part of the cycle. Not published: payables, creditor days, MSME dues, vendor advances, vendor terms, supplier concentration, subcontract share.
Crestline would measure: vendor payments against their own due dates and advance recovery against billing. Paying later than agreed is never counted as a saving.
Framing: a support the company relies on, to be checked for terms honoured, not squeezed.

## 5. More orders, more bills at once (stage: project; owner: both)
Figures: orders around Rs 570 Cr, Rs 1,147 Cr, Rs 1,279 Cr at Jun 2023, 30 Sep 2024 and 1 Jun 2025, to be executed in 18 to 36 months. CRISIL: as most orders are from government bodies, "timely commencement of the projects, availability of funding and timely realisation remain monitorables".
Mechanism: CRISIL names these as watch items. It does not say the days rose because of the order growth.
Crestline would measure: days by project, so the growth is read site by site rather than as one number.
Framing: more orders is the good news; the days belong to the sites that carry them.

## Not found, so not claimed
Certification time, retention held, unbilled work, client concentration, ageing, creditor days, MSME dues, operating cash flow, working-capital cycle as the company states it. Each is named on page 9 as not published, and is something the pilot would read.
Not claimed: ERP, interest rate, debtor days, any saving beyond the scenario in solutions.md. Kept out: README list.
