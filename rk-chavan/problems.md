# R K Chavan Infrastructure: three problems (RK-2)

Source for every figure: Crisil Ratings rationale, 30 June 2026 (cited "CRISIL"), figures "Crisil adjusted". Respect first: CRISIL reaffirmed BBB+/Stable and A2 on 30 June 2026 and raised the rated bank limits from Rs 250 Cr to Rs 347 Cr. Operating income grew from Rs 457.65 Cr (FY24) to Rs 524.35 Cr (FY25, 14.57% by script); CRISIL puts FY26 at around Rs 700 Cr (its estimate, 34% growth) and the EPC order book at Rs 2,595 Cr as of March 2026, over the next 24 to 36 months. This is a growing business. The question is only where each bill waits as the book grows.

## Problem 1. The days tied up in current assets rose faster than the story would suggest
- CRISIL, "Working capital-intensive operations": gross current assets "estimated at around 250 days as on March 31, 2026 which increased from 187 days as on March 31, 2025". Difference 63 days (script).
- Trend: 187 days (31 Mar 2025), about 250 days (31 Mar 2026, estimate). Same basis (gross current assets days), one source. No earlier years are published, so no longer trend is drawn.
- Day basis is not stated by CRISIL. No split into stock, unbilled work, debtors or payables is published.
- What the accounts cannot tell: which bills, which sites, which desk, since when.
- Worth: 10 days = Rs 14.37 Cr, 30 days = Rs 43.10 Cr, released once (calc, FY25 income per day Rs 1.44 Cr).

## Problem 2. Debtors wait 100 to 140 days, and the stated reasons are timing and retention
- CRISIL: "higher debtors in the range of 100-140 days due to high revenue booking in Q4 and retenetion money" (source spelling). Inventory "in the range of 70-90 days which majorky represents work in progress" (source spelling).
- A range, not a point: no day is printed as a headline. This is a timing and retention story as CRISIL tells it, not a claim that a client pays late.
- Ownership: the Q4 bills wait on the client's acts (acknowledge, certify, pay); retention waits on the client's release. The company's own acts (measure, prepare, review, submit, invoice) come first and set the date every later wait starts from.
- What the accounts cannot tell: how much of the range is retention, which client, which site.

## Problem 3. A growing book makes every day dearer
- EPC order book Rs 2,595 Cr, 3.71 times CRISIL's FY26 revenue estimate (script, from data.md). Rated bank limits Rs 250 Cr to Rs 347 Cr.
- A day of income is Rs 1.44 Cr at FY25 (524.35 / 365). It is conservative: FY26 income is higher on CRISIL's estimate, so a day is worth more, not less. The estimate is never a calc input.
- CRISIL itself writes that improvement in the working capital cycle "will be closely monitored" and lists an "efficient working capital cycle" among upward factors. These are the agency's words, credited to it.
