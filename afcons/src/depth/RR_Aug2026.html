Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities and non convertible debentures of Afcons Infrastructure Limited (AIL) to ‘Negative’ from ‘Stable’ while reaffirming the rating at ‘Crisil AA-’. Also, Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the commercial paper programme of the company.

 

The outlook revision reflects moderation in AIL’s operating performance and increase in working capital intensity due to build-up of contract assets and delays in receivable realisation. Weaker-than-expected order inflows in fiscal 2026 led to lower mobilisation advances inflow, while elongation in debtor days stretched working capital cycle, resulting in higher-than-expected reliance on working capital borrowings and weakening of key credit metrics during the past fiscal. Also including execution challenges owing to limited work front availability, as well as slower conversion and cancellation of certain projects at the lowest bidder (L1) stage.

 

However, the company’s operating performance may improve going forward, on account of healthy order inflow in the current fiscal as well as expected recovery of stretched receivables. Further, healthy liquidity in the form of unencumbered cash & cash equivalents as well as unutilized fund-based limits provides cushion against the stretched working capital cycle. That said, the pace of improvement in execution, working capital cycle, and debt levels will remain key rating sensitivity factors. Any delay in reducing debtor days (incl. contract assets) and improving operating cash flow generation could result in a rating downgrade.

 

Reduced pace of order inflow and execution related challenges resulted in operating income moderating to Rs 11,976 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 12,553 crore in fiscal 2025, although operating profitability remained adequate with margin above 10-11%. However, the company has secured healthy order inflows of Rs 15,695 crore in fiscal 2027 year-to-date, increasing its order book (including L1) to Rs  45,767 crore from about Rs 32,000 crore as on March 31, 2026. The strengthened order book provides healthy revenue visibility and is expected to support growth in scale from the second half of fiscal 2027 as execution ramps up.

 

Working capital intensity has increased primarily due to delays in certification and realisation of contract assets. Elongation in project execution cycles, milestone delays and slower ramp-up in certain large projects resulted in lower billings and higher unbilled revenue accumulation. Consequently, the net working capital (NWC) increased to 139 days in fiscal 2026 from 117 days in fiscal 2025 (less than 90 days in fiscal 2024). As a result, total debt increased to Rs 3,529 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 2,230 crore a year earlier, while adjusted interest coverage moderated to  2.4 times from  2.7 times.

 

The rating reaffirmation factors in the expected improvement in operating performance, working capital cycle, and cash flow generation over the medium term. AIL expects order inflows of around Rs 30,000 crore in fiscal 2027, compared with about Rs 4,500 crore in fiscal 2026, which should support higher mobilisation advances. This is also evident from the recent order inflows of Rs 15,737 crore in fiscal 2027 year-to-date. The company is also targeting significant recovery of legacy contract assets in current fiscal, which is expected to reduce debtor days and improve working capital cycle. Improvement in receivable realisation and mobilisation advances inflows should support a material recovery in cash flow from operations, which remained negative over the past two fiscals. Progress on these fronts will remain a key rating sensitivity factor.

 

The ratings continue to reflect AIL’s strong business profile, established position in the engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) segment, and healthy financial flexibility supported by strong liquidity. The company maintained cash and cash equivalents around  Rs 900 crore as on March 31, 2026,. This is supplemented by unutilised fund-based working capital limits of around Rs 1,200 crore as on June 30, 2026, and untapped interest-free mobilisation advances of about Rs 600 crore as on March 31, 2026. Expected advances from recently secured projects should provide additional liquidity support.

 

The company has also maintained a policy of holding minimum unencumbered cash balances of around Rs 500-600 crore, which remains critical given the inherently working-capital-intensive nature of the business.

 

It is to be noted that AIL has limited operating and financial linkages with the Shapoorji Pallonji Group and has historically been managed independently. Group entities account for a small portion of AIL's revenue and orders, while the company has not extended material financial support to promoter entities. Debt at the key promoter holding companies remains non-recourse to AIL's cash flows. Continued financial independence from the promoter group and absence of material support to group entities remain important rating considerations.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

  • Strong business profile with established market position in the EPC segment:

AIL continues to have a strong business risk profile, supported by its established position in the EPC segment and proven ability to execute complex infrastructure projects in India and overseas. The company has executed marquee projects such as the Atal Tunnel, Chenab  Rail Bridge, Ghana Rail and Zambia-Lusaka City Decongestion Project and is currently undertaking a section of the Mumbai-Ahmedabad High Speed Rail Corridor. AIL's order book remains robust at Rs  45,767 crore as on June 30, 2026, supported by recent order wins of Rs. 15,695 crore, providing medium-term revenue visibility. The order book is well diversified across segments such as metro, urban infrastructure, hydro and underground and marine, as well as geographically, with around 75% of the order book in India across 14 states and the remainder spread across 10 countries. 

 

  • Strong liquidity position provides financial flexibility:

AIL maintains a strong liquidity, supported by policy to maintain minimum unencumbered cash of Rs 500 – 600 crore. Cash and cash equivalents stood at around Rs 900 crore as on March 31, 2026. The company also has fund-based working capital limits, of which around 25-30% is free providing additional cushion. Additionally, it has undrawn advances of Rs 600 crore, which can be tapped. Further, the recent order wins are expected to lead to inflow of mobilisation advances of around Rs 1,500-2,000 crore, a significant portion of which is expected to be interest-free. Timely receipt of these advances should support liquidity and reduce dependence on external borrowings over the near to medium term.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

  • Moderation in operating performance and increase in working capital intensity:

Operating performance moderated in fiscal 2026 due to execution-related challenges because of fewer work front availability arising from approval delays, payment-related slowdown, land acquisition issues, and scope/design modifications in certain projects. Further, lower order inflows during fiscal 2026 and delays in conversion/ cancellation of projects from L1 stage also constrained the revenue ramp-up. Consequently, operating income declined to Rs 11,976 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 12,553 crore in fiscal 2025.

 

As a result, AIL’s profitability moderated in fiscal 2026 owing to under-absorption of fixed costs as well as one-off provisioning of around Rs 130 crore against contract assets where the arbitration award was unfavorable. This was reflected in the decline in return on capital employed (ROCE) to ~12.7% in fiscal 2026 from 17.9% in fiscal 2025.

 

Working capital intensity increased, with debtors (incl. contract assets) rising to 295 days in fiscal 2026 from 239 days in fiscal 2025 and gross current assets (excluding cash and cash equivalents) increasing to 369 days from 308 days, primarily due to elongation in project execution cycles, which have delayed project milestone achievements. In addition, the company continues to have sizeable arbitration-related receivables and contract assets pending certification and settlement across certain large projects.

 

Crisil Ratings expects revenue ramp up to happen from the second half of fiscal 2027, supported by easing of approval challenges and fresh order inflows. Crisil Ratings also expects that realisation of receivables and contract assets, along with inflow of mobilisation advances from recent order wins, will reduce working capital intensity with debtor days falling below ~270-280 days by the end of fiscal 2027, which will increase operating profitability and cash flow.

 

Any deviation from the expected improvement in scale of operations or any further delays in collections, certifications or adverse arbitration outcomes, which could increase pressure on working capital will remain monitorable from the credit standpoint.

.

  • Moderate debt coverage and leverage indicators: AIL's financial risk profile moderated in fiscal 2026, with total debt increasing to Rs 3,529 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 2,230 crore a year earlier, primarily due to higher working capital borrowings amid elongated receivable cycles and lower mobilisation advance inflows. Increased borrowings, along with weaker operating performance, led to decline in adjusted interest coverage ratio to 2.2 times from 2.7 times. Total outside liabilities to tangible net worth (TOL to TNW) also inched up to 2.5 times from 2.3 times.

 

Crisil Ratings expects ramp-up in scale of operations and higher inflow of mobilization advances to improve AIL’s financial risk profile. Consequently, total debt is expected to remain below Rs 3,200 crore, while adjusted interest coverage ratio is expected to improve to more than 2.6-2.8 times and TOL to TNW to remain below 2.3-2.5 times in the medium term.

Liquidity Strong

Cash and cash equivalent of around Rs 900 crore as on March 31, 2026, and annual cash accrual of over Rs 500 crore over the medium term against annual debt obligation of around Rs 200-250 crore should keep the liquidity position healthy. The company has been maintaining a minimum unencumbered cash balance of over Rs 500 - 600 crore, supporting liquidity. Besides, it has access to fund-based bank limits. Financial flexibility is also aided by available lines of interest-free mobilisation advances of around Rs 600 crore not yet tapped by the company. Additionally, liquidity will be aided by around Rs 1,500-2,000 crore of advances to be received against the recent awarding.

Outlook Negative

Crisil Ratings believes Afcons will continue to maintain its strong market position in the complex EPC segment, supported by its established market position and large, diversified order book. However, delays in improving project executions and reducing working capital intensity can affect the credit risk profile of the company.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • Easing of execution related challenges, leading to healthy revenue growth while sustaining double-digit operating margins
  • Timely realization of contract assets, leading to marked improvement in the working capital intensity with debtor days falling below 270-280 days
  • Improvement in adjusted interest coverage to above 3 to 3.5 times on a sustained basis

 

Downward factors

  • Sustained execution bottlenecks resulting in weaker-than-expected revenue growth, margin pressure, and continued weak cash flow generation
  • Prolonged stretch in working capital cycle, with debtor days (incl. contract assets) remaining above 300 days and net working capital materially above 100 days, leading to elevated external debt and significantly negative cash flow from operations
  • Any extension of financial support to Shapoorji Pallonji Group entities, or any lender-imposed restrictions that weaken company’s liquidity, financial flexibility, or the company’s ability to access incremental funding and working capital facilities.

About the Company

Afcons was incorporated in 1959 as Rodio Hazarat & Co and was acquired by Shapoorji Pallonji Group in 2000. It is an Indian construction and engineering company involved in infrastructure projects across elevated and underground metro, bridges, roads, tunnels, ports and marine works, and oil and gas.

 

Shapoorji Pallonji Group holds 50.17% in Afcons, with Goswami Infratech Pvt Ltd holding 25.03%, SPCPL holding 15.41% and the remaining held by other promoter entities. Afcons concluded its initial public offer (IPO) raising Rs 1,250 crore from primary issuance of shares. The company got listed on Indian stock exchanges on November 4, 2024.

 

For fiscal 2026, the company reported revenue of Rs 11,976 crore and profit after tax of Rs 251 crore against Rs 12,553 crore and Rs 487 crore, respectively, for fiscal 2025.
