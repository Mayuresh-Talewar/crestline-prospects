# Shri Swami Samarth Engineers: problems (story SS-2)

Source for every figure: Acuite press release and rating rationale, 6 Jul 2026 (standalone, years to 31 March). Re-fetched from connect.acuite.in on 2026-10-09 and traced in qa-report.md.

## Respect first
Operating income rose from 432.42 Cr in FY25 to 630.37 Cr in FY26, about 46 percent (Acuite's own figure). Profit after tax rose from 30.17 Cr to 56.28 Cr. Operating margin was 18 percent in FY26 (19.10 percent in FY25), which Acuite attributes to self-execution of orders and the company's own construction equipment. The order book still to be executed was about 1,181.27 Cr at 31 May 2026. Receivable days improved from 81 to 65.

## Problem 1: as the book grows, where does each bill wait?
- Fact: receivable days are 65 in FY26 (Acuite, 6 Jul 2026). A day of FY26 operating income is 630.37 / 365 = 1.727 Cr (calc).
- What the record cannot say: how many of the 65 days sit before a bill leaves the company and how many after it reaches the client; on which site and authority; on whose desk.
- Scenario (calc): 10 days sooner is 17.27 Cr released once; 30 days sooner is 51.81 Cr. Rows overlap, never added. No interest (no borrowing rate published).
- Ownership: the wait to be paid ends in the client's act (acknowledge, certify, pay). The company's side is measure, prepare, review, submit, invoice.

## Problem 2: a gain that is on the record but not yet explained
- Fact: receivable days fell from 81 (FY25) to 65 (FY26), and Acuite expects working capital operations to stay at similar levels.
- What the record cannot say: which bills, sites or desks produced the improvement, so whether it holds as the order book is executed.
- Context only (never added, never a second priced row): gross current assets stood at 132 days in FY26 against 169 in FY25 (Acuite).
