# Shubham EPC: three problems

The story (Garvis ruling SE 3): fast growth, with gross current assets held at 155 to 200 days and bank limit use of 80 percent over the twelve months ended April 2025, which CRISIL calls moderate. The question: as the book doubles, does the view of where each bill waits keep pace?

Entity on every figure: Shubham EPC Private Limited, standalone. Document IDs as in data.md. Calc rows: `src/se-calc.out.txt` (C1 to C8) and `calc/shubham-savings.out.txt`.

Ownership used throughout: measuring, preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing a bill are the company's own acts. Acknowledging, certifying and paying are the client's.

## Problem 1. The book has grown faster than any single view of it

**What they did well.** Operating income rose from Rs 154.6 cr (FY20) to Rs 510.05 cr (FY25), 3.30 times in five years, a 27.0% compound rate. PAT margin went from 3.3% to 7.04% and gearing from 1.06 to 0.60 over the same years. The order book stood above Rs 1,400 cr, to be executed in 24 to 30 months. The company's own newsletter puts FY26 turnover at Rs 680 cr, provisional and "subject to closure and audit". Turnover is the company's own measure, not CRISIL's operating income, so it is never drawn on the same bars.

**What the money did.** At FY25 operating income, one day is worth Rs 1.3974 cr. Every year of growth makes each waiting day dearer. Vinay Badera (Managing Director & CEO, as that issue prints the title): "Growth also brings responsibility. As we scale our operations, we must sharpen our focus on cost consciousness."

## Problem 2. Gross current assets have held at 155 to 200 days, and nobody outside can see the split

**What they did well.** CRISIL rates liquidity "Adequate" and keeps the company at Crisil BBB+/Stable and Crisil A2 (as of 02 Jul 2025).

**What the money did.** CRISIL says gross current assets stayed high, at 155 to 200 days over the three fiscals through March 2025, driven by "moderately high receivables, inventory and other current assets pertaining to contractual requirement towards work orders." The earlier rationale gave 145 to 201 days (FY22 to FY23). On FY25 income the current band is Rs 216.6 cr to Rs 279.5 cr held in the work (computed, not a reported balance).

## Problem 3. As the book grows, the limits grow with it

**What they did well.** CRISIL expects cash accrual of about Rs 40 cr against term debt obligations of Rs 5 to 8 cr a year, and its lenders raised their limits as it grew.

**What the money did.** Rated bank facilities went from Rs 165 cr (Apr 2024) to Rs 225 cr (Jul 2025), up 36.4% in 15 months; fund based rated amounts (proposed limits included) from Rs 50.6 cr to Rs 78.1 cr. CRISIL put bank limit use at "around 75%" over the 12 months through December 2023, and at "80 percent", which it calls moderate, over the twelve months ended April 2025. Each reading closes before the enhancement that followed it, so neither is a share of the larger limit. CRISIL says the working capital is funded by supplier credit and short term bank debt, and: "Execution of large orders may lead to high incremental working capital requirement."
