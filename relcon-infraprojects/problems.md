Strength: operating income ₹587.54 Cr in FY24 (₹527.03 Cr in FY23); profit after tax ₹36.18 Cr; adjusted debt to adjusted net worth 0.16 times; interest cover 21.63 times; net worth ₹346 Cr; fund-based limits 21% used on average in the 12 months through October 2024; unexecuted order book ₹2,456 Cr at 30 Sep 2024 (24 to 30 months, over 90% in Maharashtra), 4.18 times FY24 income (Crestline's arithmetic: 2,456 ÷ 587.54).

## Problem 1. Gross current assets of 199 days, and no view of which bill waits
CRISIL: gross current assets 199 days at 31 Mar 2024 (200 to 266 days in the past three fiscals), arising from "moderate debtor levels and retention money as well as deposits given to customers". The accounts cannot say which bill, at which client desk, since when. Gross current assets include plant stock, so the 199 days is not a bill wait and is not priced.

## Problem 2. A book four times the year's income raises the cost of every waiting day
As the 24 to 30 month book is executed, each bill that waits holds more money. A day of FY24 operating income is ₹1.61 Cr; ten days is ₹16.10 Cr; thirty days is ₹48.29 Cr (scenario, released once, no interest, rows overlap).

## Problem 3. Client concentration in public agencies, with no public payment days
CRISIL names MCGM, MMRDA, Bank of India, MRVC and central and state PWDs. No payment terms, ageing, retention balance or certification delay is published. What the accounts cannot tell: which agency, which bill, since when.

What the public record cannot tell (all NOT FOUND): debtor, creditor, stock and contract-asset days; retention balance; vendor terms; any borrowing rate; operating cash flow.
