## 1. What you are building

A 16-page A4 portrait booklet titled "Working-capital review", printed and posted to named readers at V.L. Infraprojects Limited. You build ONE design and produce one edition per reader listed in the reader table (see "Reader editions" below).
It answers one question for the reader: what if Crestline were already running at V.L. Infraprojects, how much money and how much time would it save?
Every word and every figure is supplied in the Content section at the end of this prompt. Your job is to design the layout, type and charts yourself. Nothing in this prompt prescribes them.

## 2. Reader editions

Build ONE 16-page design. Then, for each row of the reader table in the Content section, produce one edition of it.
- An edition changes ONLY three things: the cover's "PREPARED EXCLUSIVELY FOR" lines (that reader's name and title), the page 2 letter (that reader's letter text and address block), and the page 2 salutation.
- Every other page, word, figure and chart is IDENTICAL across editions. Do not tailor any body page to a reader.
- Name each edition after its reader as given in the table (for example "Edition: <reader name>"). That label is for the file only and is never printed.
- Body copy is written in a neutral voice. Quotes are credited by name and title, as the Content gives them; never rewrite a credit into "you" for any edition.

## 3. Rules you must follow

1. Use ONLY the text and figures in the Content section. Do not write new copy, captions, taglines, teasers, pull quotes or headings. Where a line has no text, leave it empty.
2. Copy every figure exactly as written: same digits, same decimals, same currency sign, same unit. Do not round, re-total, convert or recompute anything.
3. Headlines exactly as given for each page. Never reword them.
4. Footnotes exactly as given, on the page they belong to.
5. Text on lines starting with ">" is printed copy. Lines starting with "Data:", "With Crestline:" or "Chart name:" give a chart's name and its labelled values. Lines starting with "Footnote:" are printed as that page's footnote. All other plain lines (for example "Hero:", "Side stats:", "Panel name:", "Salutation and letter body:", "No numbers on this page.") are instructions to you and are never printed.
6. No internal references in printed text: no file names, ticket ids, checker notes or notes to the team. A source is printed only in the words the Content gives for it.
7. No printed negatives about the booklet itself: never print what is missing, unknown, unchecked or left out ("not available", "we could not find", "TBD", "placeholder"). Where a line is empty, leave it empty. Print "Not public" or "Not published" only where the Content gives it as quoted copy.
8. No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Use a comma, a full stop, "to" or a middle dot (·) as the Content does.
9. Ownership words stay as written: the client certifies and pays; V.L. Infraprojects measures, prepares, reviews, submits and invoices. Do not swap them.
10. The V.L. Infraprojects logo is the uploaded file, used as supplied. Never redraw, trace or imitate it. The Crestline logo is likewise used as supplied.

### Booklet accuracy rules (apply to every page, every edition)
A1. Claims about Crestline must be true today. Make no claim about Crestline beyond the pasted text. No certification, no hosting region, no encryption claim, no client names, no "live in X days", and no present-tense claim that Crestline connects to the company's ERP. The pilot is one entity, one segment, read-only, 14 days, as the Content words it.
A2. Every quote names the right speaker, exactly as the Content credits it. Never move a quote to another person.
A3. Ours versus the client's follows the product rule. In cash-in steps, acknowledged, certified and collected are the client's acts; measuring, preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing are the company's own. Keep every owner label as given.
A4. No "nothing went wrong" headlines. Use only the headlines given; never soften or invert one.
A5. Every chart carries exactly the labelled values the Content gives, each with its value label, in the order given. Never alter, round or estimate a value to fit.
A6. Interest only at a rate the company discloses. The Content prices no interest. Print no interest or rate figure.
A7. Savings are scenarios. Anything marked "With Crestline", "scenario" or "days less" is presented as a scenario, labelled "Scenario, not a forecast.", and never as a reported figure. Scenario rows overlap and are never added together; print no total the Content does not give. Where the Content names one combined scenario, present it as a single scenario, never as a sum of rows. Charts the Content marks as history only carry no scenario.
A8. Peers and comparatives appear only where the Content prints them. Never build a scorecard of missed promises.
A9. No other company's data. Do not bring in any company, name, figure or text from anywhere other than the Content, including from earlier booklets or your memory. No demo numbers beside the company's figures.
A10. Full address and correct names and titles on the letter page and cover, exactly as the reader table gives them, per edition.
A11. "You said" only for the reader's own words. Never print "you said", "in your own words" or any second-person credit over a quote by someone other than that edition's reader. Use the speaker's name and title or the company's name as the Content gives it. Never print a note or flag on the page.
A12. Never invent figures to fill a chart. Where the Content gives a stage or bar without a value, print it without a number. Never create an "illustrative split" of a total.
A13. No signed numbers in print. Write inflow / outflow, increase / decrease. No "+" or "-" in front of a figure.
A14. Every problem chart shows the outcome with Crestline. Beside the "today" figure, present the "With Crestline" result from the Content, labelled with the days and cash it releases and "Scenario, not a forecast." Use only the values the Content gives; if the Content gives no scenario value for a chart, show none.

### Additional content rules for this booklet
B1. Name each chart exactly as the "Chart name:" line gives it. Do not rename, shorten or reorder the chart's data.
B2. Beside every "With Crestline" result, print the two sentences that follow it in the Content, in full and in that order. Do not extend or paraphrase them.
B3. On the process map, every stage keeps its tag: "Estimate", "Published" or "Not public". Estimates are never priced and never described as the company's measured days. Print the whole RA bill line and the basis line exactly as given.
B4. Page 13 carries no numbers. Print none on it other than the footer.
B5. Where the Content names a separate measure (gross current assets), present it as a separate measure and never add it to, or merge it with, the four legs of the cycle.

## 4. Before you finish, check every page of every edition

1. Count the pages: exactly 16, A4 portrait, in the order of the Content.
2. Read every headline against the Content: same words, same punctuation.
3. Pick every figure on every page and find it in the Content, character for character. Any figure you cannot find there, delete it.
4. Search the whole booklet for en dash and em dash characters (Unicode 2013 and 2014). There must be none.
5. Every chart: every value labelled, none computed, nothing drawn from a figure that is not in the Content.
6. Every scenario value carries "Scenario, not a forecast." Charts marked history only carry none. No scenario totals added except the single named combined scenario.
7. Every footnote is on its page.
8. No word was added: no taglines, no right-hand footer teasers, no extra captions, no "Live in" line, no certification or hosting claim, no client names, no internal references, no printed negatives.
9. The V.L. Infraprojects logo is the uploaded file. Nothing redrawn.
10. The QR code on page 15 (and on page 16) opens crestlineintelligence.com/book-a-demo.
11. Instruction lines from the Content ("Chart name:", "Hero:", notes about decisions and checks) are not printed anywhere.
12. Editions: compare each edition with the first. Only the cover reader lines, the page 2 letter and the salutation differ. No "you said" sits over a quote that is not that edition's reader's own.
