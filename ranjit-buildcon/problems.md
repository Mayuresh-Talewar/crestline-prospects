# Problems: Ranjit Buildcon (RN-S23)
Source for every figure: CARE press release on Ranjit Buildcon Limited, 7 Dec 2023, standalone. All figures as published, December 2023 (the record is nearly three years old and says nothing about today).

Respect first: more than five decades in construction; total operating income Rs 1,108.24 Cr in FY22 and Rs 1,267.92 Cr in FY23 (up 14.4%); profit after tax Rs 34.43 Cr and Rs 43.23 Cr; an order book of Rs 4,338 Cr at 30 Sep 2023 (3.42x FY23 income; metro rail 51%, water supply 27%, bridges 17%); a special category-1, AA class contractor in six states.

## 1. The days grew with the income (p3 and p4 of the source)
CARE puts gross current assets at 220 days in FY23 against 190 in FY22, and the working-capital cycle at 137 days against 130 (denominator not stated by CARE). CARE names debtors outstanding for more than six months, retention money and security deposits as where cash sits; no amount is published. Respectful framing: a larger book needs more cash; the question is where each bill waits.
What the accounts cannot say: which bill, which project, which desk, since when.

## 2. A day is large against a book this size
One day of FY23 income is Rs 3.4738 Cr (1,267.92 / 365). Ten days is Rs 34.74 Cr; thirty days Rs 104.21 Cr, applied to the 220 GCA days as one block. Scenario, not a forecast. No interest (no rate published).

## 3. Nothing says where inside the bill the days sit
GCA is a broad measure and is not split by CARE into receivables, retention, deposits or stock. The process map shows the eleven stages with ESTIMATES scaled from 220 (pattern from Crestline's demonstration ledger), never Ranjit Buildcon's measured days.
