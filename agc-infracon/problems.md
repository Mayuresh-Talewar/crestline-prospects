# AGC Infracon Private Limited: problems (step 2)

Respect first. On the published record AGC Infracon is profitable and growing: total operating income Rs 567.63 Cr in FY24 against Rs 513.30 Cr (up 10.58%, computed), PAT Rs 38.39 Cr against Rs 32.57 Cr (up 17.87%, computed), with an unexecuted order book of Rs 1,702.68 Cr at 31 Dec 2024, "3x of FY24 revenue" in the agency's words.
Source for all: Infomerics Ratings press release, 23 Apr 2025, standalone, FY24 audited (pages 2, 3 and 5). The figures are dated: they are as published on 23 Apr 2025, order book as at 31 Dec 2024.

The question, not an accusation: as that book executes, where does each bill wait, and whose move ends the wait?

## Problem 1. A large book becomes bills, and each bill has a wait
- Fact: Rs 1,702.68 Cr unexecuted at 31 Dec 2024 (p3), against Rs 567.63 Cr of FY24 total operating income (p5). Infomerics: 3x of FY24 revenue.
- One day of FY24 revenue is Rs 1.555 Cr (567.63 / 365, computed). Ten days off one wait is Rs 15.55 Cr of cash, once; thirty days is Rs 46.65 Cr (calc).
- What the accounts cannot say: which bill, on which site, at which desk, since when. A rating release carries no receivable or unbilled balance and no days.
- Whose move: preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing are AGC's; acknowledging, certifying and paying are the client's.

## Problem 2. Most of the rated limits are guarantees, so the cash lines are the smaller part
- Fact (p1, p7, 23 Apr 2025): rated limits Rs 235.98 Cr. Bank guarantee 185.00 (78.4%), overdraft 30.00, cash credit 20.00, GECL 0.98; fund based total 50.98 (21.6%).
- Why it matters for working capital: the cash side of the limits is the smaller part, and bills turning into cash is the other source of cash for the work. Every day a bill waits is a day that cash is not back. Nothing here says the lines are stretched; the record does not give utilisation by project or by bill.
- Scenario, same rupee scale: thirty days off one wait is Rs 46.65 Cr, ten days Rs 15.55 Cr, once. No interest is priced (no rate is published).
- What the accounts cannot say: which bills are holding cash on which project.

## Problem 3. The agency names the working-capital cycle, and the accounts cannot show it by bill
- Fact (p1): Infomerics lists "Improvement in working capital cycle leading to improvement in liquidity" among its upward factors.
- The rationale gives totals; the ERP holds the dates. Per bill: measured, certified, invoiced, paid, and the desk each sat on. A rating release cannot rank them by rupees and days.
- What Crestline would add, once connected: a ranked list of waiting bills with value, days, owner and the client's own terms.
- What the accounts cannot say: the waits themselves. They are not published and the booklet draws none.

## Not printed (kept out per Garvis rulings)
Rating movements, statutory dues, agencies that do not rate it, the reclassification and both current ratios, the order book execution period, the "Consolidated" heading, any "cured" or system claim, the Government of Maharashtra sector figure.
