Paste everything below this line into Claude Design.

## 1. What you are building

A 16-page A4 portrait booklet titled "Working-capital review", printed and posted to named readers at Vikran Engineering Limited. You build ONE design and produce one edition per reader listed in the reader table (see "Reader editions" below).
It answers one question for the reader: what if Crestline were already running at Vikran Engineering, how much money and how much time would it save?
Every word and every figure is supplied in the Content section at the end of this prompt. The Content carries only text, figures and data; how it is laid out and drawn is yours to decide. Use the Crestline logo file as supplied and never redraw it. No Vikran Engineering logo is used: the company name appears as text.

## 2. Reader editions

Build ONE 16-page design. Then, for each row of the reader table in the Content section, produce one edition of it.
- An edition changes ONLY three things: the cover's "PREPARED EXCLUSIVELY FOR" lines (that reader's name and title), the page 2 letter (that reader's letter text and address block), and the page 2 salutation.
- Every other page, word, figure and chart is IDENTICAL across editions. Do not tailor any body page to a reader.
- Name each edition after its reader as given in the table (for example "Edition: <reader name>"). That label is for the file only and is never printed.
- Body copy is written in a neutral voice. Quotes are credited by name and title, as the Content gives them; never rewrite a credit into "you" for any edition.

## 3. Rules you must follow

1. Use ONLY the text and figures in the Content section. Do not write new copy, captions, taglines, teasers, pull quotes or headings. If a line has no text, leave it empty.
2. Copy every figure exactly as written: same digits, same decimals, same currency sign, same unit. Do not round, re-total, convert or recompute anything.
3. Headlines exactly as given. Never reword them.
4. Footnotes exactly as given, on the page they belong to. Where a footnote carries a tag in square brackets, print the caveat line that tag stands for (listed under "Footnote tags" in the Content) in its place, in full.
5. Text inside quotation marks in the Content is printed copy. Lines that start with ">" are printed copy. Words outside quotation marks and outside ">" lines (for example "Data:", "With Crestline:", "How lines:", "Chart name (exactly)", "Never compute a figure that is not given here", "No numbers on this page", "Contents:") are instructions to you and are never printed.
6. No internal references in printed text. Ids, file names, tags in square brackets, and any note addressed to the team are never printed. A source is printed only in the words the Content gives for it.
7. No printed negatives about the booklet itself: never print what is missing, unknown, unchecked or left out ("not available", "we could not find", "TBD", "placeholder"). Print "Not published" or "Not public" only where the Content gives it as copy.
8. No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Use a comma, a full stop, "to" or a middle dot (·) as the Content does.
9. Ownership words stay as written: the client certifies and pays; Vikran measures, prepares, submits and escalates. Do not swap them.
10. Chart names are printed exactly as the Content gives them. Where the Content gives a chart as "Data:" lines, those labels and values are the whole chart: print every label with its value.

### Booklet accuracy rules (apply to every page, every edition)
A1. Claims about Crestline must be true today. Make no claim about Crestline beyond the pasted text. No certification, hosting region or encryption claim, no client names, no "live in X days", and no present-tense claim that Crestline connects to the company's ERP. A3. Ours versus the client's follows the product rule. In cash-in steps, acknowledged, certified and collected are the client's acts; measuring, preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing are the company's own. Keep every owner tag as given.
A4. No "nothing went wrong" headlines. Use only the headlines given; never soften or invert one.
A5. Every value printed with a chart is exactly the value the Content gives for that label, with its label.
A6. No interest or borrowing figure appears anywhere. The Content prices none.
A7. Savings are scenarios. Every "With Crestline" or scenario value is labelled "Scenario, not a forecast." Scenario rows overlap and are never added together; print no total the Content does not give.
A8. No peers or comparatives appear. Build no scorecard.
A9. No other company's data. Do not bring in any company, name, figure or text from anywhere other than the Content, including from earlier booklets or memory. No demo numbers beside the company's figures.
A10. Full address and correct names and titles on the cover and the letter page, exactly as the reader table gives them, per edition.
A11. "You said" only for the reader's own words. Never print "you said" or "in your own words" over a quote by someone other than that edition's reader.
A12. Never invent figures. Where the Content gives a stage or line without a value, print it without a number. Never create a split of a total.
A13. No signed numbers in print. Write inflow / outflow, increase / decrease. No "+" or "-" in front of a figure.
A14. Every problem chart shows "today" and "With Crestline" from the Content only. Where the Content gives no scenario value for a chart, show none.

### Additional rules for this booklet

10. Editions: compare each edition with the first. Only the cover reader lines, the page 2 address block, the salutation and the letter differ. No "you said" sits over a quote that is not that edition's reader's own.

### How Crestline saves it (two lines each, printed in full wherever a result says "How lines")
- How lines, Receivables:
> Once connected, Crestline ages every unpaid invoice against its own due date and ranks them by money × days past term, naming the client and the desk.
> Each one becomes a ticket with an owner and a date, and the same figure is re-measured until the bill is paid.
- How lines, Unbilled work:
> Once connected, Crestline rebuilds each bill from its own dates and shows how long the work has waited to be measured, submitted and certified, and whose move ends the wait.
> The longest waits are raised first as tickets, ours to fix where the step is ours and chased where it is the client's.
- How lines, Retention:
> Once connected, Crestline tracks retention bill by bill against each contract's release condition, so nothing that is due to come back is forgotten.
> Each release that is due becomes a ticket with an owner and a date, and is re-measured until the money arrives.
- How lines, Site stock:
> Once connected, Crestline replays stock movements on their posting dates and shows what has sat on site unissued, by site and by value.
> It names the stock to draw down first, so cash stops waiting in material that is not yet in the work.
- How lines, Process map:
> Once connected, Crestline measures each of the eleven stages from the dates your ERP already records, so the wait has a stage, a desk and a number.
> The step that holds the most money for the most days is shown first, with the person who usually records it.
- How lines, Ranking:
> Crestline prices every move in one currency, cash released once, and ranks them, so the first move is the largest one on the evidence.
> The simulator stops at what the contract allows: no lever is moved past the term the client or vendor signed.
