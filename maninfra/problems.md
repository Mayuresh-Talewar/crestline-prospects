# Man Infraconstruction Limited: problems (MI-3)

Group booklet. Lead: Man Infraconstruction Limited (MAN EPC); MICL (Real Estate) named. Prepared 8 Oct 2026.
Every figure is tagged: **[EPC]** = EPC segment, read on the STANDALONE accounts (standalone is a single EPC segment, AR FY2025-26 note 4.06(a), p.148); **[MICL]** = real-estate segment; **[Consol]** = company-wide, consolidated. Computed figures come from `calc/maninfra-savings.output.txt` (section in brackets); sources and pages are in `data.md` / `depth.md`. Rs Cr unless stated.

## Plain verdict first
The EPC working-capital problem is SMALL, and the booklet should say so. In FY26 the EPC business held Rs 37.9 Cr of receivables and Rs 11.6 Cr of contract assets, against Rs 74.6 Cr of contract liabilities (client money received ahead of work). Net trade working capital was **-Rs 42.8 Cr**: clients were funding the EPC business, not the reverse [EPC, calc s.1]. There is no EPC borrowing (standalone borrowings nil, AR26 p.108), so there is no interest to save.
The honest angle is forward-looking: **keep the next large EPC order cash-healthy from its first bill.** The MD said on 21 Aug 2026 that one "significantly large order for EPC" is "on the final verge of negotiations" (analyst meet p.6). It is NOT signed and is never presented as won.

## P1. The third-party EPC book is running off [EPC]
- Revenue 797.8 (FY23) to 285.6 (FY26): **-64.2%**; -27.7% on FY25 [calc s.1].
- Order book Rs 980+ Cr (31 Mar 2023) to Rs 305 Cr (30 Jun 2026): **-68.9%** [calc s.1; IP Q4FY23 slide 19, IP Q1FY27 slide 28, 12 Aug 2026].
- The one large external contract, BMCT Terminal 4 (Rs 1,800+ Cr), is "95%+ executed, as on Jun-26" (IP Q1FY27 slide 27).
- Management says growth will lean to real estate: "real estate would definitely be the heavy lifter" (Manan Shah, MD, Q4 FY26 call, 14 May 2026, p.9).
- What this means for working capital: small balances today, and the next order will set the pattern for years. A large order that starts with slow certification and billing locks cash in from month one.

## P2. Collections are fast on average, with an older tail that grew [EPC]
- Receivables fell from 277.1 to 37.9 (-86.3% on FY23); crude receivable days 127 to 48 [calc s.1].
- FY26 ageing: 84.5% under six months, but **Rs 3.78 Cr (10.5%) past due over six months**, against Rs 1.19 Cr (2.0%) in FY25 (AR26 p.127). The "not due" cell (180.46 lakh) equals the non-current balance; printed as reported, possible misprint.
- A receivable-days figure is a company average: it cannot say which contract, which bill, or which client desk the older Rs 3.78 Cr sits with.

## P3. Work done, not yet billable, came back [EPC]
- Contract assets 0.0 (FY25) to **11.6 (FY26) = 14.8 days of revenue** [calc s.2]. A Crestline analytical metric, not GAAP.
- It is offset at company level by Rs 74.6 Cr of contract liabilities, so it is not a funding strain today. On a new large order the same queue (measured, not yet certified or billed) is where the first months' cash waits.

## P4. Part of what is owed comes from group companies [EPC; group work, never collection risk]
- Related-party trade receivables FY26 **Rs 6.40 Cr = 16.9%** of 37.9 [calc s.1]; Atmosphere Realty (associate) Rs 5.49 Cr, up from Rs 4.45 Cr in FY25 (AR26 p.160). Third-party receivables Rs 31.5 Cr [calc s.1].
- Related-party contract revenue fell to Rs 14.9 Cr (5.2% of revenue) in FY26 (note 4.09). Most of the current order book is group projects (IP Q1FY27 slide 28).
- This is group work, paid from group funds; it is shown labelled and never called a collection risk. The problem is visibility: the accounts net it into one receivable line, so third-party and group cash cycles cannot be read apart.

## P5. Cash from operations swings year to year [EPC]
- 15.9, 24.8, 230.2, 15.4, 46.8 over FY22 to FY26 [calc s.1]. FY24's 230.2 coincides with receivables falling from 277.1 to 67.2: the cash came when the FY23 balance was collected, and the accounts show the year, not the bills that moved.

## What their books cannot tell them [EPC]
- Per contract and per RA bill: the date work was measured, submitted, certified, invoiced and paid, so which step a rupee is waiting at.
- Whose desk it is: their own (measure, submit, invoice) or the client's (acknowledge, certify, pay).
- Since when: the age of each bill at its current step, not an annual average.
- Against what: each contract's own payment term and certification window. None is published, so today no "to terms" target can be set (calc s.2).
- Retention: which amounts have met their defect-liability milestone (not published; calc s.4 treats the whole Rs 3.6 Cr as a ceiling).
- Group and third-party cash cycles side by side, labelled.
