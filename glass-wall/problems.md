# Glass Wall Systems: three working capital problems (GW-2)

Basis for every figure: RHP dated 1 Sep 2026, restated consolidated (Rs million in the filing, Rs crore by script). Page numbers are the printed pages, checked on their images. The one exception is stated where it appears.

## Opening note

Glass Wall Systems listed on 16 Sep 2026 with net cash of Rs 79.25 Cr (cash and bank balances Rs 85.93 Cr less borrowings Rs 6.68 Cr, RHP p82 and p83), revenue of Rs 456.97 Cr in FY26 (RHP p84) and a company whose prospectus sets out its own working capital table in risk factor 13 (RHP p38). That is a business that funds itself and explains itself. The three problems below are not about weakness. They are about where cash waits inside a healthy contractor, and what the accounts, by their nature, cannot show.

## Problem 1: the headline of 45 days is not the whole wait

**What the filing shows.** The company's table prints net working capital days of 45 for FY26, against 69 for FY25 and 57 for FY24 (RHP p38). That measure takes trade receivable days (88) less trade payable days (109) plus inventory days (66), the last two measured on the cost of raw materials. It leaves out contract assets, which are unbilled revenue and retention pending milestone: Rs 49.15 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 (RHP p369). On the revenue clock, trade receivables of Rs 108.96 Cr (RHP p38) plus contract assets of Rs 49.15 Cr make Rs 158.12 Cr (summed from the unrounded Rs million, so the rounded parts differ by Rs 0.01 Cr), which is 126.3 days of FY26 revenue (computed, 365 days). The same block was 120.3 days in FY25 and 140.8 days in FY24 (366 days). These are two different measures, built on different totals, and they are never subtracted from each other here.

**Fairness.** Customers also fund the work: contract liabilities, which are advances from customers and billing in excess, stood at Rs 88.00 Cr (RHP p369). A block of 126.3 days is cash waiting, not cash lost.

**What the filings cannot tell.** Which project, which client, which bill, which desk, and since when. Whether a day in the block sits on the company's side (measure, prepare, review, submit, invoice) or on the client's (acknowledge, certify, pay). Only a bill by bill trail shows that.

## Problem 2: receivables grew faster than revenue in FY26

**What the filing shows.** Trade receivables rose from Rs 50.77 Cr to Rs 108.96 Cr, growth of 114.6%, while revenue from operations rose from Rs 278.33 Cr to Rs 456.97 Cr, growth of 64.2% (RHP p84 and p38, growth computed). The company's printed receivable days moved from 77 in FY24 to 67 in FY25 and 88 in FY26 (RHP p38). Receivables past due by more than 6 months were Rs 11.95 Cr, 11.0% of receivables, down from 23.0% in FY25 (RHP p39, shares computed), so the share of old balances fell. In the company's investor presentation of 6 Oct 2026 (slide 28, page 29 of the file), the cash flow statement shows changes in working capital of minus Rs 17.7 Cr for FY26, that is cash absorbed, against Rs 2.6 Cr released in FY25. That slide is a different document from the RHP and is quoted as printed, not combined with any RHP figure.

**Fairness.** A year in which revenue grew by nearly two thirds will carry more billed work at year end. The question is how long that billed work waits.

**What the filings cannot tell.** How much of the new receivable is inside its agreed credit period (the RHP says 0 to 60 days, p429, as a general range, not per contract), how much is waiting on a client's certification, and which clients hold it. The top 10 clients are 86.40% of FY26 revenue (RHP p27), so a handful of desks decide the figure.

## Problem 3: retention is held for 12 to 24 months

**What the filing shows.** "Our customers typically withhold 5.00% of each contract value as retention, typically releasing it only after the 12 to 24 months retention period." At 31 Mar 2026, retention held for more than 12 months was Rs 15.38 Cr and retention held for less than 12 months was Rs 20.58 Cr: Rs 35.96 Cr in all, equal to 28.7 days of FY26 revenue, with 42.8% of it held more than 12 months (RHP p39, totals and shares computed). The filing says release depends on "defect-free performance, final documentation and client's liquidity position" and that in some cases retentions are substituted with bank guarantees (RHP p39 and p429).

**Fairness.** Retention is a standard term in the trade, the client's right under the contract, and it is not a sign of anything wrong. Retention pending milestone is reported inside contract assets, and retention that has fallen due is reclassified into trade receivables (RHP p369). It therefore sits inside the Rs 158.12 Cr of Problem 1 and is never added to it.

**What the filings cannot tell.** Which contracts hold the retention, the date each release is due, which releases wait on final documents the company prepares and which wait on the client's certificate, and how much of the Rs 35.96 Cr is in contract assets and how much in receivables.
