## 2. Framing, trust, legal

Evidence on "confidential/classified" styling in B2B: I found NO measured test. Only adjacent evidence: regulators and the DMA treat envelopes that mislead on who sent them as deceptive (https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna9881857, US, consumer; https://www.uspis.gov/news/scam-article/government-look-alike-mail). Strength: Weak, wrong market, wrong audience. So "backlash" is plausible but unmeasured; "it lifts open rate" is equally unmeasured.

Legal (India), not legal advice, get a lawyer to clear the final piece:
- Forgery/false document: BNS s.335 = a document made dishonestly to deceive about its authenticity or authority (https://www.vidhijudicial.com/section-335-of-the-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-(bns)-of-2023.html). A mock stamp is low risk ONLY if it cannot be read as a real government/target-company classification. Do NOT use government marks, "Secret/Top Secret", or the target's own letterhead/logo as though the file were theirs. Intent to deceive is the test (https://hyperverge.co/blog/forgery-laws-punishment/). Moderate.
- Official Secrets Act 1923 concerns government secrets (https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/18647); avoid "classified"/"official secret" words anyway.
- Defamation, BNS s.356: truth is a defence only if made "for the public good"; whether that holds is a question of fact (https://devgan.in/bns/section/356/). A private mailing of accurate public figures to the CEO himself has little "publication" to third parties (the EA/CFO will see it), but anything that says "mismanaged", "failing" or implies fault is the exposure. Frame figures as arithmetic on published numbers, not judgments. Moderate.
- Listed-company data: annual reports, results and rating rationales are public. Use only public data and cite each. If any figure came from a non-public source (a leak, an ex-employee), stop: unpublished price-sensitive information issues under SEBI rules (not researched here; ask counsel).
- The word "Confidential" on your OWN analysis of public data is honest if the page says "prepared by Crestline from public filings, for [CEO] only". The risk is claiming insider knowledge you do not have.

Weakness-first vs opportunity-first: no controlled evidence found for either in B2B. Reasoning from the sources: CEOs of NCC/KEC already know the receivable problem (rating agencies say it in public, below). Weakness-first tells them what they know and risks defensiveness, and the EA/CFO sees "your company is failing". Lead with the money (Rs X Cr of capital tied up, Rs Y Cr a year in carry) and the one thing public data cannot show. Treat this as a judgement, not a finding.

## 4. Brochure, follow-up, cost, failure

Page by page (structure only):
1. Cover: addressed to the CEO by name; "Prepared by Crestline from NCC's public filings and CARE's rating note for [Name] only". No faked classification mark.
2. "What the public record already says": 3 sharpest numbers, each with source line and a "computed by us" tag. Arithmetic, no adjectives.
3. "What the public record cannot say": the 4-5 questions only the ERP answers (above), framed as the money to release.
4. "How Crestline answers them": one screen, one project example. Use Crestline's own demo data (Grey Infra); never NCC data it does not have.
5. About Crestline + one ask: 30 minutes, with the CFO, and a QR/short URL to book.
Keep it to 6 pages max; an A5 cover note signed by a named founder.

Follow-up sequence (all single-source reasoning; firstsales.io supports "mail needs a follow-up"):
- Day 0: courier delivery, confirm via tracking/POD that it reached the office.
- Day 2-3: personal email + LinkedIn note to the CEO, and a separate note to the CFO ("sent a note to Mr X's office").
- Day 7: call the EA to confirm receipt. Day 14: email with one new public number (e.g. next rating or quarterly result). Day 30: stop.

Cost/time ballpark (UNVERIFIED estimate, no source found; get 2 quotes): 6-page A4 on 170gsm + rigid folder/envelope Rs 800-2,500 per piece at 1-5 pieces (digital print); premium courier Rs 150-600 domestic. Total under Rs 5,000. Real cost: 2-3 days of analysis and design, the CFO-level follow-up and a lawyer's read.

3 biggest failures:
1. Dies at the gatekeeper or goes to the CFO's tray; no follow-up lands. (firstsales.io failure mode.)
2. A number is wrong or reads as an accusation; the CEO's team checks it in minutes. The CFO knows the real DPO/DIO; if the pack prints a screener-style "DPO 384" the whole pack is dismissed. Defamation/forgery framing issues (section 2).
3. Wrong buyer or timing: NCC's CEO is not the economic buyer for ERP-layer tools, and its team may be mid-recovery (Rs 2,500 Cr+ collected since Q4 FY26), so "your receivables are a problem" may be stale.

## 5. Verdict: PARTLY

Will it get a meeting? As a single piece to one CEO, "maybe": the research cannot quantify odds for India infra buyers, and the vendor stats are weak. It will work as one component of a 3-channel, 30-day sequence to the CEO plus CFO, with a bounded ask. As the only touch, probably not.
Changes that raise the odds:
- Lead with money and the unanswerable questions, not a verdict on the company; keep the "confidential" device to a plain "Prepared for" label.
- Print only numbers traceable to a filing or rating note, with the source line printed; no unaudited DPO/DIO.
- Address CFO and CEO; get a warm intro (a shared lender, rating-agency or auditor contact) if one exists, since cold mail has no measured edge in India.
- Pre-qualify: run a 5-minute check that NCC is not already running a collections programme, and plan a Plan B account (KEC) in case the first piece misses.
- Treat it as an experiment: log delivery, open, reply; send 3-5 similar pieces to different companies before concluding anything.
- Have a lawyer clear the final copy.

Unverified in this pass: FY26 annual-report notes (payables, retention, inventory), PNC/HG/Dilip data, India print/courier prices, ANA/DMA source report itself.
