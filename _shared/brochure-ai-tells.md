# Brochure AI-tells: what to avoid, what to do (SALES-3)

Scope: 5-page A4 print, "Personal & Confidential", to Capacite's Executive Chairman. Research ≤15 min, 7 searches, no page fetched in full. Evidence ratings: Strong = 3+ independent sources, Moderate = 2, Weak = 1 or practitioner opinion.

## 1. Visual tells (rating: Strong for web UI, Moderate for print)
Sources: [Fountain Institute, 7 tells](https://pages.thefountaininstitute.com/posts/7-tells-that-a-ui-is-ai-generated), [Impeccable slop list](https://impeccable.style/slop), [925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells), [prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website), [Capital&Compute](https://capitalandcompute.net/blog/fix-ai-slop-design/).
- Purple/indigo-blue gradients, cyan on dark, gradient text on headings or metrics.
- Three icon cards in a row; emoji or generic line icons as bullets; identical rounded cards with equal padding.
- Inter / default sans everywhere; perfect symmetry; uniform spacing with nothing out of rhythm.
- Cause: models return the median of their training data (Tailwind defaults post-2019). Median = tell.
- Caveat: these lists are about web UI. Print transfer (stock photos, generic bar/donut charts, hero image + 3 stats) is my inference, not sourced. Weak.

## 2. Copy tells (rating: Moderate; all practitioner/blog, one academic mention)
Sources: [Beazley top-50 list](https://gregbeazley.beehiiv.com/p/top-50-ai-detectable-words-and-phrases-for-chatgpt), [PlusAI overused words](https://plusai.com/blog/the-most-overused-chatgpt-words), [Medium 15 phrases](https://medium.com/everyday-ai/these-15-phrases-prove-you-used-chatgpt-and-everyone-can-tell-53accf90a742), [Mere Sophistry on "delve"](https://meresophistry.substack.com/p/id-like-to-delve-into-how-ai-is-fostering) (cites Leppanen 2025: "delve" ~10.5x, "foster" ~10.8x post-ChatGPT).
- Words: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, vibrant, unlock, unleash, leverage, comprehensive, robust, seamless, navigate, testament, holistic.
- Phrases: "it's important to note", "in today's fast-paced...", "a testament to", "not just X, but Y".
- Rhythm (not sourced in my searches, widely reported; treat Weak): triads everywhere, em-dash in every other sentence, every paragraph the same length, a tidy punchline closing each block, claims with no number, name, date or place.

## 3. Executive trust / response penalty
- [MarketScale / TrustRadius](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/business-services/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-outputs-and-vendors-are-underestimating-how-far-trust-has-fallen): 94% of B2B buyers fact-check AI outputs; 47% trust online resources (incl. AI content) less than before. Moderate (vendor-reported surveys).
- [arXiv 2509.09645](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09645v1) and [Macquarie "AI penalty and disclosure paradox"](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/the-ai-penalty-and-disclosure-paradox-trust-authenticity-and-know/): when readers KNOW a message is AI-written they rate the sender "lazy, insincere, low effort". Readers told nothing rated it as well as human text. Hohenstein & Jung 2020/2023 show the same warmth penalty. Strong that the penalty exists; note it bites on perceived AI, and people often miss it ([U-Michigan summary](https://prod.lsa.umich.edu/psych/news-events/all-news/faculty-news/most-people-do-not-realize-when-a-personal-message-they-receive-.html)). So the risk is a visible tell, not AI use itself.
- Disconfirming: no data found on Indian promoters/chairmen specifically, or on printed vs email penalty. The client's "Indian executives dismiss AI" is plausible, unproven here.

## 4. What reads as crafted and credible (rating: Moderate for personalisation, Weak for finishes)
- Hand-signed + named letters lift response: [meta-analysis, PMC1574304](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1574304/) (+4 to +10 points absolute). Caveat: one RCT on 3,799 gynaecologists found hand-signed 79.1% vs printed signature 78.4%, no difference. So a signature helps average cold mail but is no magic for an already-engaged elite audience.
- Doctor survey: 36% plain letter, 48% with handwritten note, 75% with handwritten Post-it ([Neuroscience Marketing](https://www.neurosciencemarketing.com/blog/articles/post-it-personalization.htm)). Weak-moderate (old, one study).
- Handwritten direct mail claims of 5-20% response are vendor claims ([Inkpact](https://inkpact.com/blog/handwritten-direct-mail)). Weak.
- Paper stock, letterpress, wax seal, marginalia: no study found. Practitioner reasoning only; the logic is cost and effort signalling, which is the opposite of the "lazy" penalty above.
- Real specifics (his own filings, his sites, his numbers) are the one thing a template cannot fake. This is the strongest lever by inference from the fact-check data.

## 5. Checklist, ranked by impact

### Never (15)
1. Never use a claim without a named figure, source, date or site (his own annual report numbers beat any benchmark).
2. Never write "unlock / leverage / seamless / holistic / empower / landscape / delve / journey".
3. Never use "not just X, but Y" or "it's not about A, it's about B".
4. Never write in triads by reflex ("faster, smarter, leaner"); vary to two or four.
5. Never use em-dashes as rhythm; use commas, full stops, brackets.
6. Never use three-equal-card rows with icons.
7. Never use gradients, glow, glassmorphism, or purple/blue tech palette.
8. Never use stock photos, AI imagery, emoji, or generic line-icon sets.
9. Never use a decorative infographic or donut/bar chart where a small ruled table says it better.
10. Never use a printed signature font; sign by hand (scan acceptable only if it is a real signature).
11. Never open with "I hope this finds you well" or praise of his company in superlatives.
12. Never make every paragraph the same length or end each with a punchy one-liner.
13. Never use perfectly uniform spacing, centred everything, or a hero + 3 stats layout.
14. Never mention AI, "platform", "revolutionary", or buzzword acronyms to this reader; say "working capital" and "receivables".
15. Never put a generic CTA ("Book a demo"); ask for 30 minutes on a named day.

### Do (15)
1. Lead with his numbers: Capacite's receivables, retention, contract-asset days from public filings, each cited (annual report page, quarter).
2. Open with a signed one-page letter from a named human, first person, one idea, plain sentences.
3. Sign by hand in ink; add one handwritten line (a date or "Mr. X, I'll ring Thursday").
4. Use small ruled tables with mono figures; one chart max, hand-labelled.
5. Add marginalia: a handwritten-style note beside one figure ("source: Q2 FY26 results, p.14"), real, not a font gimmick everywhere.
6. Name the money in rupees and days, as he counts them (Cr, not millions).
7. Show an uncertainty or a limit: "we have not seen your ERP; this is from public data only."
8. Serif headlines with unequal sizes, asymmetric margins, one deliberate off-grid element.
9. Choose uncoated cream stock (about 120 gsm+) and a real envelope, hand-addressed, "Personal & Confidential" stamped.
10. Include an intentional imperfection (slightly uneven column, pen correction on one line).
11. Keep copy short, concrete, varied in length; read aloud to a human before print.
12. Put a verifiable contact: direct mobile, office address, and one referee or precedent he can phone.
13. Use industry vocabulary of site work (RA bills, certification, retention release) used correctly.
14. End with one specific ask: a 30-minute call, two proposed slots.
15. Have a real person (ideally someone who knows the sector) proofread for tone; Indian business English conventions (lakh/crore, "kindly" avoided, formal address).
