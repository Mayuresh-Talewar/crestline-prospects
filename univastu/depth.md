# Univastu India: story angle (UV-1)

## Respect first
A Pune civil contractor founded in 2009 that went from Rs 59 Cr of revenue in FY22 to Rs 243 Cr in FY26 (CRISIL 15 Jul 2026; AR FY26 p146: Rs 243.35 Cr, +42.16%), PAT up 65.55% to Rs 25.69 Cr, 17.10% EBITDA margin (AR p5), and an order book its own AR puts at Rs 1,854 Cr at 31 Mar 2026. CRISIL upgraded it to BBB-/A3 on 15 Jul 2026 and raised the rated limits from Rs 35 Cr to Rs 113 Cr. The MD writes that "improving cash conversion and timely collections will remain key priorities as our execution scale increases" (AR p5). That sentence is the opening.

## Then the question: where do the bills wait?
Every point is the company's own figure, consolidated, FY26 unless stated.
1. **A year-end that is mostly one quarter.** Q4 revenue was about Rs 109.44 Cr, 45% of the year (AR p5). Current trade receivables went from Rs 7.23 Cr to Rs 80.63 Cr (AR p145, note 12). CRISIL: "receivables remained high ... due to high fourth-quarter revenue bookings". Management says about 87% of that was recovered within 45 days of year end (CFO, call 26 Jun 2026). Question: in which weeks of the quarter do bills sit before they are raised, certified or paid, and on which sites?
2. **Work done but not billed.** Unbilled revenue Rs 66.36 Cr (FY25 Rs 16.19 Cr) and net contract assets Rs 87.47 Cr (note 16, AR p162): about Rs 153.8 Cr in all, against Rs 243.35 Cr of annual revenue. The auditor lists "recoverability of contract assets and trade receivables" as a key audit matter, citing work "not formally acknowledged by the customer" (AR p85). Question: which running bills are waiting for the client's certificate, and since when?
3. **Days on the face of the rating.** CRISIL: GCA 287-402 days over three years; receivables 60-160 (our check: 159.8 in FY26 against 59.9 in FY25); upward rating trigger is "GCAs below 300 days". Question: what would 100 days out of a 160-day receivable cycle be worth, and on what bills?
4. **A long tail, small but old.** Rs 3.65 Cr of current receivables are more than three years past due date and Rs 0.15 Cr 2-3 years (AR p160). Small beside the whole book, but it is the one place the accounts show bills that stopped moving.
5. **Payables on the other side.** CRISIL puts supplier credit at about 813 days at March 2026 "pertaining to contractual requirement towards work orders" (payable days unreconciled, see data.md). The MD says the cycle is 90 days. Whichever is right, vendor timing and goods-received matching is the other half of the cash cycle and is invisible in a year-end balance.
6. **Systems already in place.** The AR says the Quadra ERP runs "across all project sites". Crestline sits above it: it does not replace it, it reads which desk each bill waits on.

## What the accounts cannot tell them
Site, bill, desk, and since when. A balance sheet says Rs 80.63 Cr is owed; it cannot say which of the metro, sports-complex and PWD packages holds it, whose queue it is in, or how long each step took.

## Guardrails for the next step
- Respect framing: growth, upgrade and honest MD wording come first. No "stretched" or "poor" language; use CRISIL's own words.
- Use one basis: consolidated FY26. Never pair standalone Rs 22,921.58 L total income or PAT Rs 2,338.50 L with these.
- Order book: say "1,854 Cr, as stated in the annual report" or "about 1,734 Cr per CRISIL", never merge.
- Do not use the dossier's 35% JV share or Rs 601 Cr; unverified.
- Payable days stay out until reconciled. Interest rate: none disclosed for working capital, so no interest savings.
- Collection claim (87%/80%/85%) is management's spoken statement, not audited; quote with the speaker.
