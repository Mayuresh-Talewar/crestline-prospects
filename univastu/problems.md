# Univastu India: problems (UV-S23, deepened 2026-10-10 under DP-UV). Respect first, then the question.

Source for every figure: Univastu India Limited, Annual Report FY26 (17th), consolidated, Ind AS, published August 2026; accounts signed 27 May 2026; page images checked 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-10. Page numbers and the full evidence are in data.md ("Depth (2026-10-10)"). Cr figures are the lakh figure divided by 100. Ownership follows the product rule: measure, prepare, review, submit and invoice are Univastu's acts; acknowledge, certify and pay are the client's; receive a vendor bill, match and pay are Univastu's; raise the bill and deliver are the vendor's.

Respect: revenue from operations 171.18 Cr (FY25) to 243.35 Cr (FY26), up 42.16%; EBITDA 41.61 Cr (17.10%); PAT 25.69 Cr; order book about 1,854 Cr at 31 Mar 2026, the company's own figure; Quadra ERP across all sites; net cash from operating activities 11.99 Cr in FY26 after an outflow of 5.66 Cr in FY25. The MD writes: "Improving cash conversion and timely collections will remain key priorities as our execution scale increases." (Dr. Pradeep Khandagale, Chairman and Managing Director). The same report lists "Delayed realization of receivables can impact cash flows" as a risk and names its mitigation: the company "prefers projects backed by central and multilateral funding agencies" (MD&A, risk section).

Each problem: stage, owner, verified figures, mechanism (what the source says, or what would be measured), what Crestline shows, respect-first framing. Where the source does not state a cause, none is given.

## Problem 1. Work done and not yet billed (stage: certification and billing). Owner: split.
Figures (consolidated, 31 Mar 2026 against 31 Mar 2025, note 16): unbilled revenue 66.36 Cr (16.19 Cr); contract assets 87.47 Cr (86.83 Cr), made of actual work performed 655.29 Cr (387.88 Cr) less progress billing 567.83 Cr (301.05 Cr). Together 153.8 Cr. Per day 243.35 / 365 = 0.6667 Cr; contract asset days 230.7 (Crestline analytical measure, not an accounting standard). Q4 FY26 revenue was approximately 109.44 Cr (MD message), "reflecting the acceleration in project execution and billing".
Mechanism, from the company's own policy 2.10: revenue is recognised as cost incurred over total estimated cost; and the amount due from customers "is shown as part of other current assets as the contractual right for consideration is dependant on completion of contractual milestones". So the wait runs from work done to a client's act (measurement agreed, certificate) and a Univastu act (bill prepared, submitted). Which of the two holds each rupee, on which site, is not in the accounts. Measure instead: per running bill, the dates executed, jointly measured, prepared, submitted, acknowledged, certified, and invoiced.
Crestline shows (read-only, once an adapter for Quadra exists): the waterfall from work done to bill, each wait tagged ours or the client's, ranked by rupee-days. Scenario on contract asset days only: 10 days less is 6.67 Cr, 30 days less is 20.00 Cr, once, never added (calc unchanged).
Framing: strong execution is what builds this balance; the question is how fast each bill is certified and raised, never whether the work was done.

## Problem 2. Bills raised and not yet paid (stage: collection). Owner: the client's act (pay); the chase and the invoice quality are Univastu's.
Figures (note 12, consolidated): current trade receivables 80.63 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 from 7.23 Cr a year earlier. The ageing schedule is drawn "from due date of payment" for each bill, with nothing shown as not yet due. Printed as balances only; no days computed (ruling UV-2, B6).
Mechanism: the source ties the rise to the year's billing ("acceleration in project execution and billing", MD message) and the company's stated mitigation is its choice of centrally and multilaterally funded projects. Who is late, on which invoice, against which contract term, is not published; the credit period given to each client is NOT FOUND. Measure instead: per invoice, due date against payment date, by client and by site, against each client's own term.
Crestline shows: open invoices by client against that client's own due date; the owner of each wait (client's payment, or Univastu's follow-up).
Framing: the company states how it protects itself; the question is where, inside that protection, a bill sits.

## Problem 3. Money held back until later (stage: retention and long-dated receivables). Owner: the client's act (release).
Figures (note 7, consolidated): non-current trade receivables 25.91 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 from 20.85 Cr, all shown as not yet due. The accounting policy says "the amount of retention money held by the customers is disclosed as part of other trade receivables". The AR does not say that this balance is retention, and publishes no retention amount.
Mechanism: not stated. Measure instead: retention held per bill and per contract, the release condition and the release date, so the 25.91 Cr can be split into what has a date and what has none.
Crestline shows: retention held per project with its release date, kept apart from open invoices.
Framing: a balance that is not yet due is not a delay; the question is whether each part of it has a date anybody is tracking.

## Problem 4. Vendor bills not yet on the ledger, and vendor dues past their own dates (stage: vendor). Owner: Univastu (receive, match, pay) and the vendor (bill).
Figures (note 28, consolidated): current trade payables 152.00 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 from 43.08 Cr. Of these, 99.42 Cr are listed by the company as "unbilled payables" (20.33 Cr a year earlier). Non-current trade payables 9.61 Cr (6.84 Cr), all shown as not yet due. Dues to micro and small enterprises: principal 19.84 lakh (23.97 lakh), with 0.54 lakh of interest due, note 39.02. Ageing from due date of payment, same note: 28.42 Cr is more than one year past its due date (1,575.81 + 117.17 + 1,149.01 lakh; Crestline's arithmetic on the published accounts, one table). That last figure is internal only, not for print.
Mechanism: the source does not define "unbilled payables" beyond the label, and states no vendor credit terms (the CFO's spoken "90 days" cannot be reconciled with this table, so no payable days are printed). Measure instead: per vendor, the date goods were received or work certified, the date the vendor's bill arrived, the date it was matched, the due date under that vendor's terms, and the date paid.
Crestline shows: vendor bills by stage (received, billed, matched, due, paid) against each vendor's own due date, and MSME dues against the statutory limit. Paying vendors later is never shown as a saving.
Framing: trade payables rose by 108.83 Cr in the year's cash flow statement, so vendors are part of this year's story; the question is whether each vendor knows the date it will be paid and whether Univastu knows which bill has not arrived.

## Problem 5. Money paid ahead of material (stage: vendor advances and stock). Owner: Univastu (the order) and the vendor (delivery).
Figures: advances to suppliers of goods 4.97 Cr (current, note 16; 5.00 Cr a year earlier) and advances to other suppliers 1.97 Cr (non-current, note 10; unchanged); together 6.95 Cr (Crestline's arithmetic, same date and kind). Stock held is work in progress of 2.48 Cr (note 11), against purchases of materials in the year of 73.73 Cr (note 34).
Mechanism: not stated. The advance balance barely moved while payables and purchases grew. Measure instead: each advance against its purchase order, the delivery date promised, the goods-received date, and the age of the advance.
Crestline shows: advances by vendor and order, with the days since paid and what has been received against them.
Framing: an advance is a normal way to secure material on a fast-growing job; the question is which orders it sits against and since when.

## Problem 6. A year in which payables and receivables both grew (stage: cash). Owner: both.
Figures (cash flow statement, consolidated): net cash from operating activities 11.99 Cr in FY26 against an outflow of 5.66 Cr in FY25, from cash generated from operations of 21.64 Cr less 9.65 Cr of tax paid. In the same statement, FY26 lines: trade payables increased 108.83 Cr; current trade receivables increased 70.51 Cr; other current assets increased 51.05 Cr; non-current trade receivables increased 5.06 Cr.
Mechanism: the statement shows the lines and not the reasons. It is an annual statement, so the deepest point of cash inside the year is not visible. Measure instead: month by month, cash in against cash out by project, and the deepest cumulative point (peak funding need), which no balance sheet shows.
Crestline shows: the cash movement panel with net, cumulative position and peak funding need, per project, from the source's own dates.
Framing: operating cash turned positive in the year the business grew 42%; the question is how steady that is month to month as the order book is executed.

## Problem 7. Many sites, each with its own bill clock (stage: billing across projects). Owner: both.
Figures: order book approximately 1,854 Cr (company figure, MD message), "diversified across metro and urban infrastructure, sports infrastructure, institutional and government projects, sustainable construction and specialised civil works"; projects named in the report include sports complexes at Karad, Shillong and Jalgaon and electrical and mechanical works on Mumbai Metro Line 6 and Line 4; states shown: Maharashtra, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Chandigarh, Goa. Average headcount 143. The MD lists "execution delays" and "working-capital requirements" among the risks.
Mechanism: each client and package has its own milestone and certification routine; the accounts total them. Whether one package or a few hold most of the waiting money is not published (client concentration, order book split in numbers and number of projects are NOT FOUND). Measure instead: for each project, the same bill stages and the same owners, so projects can be compared on the gap to their own terms.
Crestline shows: the project grid with each job's own waits and owners, never a peer average.
Framing: a wide, growing book is a strength; the question is which packages certify quickly and why.

## Problem 8. A year end holds no waits (stage: all). Owner: both.
A balance sheet is a day, not a clock. The wait between two recorded steps is in Quadra's dates, not in the report. Crestline would measure it, read-only, once an adapter is built.
