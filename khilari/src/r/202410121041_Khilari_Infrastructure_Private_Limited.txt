Rationale and key rating drivers 
Ratings assigned to bank facilities of Khilari Infrastructure Private Limited (KIPL) derive strengths from experienced promoters in 
engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contracts of wastewater treatment and solid waste management plants, sizable 
and diversified order book position in over nine states of India. Ratings also derive strength from satisfactory financial risk profile 
marked by healthy profit margins, adequate liquidity position, comfortable debt coverage indicators and capital structure despite 
diversion of funds in form of investments and corporate guarantee extended for bank finance to its subsidiary Kinvan Private 
Limited (KPL). 
Rating strengths are tempered by moderate scale of operations, diversion of funds to unrelated business segment in 
pharmaceutical industry and presence in highly competitive industry due to the fragmented and tender-driven business, exposure 
to delays in project execution due to regulatory hurdles and attendant risks.  
 
Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions. 
Positive factors 
• 
Growing scale of operations marked by total operating income (TOI) exceeding ₹800 crore and successful execution of 
orders on a sustained basis. 
• 
Sustaining profitability marked by profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation, and taxation (PBILDT) margin above 
20% on a sustained basis.  
• 
Timely recovery of funds diverted to its group entity as envisaged on a sustained basis. 
 
Negative factors 
• 
Deteriorating capital structure marked by adjusted overall gearing above 1x on sustained basis. 
• 
Deteriorating debt coverage indicators marked by interest coverage below 10x on a sustained basis. 
• 
Elongating gross current asset period exceeding 180 days leading to deteriorating liquidity profile on a sustained basis. 
 
Analytical approach: Standalone 
Analytical team has considered standalone approach for the rating, factoring financial support given by investments and corporate 
guarantee extended for bank finance to its subsidiary KPL. 
 
Outlook: Stable.  
CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) expects to sustain its operating and financial performance on the back of healthy order 
book position supported by adequate liquidity despite considerable diversion of funds to its subsidiary i.e. KPL. The financial risk 
profile expected to remain comfortable due to lower reliance on the external debt with no debt funded capex and no significant 
diversion of fund envisaged in the near to medium terms. 
 
Detailed description of key rating drivers 
Key strengths 
Experienced promoters in construction industry 
KIPL is promoted by Khilari family with Sahebrao Khilari, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer (CEO), who is B.E. Civil and has 
over three decades of experience in sanitary infrastructure segments, which involves water treatment plants and solid waste 
management. Sahebrao Khilari is assisted by Anjali Khilari, director and Ayush Khilari, director, who is M.E. in Civil Engineering, 
having over 10 years of experience and looking after distinct business segments within KIPL and a team of professionals having 
technical expertise and significant experience in construction field. Having successfully executed numerous government contracts, 
KIPL has gained reputation and has established strong relationship with its clients and government agencies.

Sizable and diversified order book position 
KIPL has demonstrated its project execution capability in mid-and-large-size projects ranging from ₹30-840 crore in the last few 
years for total value of ₹1767.82 crore. As on June 30, 2024, KIPL has an unexecuted order book of ₹2,681 crore (4.39x of the 
total income for FY24 (Provisional) to be executed over 1-3 years. Moreover, orderbook is diversified in nine states in India under 
AMRUT, Namame Gange Programmes and World bank, German Bank funded projects. The order book position inclined towards 
Maharashtra and Rajasthan states due to sizable projects awarded in these states. The company also has pipelined order of ₹338 
crore, for which, company is eligible as L1 bidder. However, of the total order book, orders worth ₹425.01 crore are slow moving, 
mainly due to approval issues form respected authorities. Ratings also take a note majority order book being in nascent stages 
of execution. Hence, timely approvals and subsequent execution remains a key monitorable.  
 
Healthy profit margins 
KIPL’s PBILDT margin remained healthy and in the range of 20-23% for the last four years ending FY24, mainly considering 
savings in cost of major raw material (cement, steel, electromechanical material) due to direct sourcing from OEMs mainly on 
cash basis. The company has sizable portion of revenue coming from operations & maintenance of projects executed in the past, 
which are high profit margin income. 
In FY24, PBILDT margin improved to 22.15% compared to 20.34% in FY23 is mainly considering decline in cost of raw material 
consumed (RMC) to 29% in FY24 compared to 38% in FY23 and decline in other expenses in FY24. RMC cost defers across stages 
of the order execution, leading to variations. 
 
However, PAT margin declined to 18.21% in FY24 compared to 19.81% in FY23 mainly considering decrease in non-operating 
income to ₹23.88 crore in FY24 from ₹42.72 crore in FY23. 
In Q1FY25, PBILDT margin and PAT margin remained at 22.67% and 16.57% respectively. Profitability margins are expected to 
remain healthy from higher execution of orders expected in the near-to-medium terms.  
 
Healthy financial risk profile  
KIPL’s capital structure marked by overall gearing remains comfortable and in the range of 0.03x to 0.16x from FY24 considering 
sizable tangible net worth base and low reliance on external borrowings. Comfort can be drawn from the fact that fund-based 
limit in form of overdraft facility of ₹44.90 crore is fully backed by fixed deposits. The company’s overall gearing remained 
comfortable at 0.09x as on March 31, 2024, compared to 0.08x as on March 31, 2024, considering lower utilisation on working 
capital bank borrowing as on March 31, 2024, and repayment of term loan in FY24. 
KIPL has diverted its funds towards a subsidiary, where it has invested ₹208.76 crore and corporate guarantee extended to bank 
facilities of ₹135.70 crore outstanding as on March 31, 2024. However, post deduction of these amounts from tangible net worth, 
KPIL’s adjusted overall gearing deteriorated despite remained comfortable at 0.61x compared to 0.22x as on March 31, 2023. 
KIPL has proposed additional support in the form of unsecured loans of ₹54.58 crore in FY25, despite further diversion of funds. 
Going forward adjusted overall gearing is expected to remain at comfortable level. 
Due to its lower reliance on external debt and higher cash accruals, debt coverage indicators remained healthy with the total debt 
to gross cash accruals (TD/GCA) remained at 0.39x in FY24 compared to 0.24x in FY23. PBILDT interest coverage ratio also 
remained healthy, however, deteriorated to 29.16x in FY24 compared to 56.03x in FY23 due to increase in interest cost in FY24. 
Absolute GCA stood at ₹114.52 crore in FY24 (against ₹129.45 crore in FY23). 
 
 
Key weaknesses 
Moderate scale of operations  
KIPL’s TOI grew and remained in the range of ₹600 -640 crore from FY22-FY24 against ₹200-250 crore of TOI till FY21. This 
growth in TOI was mainly attributable due to bidding for high value contracts undertaken in the last, which resulted to higher 
execution of the orders. In FY24, TOI moderated to ₹610.85 crore against ₹638.08 crore in FY24 considering slower order 
execution and billings done by authorities due to general assembly elections. In Q1FY25, it posted total income of ₹97.06 crore. 
The company expects its TOI to increase further from healthy order book position. 
 
Diversion of funds to unrelated business segment 
KIPL has diverted its funds towards unrelated business segment in pharmaceutical industry and formed an entity under Joint 
Venture KPL engaged in manufacturing drug intermediary Clavulanic acid used in anti-infectious drugs. KIPL has invested ₹208.76 
crore by way of preference shares of ₹156.50 crore and extended unsecured loan ₹52.26 crore till March 31, 2024. KPIL has also 
extended corporate guarantee to bank facilities of KPL amounting to ₹135.70 crore outstanding as on March 31, 2024 (out of 
total sanctioned term loan of ₹140 crore).  
 
KIPL has proposed additional support in the form of unsecured loans of ₹54.58 crore in KPL totalling to ₹263.34 crore in FY25 (as 
against ₹208.76 crore in FY24). Hence, timely recovery of the given funds remains key monitorable from KIPL’s credit perspective.  
 
Presence in competitive industry due to the fragmented and tender-driven business 
The EPC industry is fragmented with a large number of medium and large-scale players present at regional and national level. 
Players in civil construction are also now competing for orders by signing joint ventures (JVs) with small players in the water 
treatment industry, increasing competition. Projects in the industry are awarded based on the bidder’s relevant experience,

Liquidity: Adequate 
Liquidity remained adequate characterised by sufficient cushion in gross accruals against its debt repayment obligations of ₹0.40 
crore for FY25. The free cash & bank balance stood at ₹43.33 crore as on March 31, 2024 (against ₹59.79 crore as on March 31, 
2023). The current ratio remained at 1.38x as on March 31, 2024, compared to 1.73x as on March 31, 2023. 
KIPL’s operating cycle remained meagre at 12 days in FY24 compared to negative operating cycle in FY23. The collection period 
remains of around one-month post invoice is raised, therefore it remained at 39 days in FY24 compared to 26 days in FY23. It 
holds minimal inventory at year-end leading to inventory period of 18 days compared to 11 days in FY23. Working capital 
requirement is partially offset by creditors period of 45 days in FY24 compared to 53 days in FY23, therefore operating cycle 
remained lean to 12 days in FY24. Creditors primarily comprised towards the company’s sub-contractors. Gross current asset 
period remained high at 236 days in FY24 compared to 167 days in FY23 mainly due to funds blocked in lien marked FDs which 
has increased to ₹191.97 crore as on March 31, 2024, against ₹125.11 crore as on March 31, 2023. 
Average utilisation of working capital limits of ₹44.90 crore stood low at ~44.80% and average of maximum utilisation remained 
at 71.96% for 12-months ended August 2024. Bank guarantee limit of ₹220 crore remains fully utilised in 12-months ended 
August 2024. Fund-based and non-fund-based limits remained fully backed by FDs, providing cushion to its liquidity. Cash flow 
from operating activities stood positive of ₹135.10 crore in FY24 (against positive ₹18.29 crore in FY23).

About us:  
Established in 1993, CARE Ratings is one of the leading credit rating agencies in India. Registered under the Securities and
