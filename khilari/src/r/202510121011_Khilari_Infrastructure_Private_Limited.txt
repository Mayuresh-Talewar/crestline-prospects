Rationale and key rating drivers 
Reaffirmation in ratings assigned to bank facilities of Khilari Infrastructure Private Limited (KIPL) continues to derive strengths 
from promoters’ extensive experience in executing engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contracts for wastewater 
treatment and solid waste management projects. Ratings are further supported by a sizable and geographically diversified order 
book spanning nine states across India. The company maintains a satisfactory financial risk profile characterised by healthy profit 
margins, strong debt coverage metrics, adequate liquidity, and comfortable capital structure despite the diversion of funds through 
investments and corporate guarantees extended to its subsidiary, Kinvan Private Limited (KPL). 
However, these strengths are partially offset by KIPL’s moderate scale of operations, allocation of funds to an unrelated 
pharmaceutical business segment, and its presence in a highly competitive and fragmented industry that is largely tender driven. 
The company also exposed to risks associated with potential delays in project execution due to regulatory challenges. 
 
Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions 
Positive factors 
• 
Growing scale of operations marked by total operating income (TOI) exceeding ₹800 crore and successful execution of 
orders on a sustained basis. 
• 
Improvement in profitability marked by profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation, and taxation (PBILDT) margin 
above 20% on a sustained basis. 
• 
Timely recovery of funds diverted to its group entity as envisaged on a sustained basis. 
 
Negative factors  
 
• 
Deteriorating capital structure marked by adjusted overall gearing above 1x on a sustained basis.  
• 
Deteriorating debt coverage indicators marked by interest coverage below 10x on a sustained basis.  
• 
Significant reduction in the scale of operations or profitability leading to deterioration in liquidity position on a sustained 
basis. 
 
Analytical approach: Standalone 
Standalone approach has been considered for ratings, factoring financial support given by KIPL in the form of investments and 
corporate guarantee extended for bank finance to its subsidiary, KPL. 
 
Outlook: Stable 
CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) expects to sustain its operating and financial performances on the back of healthy order 
book position supported by adequate liquidity despite considerable diversion of funds to its subsidiary. The financial risk profile is 
expected to remain comfortable due to lower reliance on the external debt with no debt-funded capex and no significant diversion 
of fund envisaged in the near-to-medium term. 
 
Detailed description of key rating drivers: 
 
Key strengths 
Experienced promoters in construction industry 
KIPL is promoted by the Khilari family, with Sahebrao Khilari serving as the Chairman and Chief Executive Officer (CEO). He is 
B.E. Civil and has over three decades of experience in sanitary infrastructure segments, involving water treatment plants and 
solid waste management. He is assisted by Anjali Khilari, Director, and Ayush Khilari, Director, who is M.E. in Civil Engineering, 
having over a decade of experience and looking after distinct business segments within KIPL, and a team of professionals having 
technical expertise and significant experience in construction field. Having successfully executed numerous government contracts, 
KIPL has gained reputation and has established strong relationship with its clients and government agencies.

Sizable and diversified order book position 
The company has continued to secure strong order book position of ₹1,878.72 crore as on June 30, 2025 (against ₹2,681 crore 
as on June 30, 2023), which is 2.93x of net sales for FY25 and the same to be executed in span of two-three years depending on 
the nature work to be executed, providing medium-term revenue visibility to the company. The decline in the order book in FY25 
is primarily attributed to the mobilisation of newly awarded projects in Ahmedabad and Uttarakhand. As Uttarakhand is the new 
geography for the company, the focus in the previous year was on resource mobilisation and groundwork.  
 
Geographically, the order book is moderately diversified, with projects spread across multiple states, including Maharashtra, 
Rajasthan, Uttarakhand, Gujarat, West Bengal, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, and Andhra Pradesh. The counterparty 
risk remains low, as the entire order book comprises contracts awarded by government entities, including urban local bodies. Of 
the total order book, projects worth ₹196.37 crore are currently slow-moving, primarily due to pending approvals from relevant 
authorities. Ratings also factor in that majority order book is in the early stages of execution. Therefore, timely receipt of approvals 
and subsequent execution progress remain key monitorable. 
 
Healthy profit margins 
KIPL’s PBILDT margin declined from 21.52% in FY24 to 15.43% in FY25 considering many of the projects under initial stage of 
execution in FY25 and operational expenses are generally higher in the initial phase of the projects which led to increase in the 
cost of raw material consumed and operational cost during the year. The material cost (steel and cement mainly) increased from 
31% in FY24 to 36% in FY25, similarly the labour and employee cost also increased y-o-y impacted the PBILDT margin. The profit 
after tax (PAT) margin also declined from 17.93% in FY24 to 14.08% in FY25 in line with PBILDT margin. Despite the said decline, 
profit margins continued to remain healthy. Hence, profitability margins remain susceptible to fluctuations in input prices and 
stage of completion of the orderbook. CareEdge Ratings expects margins to stabilise and improve marginally over the medium 
term with progress of the execution of ongoing orders. 
 
Healthy financial risk profile despite sizable exposure to subsidiaries and affiliated companies 
The company’s overall gearing remained comfortable at 0.15x as on March 31, 2025, compared to 0.09x as on March 31, 2024. 
The deterioration on gearing was primarily driven by higher mobilisation advances and higher utilisation of working capital during 
the year. The company secured over ₹1,050 crore of new orders from the Uttarakhand and Gujarat regions in FY24-FY25, which 
led to a rise in mobilisation advances availed from customers. Working capital utilisation increased as project-related expenses 
tend to be higher in the initial phases of execution.   
 
KIPL has diverted its funds to a subsidiary and affiliated parties, investing ₹322.04 crore and extending corporate guarantee to 
bank facilities of ₹135.70 crore outstanding as on March 31, 2025 (PY: ₹232.58 and ₹135.70), respectively. However, post 
deduction of these amounts from tangible net worth, KIPL’s adjusted overall gearing deteriorated, despite remaining comfortable 
at 0.83x as on March 31, 2025 (PY: 0.67x). Going forward, adjusted overall gearing is expected to remain at comfortable level 
considering no further fund diversion expected in near future and accretion of profits to reserve. Recovery of these loans in 
coming years would remain key monitorable. 
 
Due to its lower reliance on external debt and higher cash accruals, debt coverage indicators remained healthy with the total debt 
to gross cash accruals (TD/GCA) remained at 0.95x in FY25 compared to 0.39x in FY24. PBILDT interest coverage also remained 
healthy, though, moderated to 23.93x in FY25 compared to 28.33x in FY24 due to reduction in profitability in FY25. 
 
Key weaknesses 
Moderate scale of operations  
The company’s overall scale remained moderate with TOI stood in the range of ₹610 crore to ₹640 crore in FY22 to FY25. TOI 
grew by 5%, reaching ₹639.86 crore in FY25 (against ₹610.85 crore in FY24), supported by steady execution of contracts. in 
4MFY26, the company reported revenue of ₹210.79 crore, significantly higher than ₹97.06 crore in Q1FY25. This y-o-y increase 
in Q1FY26 was primarily driven by unbilled revenue of ₹71 crore in FY25, which included spillover billing from completed contracts 
and billing recognised in the current year. The company experienced slower execution in FY25, as it ventured into Uttarakhand 
for the first time. However, with resource mobilisation complete, execution is expected to regain momentum. Given these 
developments, the company is expected to sustained growth, and its revenue is projected to rise in the near-to-medium term.  
 
Diversion of funds to unrelated business segment 
KIPL has diverted its funds towards unrelated business segment in pharmaceutical industry and formed an entity under joint 
venture, KPL, which is engaged in manufacturing drug intermediary, Clavulanic acid, used in anti-infectious drugs. KIPL has 
invested ₹296.83 crore (PY: ₹208.76 crore) by way of equity shares of ₹8.70 crore, preference shares of ₹156.50 crore, and 
extended unsecured loan ₹131.63 crore till March 31, 2025. The increased funding towards subsidiary in FY25 due to increase in 
scope of work for capex of KPL. KIPL invested ₹25.19 crore in other investments in FY25 (PY: ₹23.81 crore), resulting in total 
fund diversion of ₹322.02 crore in FY25 (PY: ₹232.58 crore). KIPL has also extended corporate guarantee to KPL’s bank facilities 
amounting to ₹135.70 crore outstanding as on March 31, 2025 (out of total sanctioned term loan of ₹140 crore). As informed by 
the management, no further fund diversion is expected in the near future. Nevertheless, timely recovery of the given funds 
remains key monitorable from KIPL’s credit perspective.  
 
Presence in competitive industry due to the fragmented and tender-driven business

The EPC industry is fragmented with a large number of medium and large-scale players present at regional and national levels. 
Players in civil construction are also now competing for orders by signing JVs with small players in the water treatment industry, 
increasing competition. Projects in the industry are awarded based on the bidder’s relevant experience, financial capability, and 
most attractive bid price. KIPL competes with some large established infrastructure players in this space, increasing the overall 
competition in the segment. 
 
Liquidity: Adequate 
Liquidity remained adequate characterised by sufficient cushion in gross accruals against its debt repayment obligations of ₹0.03 
crore for FY26. The free cash and bank balance stood at ₹25.24 crore as on March 31, 2025 (against ₹43.10 crore as on March 
31, 2024). The current ratio remained at 1.20x as on March 31, 2025, compared to 1.30x as on March 31, 2024. Maximum 
average utilisation of working capital limits of ₹44.90 crore stood high at ~91.40% (PY: 71.96%) for 12-months ended June 2025. 
Maximum average utilisation of bank guarantee limit of ₹300 crore remained at 84.69% for 12 months ended on June 2025. 
Fund-based and non-fund-based limits remained fully backed by fixed deposits (FDs), providing cushion to its liquidity. Cash flow 
from operating activities stood negative of ₹4.66 crore in FY25 (against positive ₹100 crore in FY24) considering unfavourable 
working capital changes. 
 
KIPL’s operating cycle elongated in FY25, though remained low at 44 days in FY25 compared to eight days in FY24. The elongation 
in operating cycle was mainly due to increase in inventory period from 17 days in FY24 to 32 days in FY25 due to increase in 
amount of unbilled revenue. The collection period increased to 51 days in FY25 from 33 days in FY24 due to increase in security 
deposit kept by customers against order execution. The creditors’ period stood at 39 days (PY: 42 days). Creditors primarily 
comprised towards the company’s sub-contractors. Gross current asset period remained high at 266 days in FY25 compared to 
220 days in FY24 mainly due to increase in debtors and inventory and investments and loans and advances extended to the JV / 
subsidiary.

India, and has a registered office in Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra.
