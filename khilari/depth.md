## 1. The rationales' own words on working capital, the operating cycle and liquidity

CARE, 06 Oct 2025 (C25), page 3, section "Liquidity: Adequate" (image-checked):
- "KIPL's operating cycle elongated in FY25, though remained low at 44 days" (FY24: eight days).
- Inventory period 17 to 32 days, attributed to an increase in the amount of unbilled revenue.
- Collection period 33 to 51 days, "due to increase in security deposit kept by customers against order execution".
- Creditors' period 39 days (PY 42), mostly sub-contractors.
- "Gross current asset period remained high at 266 days in FY25" (FY24: 220), attributed to debtors, inventory, and investments and loans to the JV / subsidiary.
- Maximum average utilisation of the Rs 44.90 crore working-capital limit "stood high at ~91.40%" (PY 71.96%), 12 months to Jun 2025.
- Cash flow from operations negative Rs 4.66 crore in FY25 "considering unfavourable working capital changes".

CARE, 06 Oct 2025 (C25), page 2 (image-checked): gearing rose to 0.15x "primarily driven by higher mobilisation advances and higher utilisation of working capital"; working-capital use rises "in the initial phases of execution".

CARE, 04 Oct 2024 (C24), page 3, "Liquidity: Adequate" (image-checked):
- "KIPL's operating cycle remained meagre at 12 days in FY24" against a negative cycle in FY23.
- Collection period "remains of around one-month post invoice is raised": 39 days FY24, 26 days FY23.
- Inventory 18 days (FY23: 11); creditors 45 days (FY23: 53).
- Gross current asset period 236 days (FY23: 167), mainly "due to funds blocked in lien marked FDs" (Rs 191.97 crore against Rs 125.11 crore).
- Bank guarantee limit of Rs 220 crore "remains fully utilised".

CRISIL, 24 Jan 2017 (CR17), page 3 (image-checked): the weakness is headed "Working Capital Intensive operations": GCA 114 days in FY16, debtors 43 days, and "high security deposits and retention money".

Brickwork, 15 Dec 2020 (B20), page 3 (image-checked): liquidity "Adequate", overdraft against FDs at 39% and current ratio 1.43x at 30 Sep 2020.

What the words add up to (analysis, not a quote): every agency that wrote about working capital named deposits or retention held by the client as the thing that ties up cash, nine years apart (CR17 2017, C25 2025). The FY25 stretch came from the two client-side waits (unbilled work, deposits held by customers), not from paying suppliers faster: creditor days barely moved (42 to 39).

Basis warning for anyone charting utilisation: C24 prints an AVERAGE utilisation (44.80%) and an AVERAGE OF MAXIMUM (71.96%); C25 prints a MAXIMUM AVERAGE (91.40%) and gives 71.96% as its prior year. Only 71.96% to 91.40% is like for like. Never chart 44.80% against 91.40%.

## 2. Rating sensitivities and triggers

CARE, 06 Oct 2025 (C25 p1, image-checked)
- Up: TOI above Rs 800 crore with sustained execution; PBILDT margin above 20% sustained; timely recovery of funds diverted to the group entity.
- Down: adjusted overall gearing above 1x; interest coverage below 10x; significant fall in scale or profitability hurting liquidity.

CARE, 04 Oct 2024 (C24 p1, image-checked)
- Up: same three (the margin line reads "Sustaining" rather than "Improvement").
- Down: gearing above 1x; interest coverage below 10x; and "Elongating gross current asset period exceeding 180 days".

Where the company sits against each trigger (Computed or as printed):

|---|---|---|---|

| PBILDT margin (up) | above 20% | 15.43% FY25; 17.0% 4MFY26 | below; was above in FY23 and FY24 |

The last row is the sharpest fact in the file: in Oct 2024 CARE named GCA above 180 days as a downgrade trigger while printing 236 days on page 3 of the same release; a year later GCA was 266 days and the trigger was no longer listed. A reader should not infer a reason; the release gives none.

Outlook triggers in older rationales: B20 p2 (positive: growth with better margins, prudent working-capital management; negative: fall in revenue or profit, weaker capital structure or liquidity); CR17 p3 (negative if accruals fall and stretch the working-capital cycle, or debt-funded capex).
