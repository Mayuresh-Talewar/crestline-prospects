# Khilari Infrastructure: problems (KH-3, QA fixes KH-4)

The story, in one line: a business that stepped up in scale, holds a full government order book, and pays its suppliers much as it always did, while three years of its own working capital days moved one way: the inventory period and the collection period rose, and supplier credit did not. The question is not whether anything went wrong. It is whether the view of where each bill waits has kept pace with a book spread over nine states and many departments.

Basis for every figure: Khilari Infrastructure Private Limited, standalone, as CARE Ratings states it.
- FY23: CARE press release 04 Oct 2024, page 3 (audited; the only vintage for FY23).
- FY24 and FY25: CARE press release 06 Oct 2025, page 3. FY24 audited, FY25 UNAUDITED in that release.
- The FY24 figures in the 04 Oct 2024 release are an earlier unaudited vintage and are never used beside these.
- Context: total operating income Rs 639.86 crore in FY25, unaudited; order book Rs 1,878.72 crore at 30 Jun 2025, 2.93x FY25 net sales; entire order book from government entities, including urban local bodies (CARE 06 Oct 2025 p2, p3).

## Problem 1. Bills wait longer to turn into cash
| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 (unaudited) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Collection period, days | 26 | 33 | 51 |

Source: CARE 04 Oct 2024 p3 (FY23); CARE 06 Oct 2025 p3 (FY24, FY25). Standalone.

CARE's reason, in its words: the rise was "due to increase in security deposit kept by customers against order execution" (06 Oct 2025 p3).

Respectful reading: deposits held against execution are part of the contracts this company wins, and a full government book brings more of them. CARE does not describe this as late payment. But 18 more days of collection in one year, valued at Rs 1.753 crore a day of FY25 income, is a large sum waiting with clients. CARE does not state the basis of its collection period, so valuing a day at income per day is our approximation, not CARE's.

What the rating report cannot tell:
- which state and which department or municipal body is holding the money;
- which bills, and how much is deposit against how much is a payment simply not yet made;
- which deposits have already met the milestone that releases them;
- whose desk is following each one up, and since when.

## Problem 2. The inventory period nearly doubled, and CARE ties the rise to unbilled revenue
| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 (unaudited) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inventory period, days | 11 | 17 | 32 |

Source: CARE 04 Oct 2024 p3 (FY23); CARE 06 Oct 2025 p3 (FY24, FY25). Standalone.

CARE's reason, in its words: the inventory period rose from 17 days in FY24 to 32 days in FY25 "due to increase in amount of unbilled revenue" (06 Oct 2025 p3). CARE gives this as the reason for the rise; it does not say how much of the inventory period is unbilled work. Elsewhere it puts unbilled revenue at Rs 71 crore in FY25 and says that sum drove the rise in revenue early in FY26 (06 Oct 2025 p2).

Respectful reading: CARE notes that working capital use rises "in the initial phases of execution" (06 Oct 2025 p2), and this company was mobilising new projects. The point is that the unbilled part of this wait is the part the company controls most: measuring, preparing, reviewing and submitting a bill are its own acts; acknowledging and certifying it are the department's.

What the rating report cannot tell:
- how much of the inventory period is unbilled work and how much is material on site;
- which project, and which stage the work is waiting at: measurement, bill preparation, submission, or the department's certification;
- how much of the wait is the company's own and how much is the client's;
- which site engineer or billing desk holds the next move.

## Problem 3. The cycle grew on the inventory and collection side, not the supplier side
| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 (unaudited) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cycle, days | below zero (CARE: "negative") | 8 | 44 |
| Creditors period, days | 53 | 42 | 39 |
| Gross current asset days (context only) | 167 | 220 | 266 |

Source: CARE 04 Oct 2024 p3 (FY23); CARE 06 Oct 2025 p3 (FY24, FY25). Standalone. CARE prints FY23's cycle only as "negative", and it is written here in words, with no number. Inventory plus collection less creditors reproduces CARE's printed 8 and 44.

Respectful reading: the company did not lean on its suppliers. Creditor days barely moved (42 to 39). Of the 36 extra days, 33 came from the inventory period (up 15) and the collection period (up 18), and 3 from creditor days falling. CARE still calls the cycle "low at 44 days" (06 Oct 2025 p3). In the 12 months to June 2025 the maximum average use of the Rs 44.90 crore working capital limits stood at about 91.40%, against 71.96% the year before (CARE 06 Oct 2025 p3, like for like).

Gross current asset days are shown for context only. Gross current assets also hold balances outside the billing cycle, so this line is never priced.

What the rating report cannot tell:
- which projects carry the cycle and which fund themselves;
- for each project, the to scale split of days into stock, unbilled work, bills awaiting payment and supplier credit;
- whether suppliers are being paid on their own due dates, early or late, vendor by vendor.
