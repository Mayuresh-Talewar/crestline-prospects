# Bajel Projects: problems (BJ-3, QA fixes BJ-4)

Basis for every figure: Bajel Projects Limited, **STANDALONE**, Rs Cr. Charted figures are from the Annual Report FY2025-26 (AR26, filed on NSE 17 Jul 2026), which prints FY26 with FY25 beside it. Earlier years appear in words only, each with its own report and filing date. Sources and grades are in data.md and depth.md; days and shares are from src/bj-calc-output.txt.

## Where the story starts: the CFO's own account of FY26

Receivables rose from Rs 1,258.65 Cr to Rs 1,849.05 Cr in FY26 (AR26 p256). On the company's first earnings call (29 May 2026, transcript filed 3 Jun 2026), Nitesh Bhandari, Chief Financial Officer, explained the rise himself: higher billing in the last quarter (about Rs 194 Cr), year-end timing of collections, and retention that "keeps increasing on for every project, that's the contractual terms" (about Rs 100 Cr). He said "till date we have collected most part of it". CRISIL, in its rationale of 5 Aug 2026, called the year-end debtor position "a one-off event". Money overdue fell, from Rs 448.34 Cr to Rs 399.35 Cr (AR26 p280).

So this is not a story about a bad year. It is about the structure underneath a business that more than doubled: how much of the cash is waiting by contract, how much is waiting on certification, and which part could move sooner.

## P1. Receivables sit near 200 days of revenue, even in a normal year

**The figure.** Trade receivables turnover, the company's own ratio (revenue including other operating income over average receivables), was 2.61 in FY25 and 1.80 in FY26: **139.8 and 202.8 days** (AR26 p320 Note 45, filed 17 Jul 2026, standalone; days = 365 / turnover). The company's stated reason: "Decrease in trade receivables turnover ratio is on account of delay in collection from customers in comparison to projects execution."

**The company's own account, printed beside the 202.8 days.** Year-end timing of collections, largely collected after year end, per the company (Nitesh Bhandari, Chief Financial Officer, Q4 FY26 earnings call, 29 May 2026, transcript p14, filed 3 Jun 2026).

**The longer view, in words only.** FY24 was 182.5 days on the same definition (AR24 p112, filed 29 Jul 2024). FY25 was the lowest of the three years. In two of the three years the book held about half a year of revenue.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Which project and which utility hold the days. Which bills are past their own due date and which are inside it. How many of the days sit before the bill is certified and how many after. Whose desk each bill is waiting on.

## P2. Nearly four rupees in five of the book are "not due", and retention sits inside that

**The figure.** Gross receivables "not due" rose from **Rs 882.77 Cr (66.32%) to Rs 1,530.12 Cr (79.30%)**, while overdue fell from Rs 448.34 Cr to Rs 399.35 Cr (AR26 p280 Note 6, filed 17 Jul 2026, standalone). The note explains why: "retention amounts due after completion of projects is categorized as Not Due until the project is completed" (AR26 p281).

**The terms behind it.** Stated customer terms are now "generally on terms of 30 to 360 days" (AR26 p281), where the previous report said "30 to 90 days" (AR25 p214, filed 18 Jul 2025). Power Grid and its group were 64.31% of FY26 revenue (AR26 p312), so its certification sets much of the clock. In FY26 the revenue note deducts Rs 72.13 Cr from revenue at contract price as "unbilled on account of work under certification" (AR26 p316 Note 42).

**What the accounts cannot tell.** How much of the Rs 1,530.12 Cr is retention and how much is billed money inside its terms; no retention balance is disclosed in any of the three reports. Which project each retention line belongs to, which milestone or completion releases it, and when. Which bills are waiting on certification and for how long.

## P3. Cash from operations has not kept pace with the work

**The figure.** Net cash from operating activities was **Rs 69.13 Cr in FY25 and Rs 21.09 Cr in FY26**, while revenue went from Rs 2,598.24 Cr to Rs 2,791.58 Cr (AR26 p258 and p257, filed 17 Jul 2026, standalone). The cash flow statement shows trade receivables taking Rs 549.43 Cr and Rs 599.82 Cr in those two years (AR26 p258).

**The longer view, in words only.** FY24 operating cash was Rs 195.37 Cr on revenue of Rs 1,169.21 Cr (AR25 p190, filed 18 Jul 2025; AR24 p165, filed 29 Jul 2024). Revenue in FY26 was 2.39 times FY24.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Which projects consumed the cash and which returned it. The month the cumulative position was deepest, which is the facility the business really needed. How much was collected in the days after 31 Mar 2026, bill by bill.

## P4. Most of the borrowing is vendor bill discounting, at 6.5% to 10%

**The figure.** Borrowings were **Rs 349.96 Cr at 31 Mar 2026**, against Rs 356.59 Cr a year earlier (AR26 p291 Note 18, filed 17 Jul 2026, standalone). Most of it is vendor bill discounting: Rs 318.96 Cr of suppliers' bills settled through banks and financial institutions on credit terms of up to 180 days. Working capital facilities were Rs 31.00 Cr. The company states the interest on both in a range of 6.5% to 10% a year (AR26 p291). Interest on borrowings was Rs 46.36 Cr in FY26 and Rs 41.29 Cr in FY25 (AR26 p297 Note 29). Interest on mobilisation advances is a separate charge (Rs 15.20 Cr in FY26, AR26 p297) and is outside this range.

**Credit where due.** The CFO told the AGM on 10 Aug 2026 that the average rate had come down from about 10% at the start of the year to "around 6.5 to 7% as of now" (AGM transcript p23). Q1 FY27 interest was Rs 11.8 Cr against Rs 15.9 Cr a year earlier (Q1 FY27 investor presentation p23, filed 7 Aug 2026). The company has brought the rate down. The number of rupees waiting to be collected is the part Crestline measures, and interest is saved only if cash released from receivables repays these facilities.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Which project's receivables the borrowing is funding. Whether discounting is drawn ahead of collections or after them. How many rupees of borrowing a given day of certification or collection would retire.
