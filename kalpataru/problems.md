# KPIL: working-capital problems, KP-3

Kalpataru Projects International Limited (NSE: KPIL, BSE: 522287). Prepared 8 Oct 2026.
Basis: STANDALONE throughout (the Indian EPC company), from the company's own filings. Rs = INR crore. Every computed figure is Crestline arithmetic on the published figures, run through the app's own functions; nothing here is typed by hand.
Documents and filing dates: AR FY26 filed 22 Jun 2026; AR FY25 filed 16 Jun 2025; AR FY23 filed 21 Jun 2023; Investor Presentation (IP) Q4 FY26 filed 14 May 2026; results and IP Q1 FY27 filed 11 Aug 2026; Q4 FY26 earnings call held 15 May 2026, transcript filed 20 May 2026; Q3 FY26 call held 5 Feb 2026, transcript filed 10 Feb 2026; CRISIL rationale dated 16 Oct 2025, filed 17 Oct 2025.

## How to read this

KPIL runs a large, profitable and growing order book, and it met the working-capital target it set itself for FY26: management guided "a net working capital cycle under 100 days" (Mr. Manish Mohnot, Managing Director and CEO, Q4 FY26 call p.2) and the standalone figure closed at 90 days (IP Q4 FY26 slide 21). India Ratings upgraded its bank-loan and debenture ratings to IND AA+/Stable (NSE filing, 3 Jul 2026). The problems below are therefore not about a company in difficulty. They are about how much cash sits between work done and cash received, and what the published accounts cannot show about where it sits.

Rules kept: one basis per chart; figures from different totals are never paired; payables days are never treated as better when higher (rule 1); ours against the client's follows the product rule (measure, prepare, review, submit and invoice are KPIL's acts; acknowledge, certify and collect are the client's).

## Problem 1. Receivables plus unbilled work hold 235.7 days of revenue

**What the filings show.** At 31 Mar 2026 the standalone balance sheet carries trade receivables of Rs 7,905.68 Cr and contract assets (work done, not yet billable) of Rs 7,083.61 Cr: together Rs 14,989.29 Cr, or 235.7 days of FY26 revenue of Rs 23,210.06 Cr (AR FY26 balance sheet p.280, Note 11 p.303, P&L p.281). Contract assets are 111.4 days and receivables 124.3 days of that.

**Trend (standalone, 31 March, same formula each year, revenue basis, our computation):**

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Receivables + contract assets, Rs Cr | 10,019.15 | 11,606.92 | 13,499.33 | 14,989.29 |
| Days of revenue | 255.1 | 252.8 | 260.9 | 235.7 |

Sources: FY23 AR FY23 p.246 and Note 11 p.265 (FY23 prints no non-current contract-asset line, so FY23 is slightly understated); FY24 AR FY25 p.234 and Note 11 p.255; FY25 and FY26 AR FY26 p.280 and Note 11 p.303.

FY26 is the best of the four years, down from 260.9 days in FY25. It is still about eight months of revenue in the O2C queue. The company's own contract wording explains part of the floor: "upto 20% of the contract value is received as an advance and upto 20% amount is retained and released by the customer at the end of project" (AR FY26 Note 11.4 p.303). CRISIL, on its group basis (not standalone), wrote that "Sizeable retention money remains blocked in projects till the end of the performance guarantee period" and that "improvement in working capital management remains a monitorable" (rationale 16 Oct 2025, p.4).

This 235.7 is our own formula and is never the company's figure. The company publishes its own working-capital days on a definition it does not state; the two are never shown together.

**Why it matters.** At Rs 63.59 Cr of revenue a day (AR FY26, revenue / 365), every day held in this queue ties up Rs 63.59 Cr of cash.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Which projects and which RA bills hold the Rs 7,083.61 Cr of contract assets; how much of it is still with KPIL (measured, not yet prepared or submitted) and how much is with the client (submitted, waiting to be certified); how much of the Rs 7,905.68 Cr is retention rather than overdue (retention is not separated in the notes; the only proxy is Rs 386.66 Cr of receivables falling due after 12 months, Note 43 p.336); since when each bill has waited; and whose desk it sits on.

## Problem 2. Receivables more than a year past due rose to 16.1% of the book

**What the filings show.** Gross trade receivables more than one year past their due date were Rs 1,311.12 Cr of Rs 8,162.44 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 (AR FY26 Note 42 p.334, undisputed and disputed together).

**Trend (standalone, ageing by due date, gross, 31 March):**

| | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| More than 1 year past due, Rs Cr | 767.91 | 961.06 | 1,311.12 |
| Total receivables in the ageing table, Rs Cr | 5,701.22 | 7,454.91 | 8,162.44 |
| Share more than 1 year | 13.5% | 12.9% | 16.1% |

Sources: FY24 and FY25 AR FY25 Note 42 p.284 (read from the page image); FY26 AR FY26 Note 42 p.334. Three year ends are the span the two ageing tables publish.

The share was steady for two years and rose by 3.2 points in FY26, while the book grew. The company provides for expected delay: an expected credit loss allowance of Rs 256.76 Cr on trade receivables at 31 Mar 2026 (Note 42 p.334). The FY25 note is explicit that "Provision is made for expected delay in realisation of trade receivables beyond contractual terms" (AR FY25 p.284).

**What management has said (context only, never a calculator input).** Mr. Manish Mohnot named the cause himself on the Q4 FY26 call (15 May 2026): "collection delays within the Water business" (p.3). On the Water receivable: "our total outstanding receivables on that business is closer to INR1,600-odd crores" (p.10). Asked when it would clear: "we would like it to be cleared in June itself. But the indication from the ground level ... is next 4 to 5 months, entire things should get cleared" (p.14). On the Q3 FY26 call (5 Feb 2026, p.12) he said that excluding Water, "the working capital days could come down by 6 to 7 days at a standalone and consol level".

The Rs 1,600 Cr is management's spoken figure for the Water business and is not a balance-sheet line; it is not compared with the Rs 1,311.12 Cr above, because the two are on different bases. Nothing is said here about whether the "4 to 5 months" was met.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Which clients, projects and invoices make up the Rs 1,311.12 Cr; how much is retention that has reached its release milestone and how much is a payment the client has simply not made; whether the next step is the client's (certify, collect) or KPIL's (a resubmission, a document the client asked for); and how long each invoice has been past its own due date rather than in a one-to-two-year bucket.

## Problem 3. Operating cash flow fell in FY26 while earnings rose

**What the filings show.** Standalone net cash from operating activities was Rs 764.46 Cr in FY26 against Rs 837.07 Cr in FY25, down 8.7%, in a year when core EBITDA rose 27.8% and PAT rose 28.4% (AR FY26 cash flow p.284, P&L p.281). The working-capital lines of the same cash-flow statement show where it went: trade and other receivables absorbed Rs 2,066.47 Cr and inventories Rs 391.79 Cr, against Rs 1,559.42 Cr from trade and other payables.

**Trend (standalone, Rs Cr; core EBITDA = PBT before exceptional items + finance cost + depreciation less other income, our computation, matching the company's printed 1,587 and 2,029 for FY25 and FY26, IP Q4 FY26 p.10):**

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net cash from operating activities | 559.38 | 713.61 | 837.07 | 764.46 |
| Core EBITDA | 1,161.54 | 1,365.25 | 1,586.99 | 2,028.68 |
| Operating cash flow / core EBITDA | 0.48 | 0.52 | 0.53 | 0.38 |
| PAT | 531.95 | 533.00 | 647.95 | 831.75 |

Sources: AR FY23 cash flow pp.249 to 250 and P&L p.247; AR FY25 cash flow p.238 and P&L p.235; AR FY26 cash flow pp.284 to 285 and P&L p.281.

Cash conversion rose from 0.48 to 0.53 between FY23 and FY25 and fell back to 0.38 in FY26. Growth of 22.9% in revenue explains part of it: a growing EPC book funds new work before it bills it. It is still the clearest sign in the accounts that profit is arriving faster than cash.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Which projects consumed the Rs 2,066.47 Cr of receivable build; whether it was billing that ran ahead of collection or certification that ran behind execution; and which part was within contract terms and which part was late.

## Problem 4. The working-capital cycle rose four days after March in Q1 FY27

**What the filings show.** The company's own standalone NWC days rose from 90 at 31 Mar 2026 to 94 at 30 Jun 2026 (IP Q1 FY27 p.9, filed 11 Aug 2026). In fairness, the same figure was 106 at 30 Jun 2025, so Q1 FY27 is 12 days better than a year earlier; the move is against March, not against last year. A year earlier the same quarter added 12 days, from 94 to 106; this year it added 4.

**Trend (company's own figure, standalone, definition not published):**

| Year end | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NWC days | 121 | 96 | 99 | 94 | 90 |

Source: IP Q4 FY26 slide 21 (one series, one document). Quarter ends: Q1 FY26 106, Q4 FY26 90, Q1 FY27 94 (IP Q1 FY27 p.9).

**Their own target.** The FY26 target of "under 100 days" was MET at 90. It is printed as met, never as a gap. No FY27 target was given: on the Q4 FY26 call Mr. Mohnot said "if we maintain this, it would be very good for the business" (p.11). Crestline does not invent a target for them; the only scenario here is holding the level they reached.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Q1 FY27 balance-sheet lines are not published (the results filing carries the P&L and ratios only), so which balance moved, receivables, contract assets, stock or payables, is not visible from outside. Nor is which projects moved it.

## Problem 5. Payables to micro and small enterprises grew 62% in a year

**What the filings show.** Current trade payables to micro and small enterprises were Rs 432.39 Cr at 31 Mar 2026, against Rs 266.59 Cr a year earlier (AR FY26 balance sheet p.280). Of the Rs 432.39 Cr, Rs 391.37 Cr was not yet due and Rs 41.02 Cr was past its due date in the payables ageing (Note 44 p.337, our subtraction). The company's MSME note shows statutory interest accrued and unpaid at year end of Rs 1.28 Cr, against Rs 7.47 Cr the year before (Note 54 p.342). Billed MSME dues past their due date rose from Rs 28.33 Cr to Rs 41.02 Cr over the same year (Note 44 p.337). (Note 54 prints the FY25 principal as Rs 266.97 Cr; the balance sheet prints Rs 266.59 Cr. The table uses the balance sheet.)

**Trend (standalone, 31 March, balance sheet):**

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MSME payables, Rs Cr | 150.91 | 222.45 | 266.59 | 432.39 |
| Share of all trade payables, including unbilled | 3.2% | 4.2% | 4.3% | 6.6% |

Sources: AR FY23 p.246; AR FY25 p.234; AR FY26 p.280.

**Why it matters, and what it is not.** Suppliers in this category are owed payment within the statutory 45 days. A larger MSME balance is not a saving and paying it later is never one: under rule 1, payables days are judged against each supplier's due date, never as better when longer. Paying the Rs 41.02 Cr on its due date USES cash. This problem is about risk and control, and it is listed because it is the one place in the payables where timing has a legal limit.

**What the accounts cannot tell.** Which suppliers and which sites hold the Rs 41.02 Cr; how many days past due each invoice is; whether the due date in the system is the agreed term or the statutory 45 days; and whether the delay is approval, goods-receipt matching, or the payment run.
