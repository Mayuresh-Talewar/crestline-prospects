## P1. Revenue grew about 4x in three years, and the bank lines kept pace; the question is whether the view of where the cash sits grows with it
- Total operating income: FY22 65.45, FY23 112.80, FY24 162.03, FY25 261.12 (Infomerics 17 May 2023 pp.2-5; 3 Jul 2024 p.5; 28 Jul 2025 p.5). FY25 / FY22 = 3.99x (computed). FY25 growth about 61% (computed 61.16%; the rationale prints ~61.61%, 28 Jul 2025 p.2).
- PAT FY25 18.96 (28 Jul 2025 p.5).
- Rated bank limits: Rs 61 Cr (17 May 2023: 11 fund-based + 50 bank guarantee) to Rs 81 Cr (3 Jul 2024: 11 + 70) to Rs 152 Cr (28 Jul 2025: 30 fund-based + 122 bank guarantee) (Infomerics, p.1 of each). Between the May 2023 and July 2025 ratings, revenue rose 2.31x (FY23 Rs 112.80 Cr to FY25 Rs 261.12 Cr) and rated limits rose 2.49x; bank guarantees are 80.3% of the Rs 152 Cr (computed). Each rating follows its year-end close (2023 PR table ends 31.03.2023, p.4; 2025 PR table ends 31-03-2025, p.5), so these two spans are aligned. Internal only, never printed: the Rs 122 Cr guarantee limit against FY25 revenue as a ratio (mixed basis, QA should-fix 3).
- Utilisation, not one series: average fund-based utilisation about 61% (12 months to Mar 2023, 2023 PR p.4); average cash credit utilisation about 70% (12 months to Mar 2024, cash credit only, 2024 PR p.4); average fund-based utilisation about 39.46% (12 months to May 2025, 2025 PR p.4). Never drawn as one line; 2024 always labelled "cash credit only".
- Agency downward trigger, every year since 2023: "Stretch in working capital cycle leading to deterioration in liquidity position" (17 May 2023 p.2); "Elongation in the operating cycle adversely impacting the liquidity position" (3 Jul 2024 p.2); "Elongation in the operating cycle impacting liquidity" (28 Jul 2025 p.2).
- **NOT PUBLISHED:** BG utilisation, interest rate, fund vs non-fund split of actual debt, any FY26 figure.
- Respectful framing: the growth was won on new contracts and tenders (2025 PR p.2), and the banks have kept pace so far (limits 2.49x against revenue 2.31x on aligned spans). No "lagging limits" story. The question is whether visibility into where the cash sits grows with the business: how much of each job's cash sits in the wait between a milestone and the client's payment. The books state the balance, not the wait.

- Why it matters for cash: when one client holds half the work, that client's certification and payment habits set the company's cash cycle.
- Isolation note: "Talegaon" is also the name of a project in Crestline's demo data (Grey Infra). No demo figure may appear near this line.

## P4. What a fast-growing design and build firm's books cannot tell it
- The ledger says what is owed; it does not say which milestone sits uncertified, at whose desk, since when, against which contract window.
- It cannot rank jobs by money x days, so a slow small bill and a slow Rs 100 Cr milestone look alike in a total.
- It cannot say whether vendors are paid before their own due date (cash spent early) or after (risk).
- It does not price a day: one day of FY25 revenue is Rs 0.7154 Cr (261.12 / 365, computed); the books never show that.
- The company says it runs an ERP; the platform is **NOT PUBLISHED** (arwadeinfra.in/erp/, 8 Oct 2026).
