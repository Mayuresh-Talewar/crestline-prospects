# J. Kumar Infraprojects: three problems (JK-3)

Story: the company set itself a working capital target and met it. On its own key ratios the days it gained came from stock. The receivable book did not move, and inside it a block of bills went past due. The next days are in the receivable book.

Ownership rule used throughout: measuring, preparing, reviewing, submitting and invoicing a bill are the company's own acts. Acknowledging, certifying and paying are the client's.

All balance sheet figures: STANDALONE, Annual Report FY2025-26 (AR26), filed on NSE 27 Aug 2026, one vintage per chart. Rs crore.

## First, the respect it has earned
The Joint Managing Directors' message: "Working capital days improved to 99 from 112" (AR26 PDF p9, printed p13, filed 27 Aug 2026). The 99 and 112 are the company's own key ratios (MD&A, AR26 PDF p29, printed 53); the company does not state their basis, and the same page and paragraph report consolidated figures, so they are never paired with the standalone tables below. Dr. Nalin J. Gupta, Managing Director, on the call of 7 Aug 2026: "So right now also we intend to keep it in this range only of 100 and 110, 100 to 110, not more than that." (T127 p17, filed 10 Aug 2026). At 99 days for FY26 (AR26 PDF p29, printed 53) the company is inside its own range. **Target met.** Nothing below says otherwise.

## P1. The target was met without the receivable book moving

**Label (standalone):** What moved on the standalone balance sheet in FY26. J. Kumar Infraprojects, standalone balance sheet, 31 Mar 2025 and 31 Mar 2026, Rs crore. Source: AR26 PDF p89, p96, p103 (printed 172, 187, 200), filed 27 Aug 2026.

| Line (standalone, AR26 vintage) | 31 Mar 2025 | 31 Mar 2026 | Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables | 1,488.56 | 1,485.29 | flat |
| Receivable days, closing basis (computed) | 95.4 | 95.2 | flat |
| Inventories | 503.48 | 329.65 | down |
| Mobilisation and machinery advance (the client's money in the business) | 621.02 | 706.09 | up |

Reading: on the company's own key ratios the 13 days came from stock: inventory days 70 to 58, debtor days 96 to 95, creditor days 54 to 54 (AR26 PDF p29, printed 53). On the standalone balance sheet, receivables held at about 95 days of revenue and the client's mobilisation advance rose. The letter says "We tightened follow-through on measurement, certification and collections." (AR26 PDF p9, printed p13). Measurement is the company's own step; certification and payment are the client's. The receivable line has not yet moved.

**What the accounts cannot tell:** which project and which client hold the Rs 1,485.29 Cr; for each bill, whether it is waiting on our submission, the client's certification or the client's payment; whose desk it sits on; and since when.

## P2. A block of bills went past due in FY26

**Label (standalone):** Trade receivables by time past due date. J. Kumar Infraprojects, standalone, 31 Mar 2025 and 31 Mar 2026, Rs crore. Source: AR26 PDF p97 (printed 188), filed 27 Aug 2026. All undisputed.

| Past due (from due date) | 31 Mar 2025 | 31 Mar 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Not due | 1,232.24 | 1,076.79 |
| Under 6 months | 155.18 | 185.74 |
| 6 months to 1 year | 12.65 | **141.15** |
| 1 to 2 years | 46.13 | 43.98 |
| 2 to 3 years | 29.43 | 20.16 |
| Over 3 years | 12.94 | 17.47 |
| Overdue share of the book (computed) | 17.2% | **27.5%** |

Reading: the 6 months to 1 year bucket rose 11.2 times (up Rs 128.50 Cr). Over one year past due fell slightly. So a group of bills fell due in the first half of FY26 and was still unpaid at the year end. Paying is the client's act; knowing which bills, and pressing for them with the paperwork complete, is the company's.

**What the accounts cannot tell:** which bills make up the Rs 141.15 Cr, which clients and sites, whether each was certified before it fell due, who last chased it and when.

Retention is not in the audited accounts (searched AR24, AR25, AR26). Vasant Savla, Chief Financial Officer, gives it on every call:

| 31 Mar 2026 | "Unbilled revenue is INR578 crores, mobilization advance is INR706 crores and retention is INR464 crores." | T426 p4 | 20 May 2026 / 25 May 2026 |

**Label:** Retention money as stated by the CFO on four results calls, Rs crore. Not audited and not in the financial statements; the speaker does not state a basis, so none is printed. Source: call transcripts, pages and dates above.

Reading: on his own figures, retention rose every quarter of FY26. Releasing retention takes the client's certificate at a milestone; knowing which milestone each rupee waits on is the company's.

**What the accounts cannot tell:** retention by contract, the milestone each amount waits on, the date it becomes due for release, and whose desk prepares the release claim.
