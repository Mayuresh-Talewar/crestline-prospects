# Garuda: story angle (GA-1)

Basis: standalone, Rs crore, as in data.md. Respect first, then the question.

## Respect first (all from the company's own filings)
- Revenue from operations went from Rs 154.18 Cr (FY24) to 225.03 Cr (FY25) to 530.72 Cr (FY26), with PAT of Rs 122.54 Cr in FY26 (23.1% of revenue). AR26 PDF p.113; AR25 PDF p.120.
- A company with 97 people, including the MD, ran that growth on an asset-light model: subcontractors bring the equipment, materials and labour. AR26 PDF p.54; deck Oct 2025 slide 23.
- It listed on NSE and BSE in FY25 and raised Rs 173.85 Cr fresh, which it says went to working capital across running projects. Infomerics p.2-3; AR26 PDF p.144.
- Bank debt is close to nil. Rated IVR BBB+ / Stable by Infomerics on 30 Mar 2026, which cites a strong order book, healthy margins and no gearing. Infomerics p.1-3.
- The order book roughly 2.5x'd from about Rs 1,400 Cr at IPO to Rs 3,461 Cr by 27 Oct 2025 (company deck, slide 15), and Rs 4,336.72 Cr at 31 Dec 2025 per the rater.

## The question (where bills wait)
Revenue is recognised as work is done. Cash arrives only after a bill is raised, certified and paid. In the company's own balance sheet the gap is visible at three points:
1. Trade receivables: Rs 240.41 Cr at 31 Mar 2026, which is 165 days of FY26 revenue on a closing basis (417 days at FY24, 293 at FY25). Of the 240.41, Rs 106.99 Cr has been outstanding more than six months. AR26 PDF p.112, p.138.
2. Contract assets (work done, not yet billed): Rs 96.13 Cr at Mar 2025, Rs 218.91 Cr at Mar 2026. The auditor made this a key audit matter, with receivable recoverability another. AR26 PDF p.133, p.96-97.
3. The rater names it directly: "elongated operating cycle due to high collection period"; operating cycle 107 days (FY24) to 204 days (FY25); and "an upward factor" would be "reduction in the overall working capital cycle on a sustained basis". Infomerics p.1, p.4.

Combined (Computed, one basis): receivables plus contract assets equal Rs 459.32 Cr at Mar 2026, which is 316 days of FY26 revenue on closing balances (449 days at FY25 from 276.61 / 225.03). Print only with this definition stated; it is our arithmetic, not a company figure.

Management's own words on the risk: "working capital requirements" is one of five named risks (AR26 PDF p.53). The Chairman's message and MD&A do not give a target for collection or working-capital days. NOT FOUND: a stated target.

The accounts cannot say: which site, which bill, which desk, and since when a given bill has waited. The ageing is by age bucket only, with no split of "waiting for certification" vs "waiting for the client to pay". That is the opening for Crestline: the same data in an ERP/Tally trail, read as a queue by stage and owner.

## How to say it
- Open with growth and the IPO discipline (paid suppliers on time with IPO money, per the rater: creditor days 217 to 107, FY24 to FY25).
- Then: "growth of this speed needs money to move at the same pace; here is where the bills wait in your own accounts."
- Use only: debtor days on one stated basis (closing), the ageing buckets, contract assets, the rater's wording.
- Do not name counterparties. Do not split receivables by related vs third party (Garvis only; see sensitive-garvis.md).
- Interest only at a disclosed rate: 9% (related-party ICD, AR26 PDF p.130). The company has no bank working-capital debt, so a "cost of carry" must be framed as the company's own cash tied up, not as interest saved.
- Savings scenarios must treat the order book dates and Gorakhpur land-monetisation work as outside Crestline's reach (cash settled in land or flats: Infomerics p.4).
