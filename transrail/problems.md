# Transrail Lighting: three problems on the TR-2 story (2026-10-09)

STORY. Management took working-capital days from 91 (FY25) to 81 (FY26): ten days out in one year, on a business that grew 29.6%. At June 2026 the days read 85 while the order book and the growth guidance push volume. The question for each problem: where do the next days sit, bill by bill.

Source for the days: Q1 FY27 investor presentation, filed 7 Aug 2026, PDF p.19 (printed 18): Working Capital Days 53 (FY23), 73, 91, 81, 85 (Q1 FY27). The page prints no basis and no formula. Q4 FY26 call, 27 May 2026 (filed 2 Jun 2026), PDF p.6, Deepak Khandelwal, Chief Financial Officer: "Working capital days improved to 81 days in FY26 from 91 days in FY25, reflecting improved collections, efficient project execution and tighter control over working capital."

Ownership used throughout. Ours: measure, prepare, review, submit, invoice. The client's: acknowledge, certify, pay.

## Problem 1. Receivables grew a little faster than revenue
- Figures (standalone, Annual Report FY26 filed 2 Sep 2026): trade receivables, net, Rs 1,739.48 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 against Rs 1,317.14 Cr a year earlier, up 32.1% (AR PDF p.159, printed 188; note 8, PDF p.180, printed 209). Revenue from operations plus other operating revenue Rs 6,878.63 Cr against Rs 5,307.63 Cr, up 29.6% (AR PDF p.160, printed 189). Both standalone, same year end.
- Age, from bill date, as at 31 Mar 2026 (AR PDF p.180, printed 209, first schedule table): under 6 months Rs 1,565.65 Cr; 6 months to 1 year Rs 91.54 Cr.
- Days: about 92, which is 365 divided by the company's own debtors turnover of 3.96 (AR PDF p.33, printed 61, consolidated revenue over consolidated receivables).
- Respect first: management credits "improved collections" for the ten days taken out, in a year when revenue grew 29.6%.
- Ownership: invoicing and submitting are ours; paying is the client's.
- What the filings cannot tell: which clients and which sites hold the Rs 91.54 Cr past six months; how many of those days sit between submission and the client's acknowledgement, and how many between certification and payment; whose desk the bill waited on; since when.

## Problem 2. Contract assets are 36.6% of a year's revenue and have not moved
- Figures (AR FY26, filed 2 Sep 2026): contract assets Rs 2,515.24 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 against Rs 2,521.97 Cr (AR PDF p.159 printed 188; note 13, PDF p.185, printed 214, "Contract Assets represents unbilled revenue and retention due to contractual conditions."). Same figure in both statements. Against revenue Rs 6,880.11 Cr (consolidated, AR PDF p.248, printed 277): 36.6%, about 133 days of revenue (Crestline's own measure, not an accounting standard).
- Respect first: the balance did not grow while revenue grew 29.6%.
- Management on retention, Q1 FY27 call held 7 Aug 2026 (filed 12 Aug 2026), PDF p.12 (printed 11), Deepak Khandelwal, Chief Financial Officer: "It includes the dependency retention, TOC retention, and unbilled revenue." and, on direction: "It is going to increase because retention is increasing due to higher revenue per quarter". Wording as printed in the transcript.
- Ownership: measuring, preparing and submitting the bill are ours; acknowledging and certifying are the client's; retention comes back on contract milestones.
- What the filings cannot tell: the split between unbilled revenue and retention (no amount printed for retention alone), the age of each part, which contracts hold it, how much waits on a bill we have not yet prepared and how much on a certificate the client has not yet issued.

## Problem 3. Days slipped back to 85 and the debt carried the quarter
- Figures: working-capital days 81 at FY26, 85 at Q1 FY27 (deck PDF p.19, filed 7 Aug 2026). Net debt Rs 267 Cr at FY26 and Rs 548 Cr at Q1 FY27 as the deck prints it (same page; "Previous period figures are restated wherever applicable"; basis not labelled).
- Cost of carrying it: working capital demand loans Rs 252.49 Cr at 31 Mar 2026, cash credit from consortium bankers nil (AR PDF p.198, printed 227, standalone note 24); cash credit and working capital demand loans carry "an average interest rate at 10.31% p.a." (AR PDF p.199, printed 228, note i).
- Management, Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.16 (printed 15), Deepak Khandelwal, Chief Financial Officer: "net debt has increased on account of some delayed collections and working capital has been deployed more in the business, which is going to be normalized in Q2".
- Respect first: ten days came out in a year, and the same call says the movement is expected to be normalised within the year.
- Ownership: part of the move is the client's clock (payment, certification), part is ours (preparing and submitting bills on time).
- What the filings cannot tell: how the four days split between receivables, contract assets, stock and payables; the formula for the days; which collections were delayed and on which bills; the net debt basis on the deck page. Year-end facility balances are not an average through the year.
