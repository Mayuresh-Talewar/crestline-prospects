# Yash Construction Co - story angle (YC-1)

Respect first. A Latur partnership founded in 2008 grew from Rs 164.42 Cr (FY23) to Rs 491.25 Cr (FY24) and Rs 620.50 Cr (FY25), holds Rs 1,543.19 Cr of unexecuted orders (11 Jun 2026) and kept its operating margin at 12.51% in FY26 (Prov.). Acuite rates it BBB/Stable and A3+, and the rated limits rose to Rs 300 Cr in June 2026. It builds roads and bridges for Maharashtra PWD and NHAI.

Then the question: where do bills wait? Revenue eased to Rs 562.18 Cr in FY26 (Prov.) from Rs 620.50 Cr. Acuite's own words give the cause as "delays in payments from government authorities", which "impacted both project execution pace and working capital availability", on work that depends on "timely certification and release of funds from state departments". Debtor days are 64 (70 a year earlier), gross current asset days 105 (Acuite's upgrade trigger is below 50), current ratio 0.82x. The accounts cannot say which bill, which department, which stage (measurement, certification, release) or since when. That is Crestline's question: where the money waits, for how long, and who can release it.

Framing: the firm is collecting faster than a year ago; the open question is the remaining 64 days and an order book that will add to it. Use only Acuite's figures. No ERP implied. Process steps illustrative.
