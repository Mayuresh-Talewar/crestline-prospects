# Cube Construction Engineering: story angle (respect first, then where bills wait)

Respect first (all CRISIL 02-May-2025, consolidated): about three decades in building construction (ccel.in "almost 30 years"), A-/Stable and A2+ held since 2022 on Rs 611 Cr of limits, revenue Rs 520.79 Cr (FY23) to Rs 712.18 Cr (FY24) to an estimated Rs 800-810 Cr (FY25), an order book of Rs 1,522 Cr at 31-Dec-2024, hospitals, a 2,000-bed hospital at Gandhi Medical College Bhopal, ISRO, DRDO and science-city work (ccel.in). Gearing about 0.38x (est.). Technology-heavy projects fetched a "comparatively higher margin" (CRISIL).

The question (where bills wait), from CRISIL's own words, estimates, basis unstated:
- Gross current assets 260-270 days for FY25, down from 320-330 (FY24 est.) and 390 (FY23). Direction is improvement; CRISIL still lists "large working capital requirement" first among weaknesses.
- Receivables nearly 130 days, payment "realised within 130-150 days", "a large portion of receivables pertains to retention money". Bulk of revenue is booked in March, so receivables peak at year end.
- Inventory about 115 days (WIP plus raw material).
- Payables over 220 days, "improved from over 300": the cycle is carried by vendors and bank limits at ~84% utilisation.
- One client (GMERS Medical College and Hospital) is about 64% of the order book through four orders; Gujarat about 84%. Timely execution and working capital management are named monitorables.

Where bills wait: the accounts cannot say which of the four GMERS orders, which RA bill, which desk or how long certification takes before invoicing. That is the gap: the days are an annual average; the cash is stuck in specific bills.

Honest limits for the booklet: no CFO is named, no rate, no cash flow, no retention figure; frame as "a few questions" and never imply the ERP.
