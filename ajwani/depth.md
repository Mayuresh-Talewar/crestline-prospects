# Ajwani Infrastructure: story angle (depth)

Respect first, then the question. Every figure below is in data.md with its source.

## Respect first
- A builder of roads, not a name on a brochure: founded in Pune in 1975 by Shri G. H. Ajwani (1941 to 2021), a company since 1986. The founder received the Builders Association of India Life Time Achievement Award in 2017 (company site, About).
- Turnover of Rs 755.55 Cr in FY25 and net worth of Rs 240.70 Cr at 31 Mar 2025, up from Rs 205 Cr a year earlier (MGT-7 FY25; CRISIL 19 Jun 2024). Say "turnover", and keep it apart from CRISIL's operating-income years.
- Closely held by the family, a very thin lender footprint: Rs 400 Cr of rated limits, of which only Rs 5.5 Cr is term loan (CRISIL 19 Jun 2024). CRISIL: "strong financial risk profile", cash and equivalents Rs 149 Cr at Mar 2024.
- Municipal trust: Navi Mumbai, Pune and Pimpri-Chinchwad corporations, CIDCO, MMRDA, MSRDC, NHAI. CRISIL notes "consistent performance and timely execution".
- Award: Builders Association of India, December 2023, for the Nerul passenger water transport terminal for CIDCO (company site, News).
- Owns quarries at Panvel, Mahape and Pune, so the road work starts with its own material (CRISIL).

## The question
CRISIL's own weakness is the working capital: gross current assets of 195 to 317 days across five years, driven by earnest money, security deposits and retention (CRISIL 19 Jun 2024). The same document says bills are raised monthly or on milestones and paid within 30 days. So the question is not "does the client pay". It is where the rest of those current assets sit: deposits, retention, and bills between measurement, certification and payment, across how many sites and desks, and since when. A Rs 755.55 Cr turnover is about Rs 2.07 Cr a day (Computed), so a day moved is worth that much, before any lender cost.
- The accounts cannot say which bill, which site, which desk, or how long. That is the gap.
- Scale changed fast: Rs 383.85 Cr (FY23 operating income) to "over Rs 500 crore" (FY24) to Rs 755.55 Cr (FY25 turnover). The more the book grows, the more bills in flight. CRISIL names a stretch in the working-capital cycle as a downward trigger.
- Do not print a growth percentage across the FY23 and FY25 bases.

## Cautions for the story
- Rating is June 2024: say so, never "currently rated".
- No debtor days exist; do not build a "your debtor days are X" line.
- Never imply their ERP. Never call the order book a multiple of FY25 turnover.
- Leaders: address by statutory role with the exact title; no CFO is named anywhere, so no CFO letter variant from a primary source.
