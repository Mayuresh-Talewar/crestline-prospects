# Effwa Infra & Research Limited: problems (deepened 2026-10-10)

Basis for every figure: standalone, audited, year ended 31 March 2026 (FY26), Rs Cr = Rs lakh / 100. Sources: Annual Report 2025-26 (13th), accounts signed 12 May 2026, filed September 2026 (balance sheet p78, profit and loss p79, cash flow p80, notes p84 to p93 of the company-site copy); results call transcripts of 9 May 2025 and 13 May 2026. Days = closing balance / revenue from operations x 365 (Crestline's arithmetic on the published accounts). No interest is priced: no borrowing rate is published. All detail is in data.md "Depth (2026-10-10)". Nothing here is from the KEPT OUT list (README EF-2) or sensitive-garvis.md.

## Respect first
Revenue rose from Rs 145.16 Cr (FY24) to Rs 185.12 Cr (FY25) to Rs 253.29 Cr (FY26), profit after tax from Rs 13.90 Cr to Rs 20.11 Cr to Rs 28.62 Cr, and operating profit before interest, depreciation and tax was Rs 42.10 Cr. Debtor days came down from 231.9 to 185.5. Net cash from operating activities was Rs 29.43 Cr. An ERP was introduced in FY26.

## Problem 1 (billing to collection, the client's move): Rs 128.71 Cr of bills is waiting to be paid
- Stage: collection. Owner: the client (it pays); Effwa chases.
- Figures: trade receivables Rs 128.71 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 against Rs 117.60 Cr a year earlier; 185.5 days against 231.9; a day of revenue is Rs 0.694 Cr (253.29 / 365), so ten days is Rs 6.94 Cr once (calc output, unchanged).
- Mechanism, in management's words (call 13 May 2026): billing is on milestones fixed at bidding; Effwa submits the bill; the client checks and approves; "then only we account the bill." So the balance on the accounts is bills the client has already approved, and the days are counted after approval. The accounts do not say how long each bill then takes to be paid or why.
- Crestline would measure: for each approved bill, the date it was approved, the date it was paid, and the days between, by site, milestone and client desk; and which waits are the client's and which are Effwa's own paperwork.
- Framing: the improvement first (231.9 to 185.5); then the question of the days that are left, bill by bill.

## Problem 2 (growth on the same wait): sales grew 36.8%, bills waiting grew 9.5%
- Stage: collection and project pipeline. Owner: the client's wait on a book that is Effwa's to execute.
- Figures: revenue Rs 185.12 Cr to Rs 253.29 Cr; receivables Rs 117.60 Cr to Rs 128.71 Cr; the cash flow statement shows Rs 11.11 Cr more tied up in receivables in FY26 (1,111.44 lakh, equal to the balance sheet movement). Order book about Rs 750 Cr across five to six project sites, 12 to 36 month execution on the key ongoing projects (management, May 2026).
- Mechanism: stated, not inferred. Larger orders run longer (up to 36 months), billed by milestone; more milestone bills outstanding at once.
- Crestline would measure: waiting bills by site, so the five to six sites can be compared on money and days.
- Framing: more work on the same wait means more cash waiting; this is the order book doing its job.

## Problem 3 (vendor, Effwa's move): Rs 25.92 Cr is owed to suppliers and its timing against each due date is not in the accounts
- Stage: vendor payment. Owner: Effwa.
- Figures: trade payables Rs 25.92 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 (Rs 16.38 Cr a year earlier), against purchases and direct expenses of Rs 180.38 Cr in FY26 (Note 19); payables rose Rs 9.55 Cr in the year (cash flow statement). Shown as levels only.
- Mechanism: not stated by the source. The accounts carry no vendor terms, no payment dates and no supplier concentration. Management says material comes from vendors approved by the customers and construction is outsourced to a contractor (call 9 May 2025), which means the vendor chain follows what the client approves.
- Crestline would measure: each vendor payment against the due date agreed for it. Early payment is cash lent at zero unless a discount pays for it; late payment is a risk. Neither is called a saving here.
- Framing: no judgement of the level; the question is the timing, vendor by vendor.

## Problem 4 (cash): the year closed with Rs 29.43 Cr of operating cash, and the next wait is on a bigger book
- Stage: cash. Owner: shared.
- Figures: cash generated from operations Rs 39.05 Cr, tax paid Rs 9.62 Cr, net cash from operating activities Rs 29.43 Cr (cash flow statement, image-checked); Rs 11.11 Cr absorbed by receivables, Rs 9.55 Cr provided by payables.
- Mechanism: the two movements nearly offset; the cash cycle is a queue of bills on one side and of vendor payments on the other.
- Crestline would measure: the queue on both sides by project site, so which side moves first is a matter of days and rupees.
- Framing: Rs 29.43 Cr is the record of a company working on its cash.

## Problem 5 (framing only, no figure): the ERP is in, the time view is not
- An ERP records what happened. It does not say how long each step took or whose desk it sat on. That view, above the ERP and read-only, is what the pilot would build on one to three project sites.
